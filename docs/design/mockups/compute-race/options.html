How the compute race works

What the compute race is in the game today, in plain words, then three ways it could work, each pictured in the Compute button's panel, with its effect on the whole game.

My pick: A tonight, then B after the deadline

0Today, in plain words

There is no race for compute yet. Only you gather compute. Rivals have none; they move on a fixed speed plus dice. Compute reaches the race through one door: it decides how big a model you can train.

What happens now

1You gather compute by signing contracts, ordering in the era 3 queue and building era 4 power sites. You pay every month whether it is used or not.
2Compute is split into serving users, safety, control, and training. What is left for training decides the biggest model you can start: Small, Medium, Large or XL.
3A bigger model gains more. Each size step adds about 3 to 5 capability points to a run. Beyond that, extra compute adds nothing: it sits idle.
4Rivals never touch compute. Each fills a hidden progress bar at its own speed and launches when it is full, gaining about 6 to 13 points. Nothing you buy slows them. The era 3 queue shows them ordering chips, but what they get changes nothing for them; it only shrinks what is left for you.
5Rank is capability only. At each era gate you must be 2nd or better, or within 15 points of the leader, or you are left behind. At the end, 1st place wins.
Sign compute→free training units→model size→capability→rank
Rivals: fixed speed plus dice→their capability→rank

What that does to the race

AThe race tab

Sits beside the HUD lane's "Where it goes" tab. Rival progress bars already exist in the sim; they show as rumors, never as numbers.

recommended for tonightno sim change

How it plays

  • Nothing changes in the rules; you finally see them.
  • Rumor chips come from each rival's real launch progress: "launch rumored soon" past about half full.
  • The size strip shows what your free training units buy, and what the next size would need.
  • The gate box says in one sentence whether you would pass today.

What it does not fix

  • Rivals still ignore compute, so the race is still easy to win by round 10 and "left behind" stays rare.

BCompute sets the pace: rivals run on compute too

Each rival gets a compute fleet and buys from the same market as you. A rival's launch pace follows its fleet, so the lab holding the most compute ships most often, and what you take, it can't have.

BThe world's compute, shared out

One bar shows who holds the frontier's compute this month; the table turns fleet into launch pace. The lever box says what your next deal does to the leader.

new mechanicrecommended after the deadline

How it plays

  • Rivals buy each month, sized by how aggressive they are (today's speed number becomes their appetite).
  • Eras 1 and 2: plenty for everyone. Era 3: memory chips are rationed through the Verde queue for all labs, not just you. Era 4: power caps every lab's fleet.
  • Your big signings and prepaid orders take supply rivals wanted. Their pace drops; race heat rises.
  • Qilin buys only its own chips (export controls), so it can't be starved.
  • Ties at a score of 100 go to the bigger fleet, so compute keeps mattering late.

Fleets, shares and paces in this picture are illustrative. Rivals have no fleets today; these follow the proposed rule. The scores are real.

CRace for the deals: rivals take cards off your board

Experimental. The deal screen becomes a shared board. At each month's end every rival takes the card it wants most; the one you pass on may be gone. Rivals' fleets, and so their pace, grow from the cards they take.

How design B plugs into what the game already has

Era 3 queueIt already shares Verde's chips between labs by tier. Under B, what rivals get finally counts: it grows their fleets. Borrow Power Grid: labs behind on score get served first in the standard tier, which keeps the race close.
Era 4 powerRivals need power too. Their gas sites and nuclear restarts become feed news, and local opposition can slow them as it slows you.
Safety split and rival cautionEach rival's caution becomes its own safety slice. Careful Lodestar puts more compute on safety and ships slower; OpenBrain puts almost none. Your safety slice is the same trade, now visible.
Race heatToday's heat from launches stays. B adds heat when any lab's share jumps, so grabbing compute feeds "someone else's disaster".
Era 5 summit and poolingThe compute cap card limits every signer's fleet growth, so it finally slows rivals. The pooling card visibly shrinks your share.
Board promise and era gates"X units by era Y" can be judged against your share. Gates stay as they are: 2nd or better, or within 15 points. B just makes missing them possible again.

★Recommendation

A tonight

It answers your question ("how does the compute race work?") with no rule change, so it cannot shift the endings before the demo. It fills the race tab of the Compute button the HUD lane is building, so the two lanes ship one panel.

B after the deadline

It is the smallest rule change that makes compute a real race. It also closes two misses already on the plan's deferred list: slowness is not punished by rivals, and extra power stops mattering near the score cap. It reuses the era 3 queue's sharing rule for all labs.

C optional, later

The most dramatic, and it sits on top of B. Worth it only if B plays well and the between-releases stretch still feels empty.
