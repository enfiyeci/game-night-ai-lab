Decided on 2026-09-26

  1. Price (1C): a slider with four stops. Each stop shows what API customers pay per million tokens for this model. Free tier shows "ads and upgrades".
  2. Skipping a number (3C): each skipped number raises the critics' bar by 1 benchmark point. The feed always talks about the jump: impressed posts when the model beats the last one by 5 points or more, mocking posts when it doesn't.
  3. Next to the badge (5B): what customers pay per million tokens, what serving costs you per million tokens, and the margin, with who gets it and the thinking effort underneath.

A correction to what I told you earlier: the game works out running costs right after the release, not at the start of the next turn. That is what makes the cost-per-token numbers possible on the reveal.

The eight choices, for reference

Your picks are selected

Tags say how much work each option is. "Easy" means screen work only, which fits tonight's deadline. "Bigger job" means a change to the game's rules or a new system.

1

How does the player set the price?

The price decides how much each user pays and how fast users sign up. The game already has four price levels.

What we found: the plan listed three levels (cheap, market, premium), but the game only accepts four different ones: premium, market, undercut and free. The design spec also says price should be a slider, like the sliders on the training screen.

2

How does the screen show the "ship now or test more?" decision?

This is the big AI-safety choice at release. Checking the model more carefully costs money and sometimes a turn. Skipping checks is fast, but you know less about what you built.

What we found: the checking options already exist in the game. None of the AI-lab games we looked at turns "ship now or test more" into one clear choice on the release screen, so this can be our game's special moment.

3

Can the player skip ahead in the version number?

The player types the family name ("Kestrel"). The number counts up by itself (4, 5, 6...). The plan added a switch to jump ahead, for example from 4 straight to 6.

What we found: the spec says a jump should make critics expect more, and a jump with no real improvement should get mocked. The game doesn't do either yet, so today the switch would change the name and nothing else.

4

How does the reveal appear?

This is the payoff moment, so it can build up instead of appearing all at once. Press a button to play each version on the reveal below. Click the reveal to skip to the end.

What we found: in Game Dev Tycoon, a reviewer called the little "ding" when a game finishes the best moment of the whole game. Game Dev Story shows its four critics as one big reveal.

5

What does the top badge say?

The badge in the top corner of the reveal sums up the launch in one line.

What we found: the approved mockup says "5 of 5", but that was placeholder text. The game counts only the four skill tests and leaves the safety test out, and the plan says "N of 4". The badge is hidden on your first release, because there is no earlier model to beat.

6

Does the safety score say who checked it?

From era 3, a model can learn to look safer on tests than it really is. Careful outside checks catch more of that. The true safety score is shown only at the end of the game.

What we found: today the safety row looks exactly like the other rows, so the player can't tell a carefully checked score from one the lab just reported itself. Real AI launches name who tested the model.

7

Does the reveal show money?

A release brings in users, and each user brings in money but also costs money to serve.

What we found: the spec says the reveal should show the running cost and profit. The approved mockup shows only new users. (Correction: the game does know the running cost at the reveal, so option C is easier than first said.)

8

Does the game tell the player why the launch went well or badly?

The scores go up or down because of choices the player made while training and releasing. Right now the reveal shows the scores but not the reasons.

What we found: Game Dev Tycoon teaches through a short written report ("Racing and Action are a great match"). Game Dev Story uses fan letters. Our game already knows the reasons, such as skipped checks or scraped data.

Already settled

  • The player types the family name. The number counts up by itself. The four size words (Swift, Core, Grand, Apex) are named once, on the first release, with a "Rename sizes" link.
  • The reveal follows the approved design: benchmarks, press and reactions in three columns, then Continue.
  • Names the player types are shown as plain text, so they can't break the page.

Notes for the build

  • The release screen shows costs, but not predicted scores: the preview can't know the real scores.
  • The screen mockup puts the team on the left and choices on the right, like the training screen. The plan had checks on the left and channel on the right. Say if you want the plan's layout.
  • If a release sets off a misalignment warning, that post comes first in the reactions.
  • The events lane edits some of the same files, so I'll tell that lane before editing them.
