Game Night · reward loops and event cadence · 2026-10-26

The game loses its pulse between releases, then stacks three decisions on one day.

The gaps are not long in seconds; they look long because a story day lasts one second in eras 1 and 2, so a 50-second gap reads as seven empty weeks, and nothing visible changes during it. I measured the current build with the bots, played a run by hand and read six research tracks on how other games pace rewards and events. Below are seven changes, each drawn into a real game frame. Holds for every option (from the ethics research)
  • No streaks, daily rewards or penalties for leaving; pause always works.
  • No fake near misses: reveal scores stay deterministic and explainable. Suspense comes from the order of the show, not rigged odds.
  • Every reward on screen traces back to a choice the player made.

Real frame: midEra3 at 1440×900. Dashed outlines mark what is new. The bubble is the quiet backstop's non-pausing beat.

Today the scenario director, the board, the President, the cash warning, the hazard and the summit each schedule themselves. O1 puts one rule set over all of them:

  • Spacing: at most two clock-stopping dialogs in any 30 s of clock, never two on one story day (target). The rest wait in a "2 waiting" chip and open one at a time.
  • Relax window: 30–45 s with no new stop after a cluster or set piece, counted from when the last dialog closes (target).
  • Quiet backstop: after about 90 s with no decision and no visible payoff, pull the next card forward or fire a non-pausing beat (an advisor aside, a feed post that pops out of the phone).
  • Set-piece days: the cash warning folds into the era recap; the President's call moves to the day after the hazard.

Era 1 gaps of up to 140 s; era 2's first-round pile; the same-day stacks.

Real frame: era 5 office. The leaderboard scores and the quip are illustrative, not from a run.

Era 5 lasts 28 story days at 12.9 s each, and a training run takes at least a round, so the finale has almost no releases. O6 turns it into four weekly sprints:

  • Era 5 training ships checkpoints: a one-week run that adds a slice of capability at the week mark.
  • Each week mark plays a 10-second reveal: the leaderboard only, every lab's checkpoint moving at once, one line of press.
  • A race clock on the HUD for the whole era.
  • The summit and the President's second call become the era's two scheduled peaks, with quiet weeks between (works with O1).
