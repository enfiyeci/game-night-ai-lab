# Let rival labs move the deal board

Across about twenty tycoon and management games, almost none lets a rival take the specific supply deal the player wanted. That gap is the clearest finding of this research and the largest open space for Game Night. The two real exceptions are **Rise of Industry**, whose contracts became per-region auctions in May 2019 (the player and computer-run companies bid the payout down and the lowest bid wins), and **Football Manager**, whose transfer-offer screen shows a count of interested clubs and the date by which the other side wants an answer. Everywhere else, rivals are felt in three other ways. The first is shared numbers that everyone's buying moves: Offworld's black-market prices rise after any purchase, Capitalism Lab's resource sites get sharply dearer as rivals buy them, and Big Pharma's market saturation lowers drug prices. The second is visible schedules and dossiers of what rivals will do, as in Software Inc.'s six-month notice before a new rival enters a market and Scaling Laws' fourteen labs that ship on dated histories. The third is stakes and buyouts, as in Anno 1800 and Capitalism Lab. Games whose rivals simply sit there drew the harshest player verdicts: Game Dev Tycoon players called its rivals "fake competition", and a top-rated Startup Company review said its competitors do nothing but wait to be bought. Supplier prices in these games mostly **rise** with time or with waiting, and they announce their changes ahead of time. That matches the 2026 real market and cuts against O3's cards that get cheaper as they wait. For the seven existing options, the evidence strengthens O1 and O4, gives O2 a ready-made way to make rivals felt, lifts **O5 (telegraphed supplier shocks) into the recommended set**, weakens O3, leaves O6 where it was, and gives O7 its first real-time precedent along with that precedent's warnings. It also supports two new options: a **contested deal** with named rival interest and a deadline, and **stakes in suppliers with rival fire sales**. One caveat applies throughout. No game was played. Three of the five notes were read entirely through a fetch tool that returns a model-written summary of each page. Much of the developer-studio material comes from community wikis and from patch notes searched by keyword rather than read end to end.

**How sources are tagged below.** "(community wiki)" means a fan-written wiki such as Fandom. "(summarising fetch)" means the researcher saw a model-written digest of the page, not the page itself; this applies to every claim about Against the Storm, Football Manager, RimWorld, Anno 1800, Offworld, the railway and airline games, Capitalism Lab, Rise of Industry and Big Pharma. "(keyword-filtered)" means a line found by searching a patch-note post for keywords, without reading the post end to end. "(search snippet)" means only a search-result extract was seen. Every ⚠️ marks a claim that rests on less than the whole source, and the reason follows it.

## Only two of about twenty games let a rival take the deal you wanted

The researchers looked for one mechanic in particular: a rival winning a supply deal or contract the player was also after. Three separate notes reached the same negative result. None of Game Dev Tycoon, Mad Games Tycoon 2 or Software Inc. has a rival that wins a specific supplier deal before you do. None of the six AI-lab and data-centre tycoons does either. No railway or airline game in the set documents slot or contract bidding against rivals. The findings below are the closest precedents.

**Rise of Industry is the one tycoon where the contract itself is contested.** After a May 2019 bug in which one contract appeared both as a mission and as an auction, the account "Alex, Master Penguin" said all contracts were now auctions per region and that one-to-one offers were gone ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/671440/discussions/1/1652169858542073842/); summarising fetch; ⚠️ the page does not label that account as the developer, so it is treated as the probable developer). The bid *is* the payout, so rival bidders drive the reward down as they undercut each other ([Steam thread, moderator reply, January 2021](https://steamcommunity.com/app/671440/discussions/0/5185299686169862094/); summarising fetch). Experimental patch notes extend auctions to region permits and marketing events. The auction starter bids 100% automatically and wins at 100% if nobody else bids. **Game speed drops to 1 whenever an auction spawns**, which is the only example in the set of a real-time game slowing the clock so a player can bid against computer rivals ([Steam patch-notes thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/671440/discussions/4/1815422173033520728); summarising fetch; the build is labelled A11.0 and no date is shown). The stakes are deliberately harsh. Payment arrives only when the whole contract is delivered, and the developer called contracts high risk, high reward and said streamers going bankrupt on them was intended ([Steam thread, December 2019](https://steamcommunity.com/app/671440/discussions/0/3124866920644349038/); summarising fetch). Losing the auction after signing the mission version voided the contract and charged a penalty ([Steam thread, May 2019](https://steamcommunity.com/app/671440/discussions/1/1652169858542073842/); summarising fetch). The rivals behind these bids are "entities" with personalities, shown in an Entity panel. They are immune to events and research for free ([patch-notes thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/671440/discussions/4/1815422173033520728); summarising fetch). Two cautions matter for Game Night. Offers in Rise of Industry are random; the developer said only that several things need to happen for them to appear ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/671440/discussions/0/1743346190283577965/); summarising fetch). And the penalty size, time limits and a reported cap of three active contracts come only from a search snippet of the fan wiki, which returned an error when opened ([Fandom wiki, community](https://riseofindustry.fandom.com/wiki/Contracts); ⚠️ search snippet only).

**Football Manager shows rival interest as a count and a date, not as a duel.** The researcher opened the official FM26 "Transfer Offer" image. Its left column reads "Transfer Status: Listed" and "2 Interested Clubs". A Director of Football notes box states, in plain words, the deadline the other club has set for the decision and the transfer window in which the move would complete. The centre shows the base fee and date, expandable additional fees and clauses (instalments, appearance bonuses, award bonuses), and a running "Future & Potential Total Cost" line. The right-hand actions include Accept, Stall, Reject and View Alternative Offers ([official FM26 image](https://cdn.footballmanager.com/site/inline-images/4.%20EN%5B1%5D_opt.jpg), on [the FM26 recruitment article](https://footballmanager.com/fm26/features/powered-transferroom-fm26s-recruitment-revamp); the image was viewed). An older Deadline Day hub let a seller with several offers accept one and give the other bidders an ultimatum to match the highest ([official FM page](https://www.footballmanager.com/features/deadline-day); ⚠️ search snippet only, the page returned HTTP 403). A fan FAQ says stalling buys time past the cutoff but the bidder may withdraw ([fan FAQ, community](https://www.vintageisthenewold.com/faq/what-is-the-stall-offer-on-football-manager); ⚠️ search snippet only, from a weak general-topics site). FM27 promises that agent offers will be visible throughout the window and will increase as it nears its close, so that they do not appear unexpectedly ([FM27 feature page](https://www.footballmanager.com/fm27/features/deeper-more-immersive-world); summarising fetch). How FM chooses which clubs bid, and whether the deadline is fixed or calculated, is not documented in anything the researcher opened.

**Three other games come close without a head-to-head race.** In Mad Games Tycoon 2, a 2021 patch line says games the player does not accept for publishing are distributed by computer-run publishers instead, so a declined deal goes to a rival ([Steam announcement](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4186652991113913673); ⚠️ keyword-filtered). In Capitalism Lab, the player, the AI firms and an always-open "industrial seaport" (a stand-in for the world market) all draw from the same suppliers. A rival that buys your intermediate goods can use your quality against you, and the official guide gives this as a reason to switch on "Internal Sale" ([official beginner's guide](https://www.capitalismlab.com/beginners-guide-gameplay-basics/); summarising fetch). In Offworld, the winner of a black-market item is simply whoever pays the current price. There is no queue, and a lockout timer after each purchase stops anyone chaining sabotage ([Designer Notes #13](https://www.designer-notes.com/otc-designer-notes-13-black-market-part-i); summarising fetch).

**Passive rivals read as clutter or loneliness.** A Game Dev Tycoon thread asking for rival companies says the game feels lonely, and a reply calls the rivals "fake competition" and asks to buy or shut down publishers ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/3048356136489099772); ⚠️ summarising fetch). Its only named companies are console makers and four publishers ([Companies](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Companies); community wiki, a stub page). A community guide adds that magazines score you against your own past best, not against rivals ([Success Guide](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Success_Guild_updated_for_1.7.8); community wiki; ⚠️ keyword search of an 81,000-character page, though this line is its first paragraph). A top-rated negative Startup Company review says its competition does nothing but wait to be bought ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/606800/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&filterLanguage=english); ⚠️ only the first page of about ten reviews was read). Railway Empire 2 players say the AI is a challenge for three or four years and then becomes a money sink ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1644320/discussions/1/7187237851765602048); summarising fetch; four posts). Railway Empire 1 players call its track-building terrible ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/503940/discussions/0/1640919737479133376); summarising fetch). The positive counterpoint is just as telling. One Railway Empire 2 poster praised the rivals for the competition and urgency they create ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1644320/discussions/1/3810657811760998526); summarising fetch). What players liked was time pressure, not the rival's mere presence. This is the same failure Game Night's "Lodestar takes this" banner risks: a rival that acts only at the round's end, on something the player was not racing for.

## Rivals are felt through numbers everyone already watches

Where rivalry works in these games, it reaches the player through a number on a screen they already use. Four kinds of number recur.

**A price that rises when anyone buys.** Offworld's black market is the best-documented case. Every purchase raises that item's price for all players, by less than a doubling, scaled by player count and slightly more for the buyer. The price jump tells everyone that someone just bought, say, a Mutiny, and prompts defensive buys whose own prices then rise. The designer, Soren Johnson, says the rising price was also the brake that kept sabotage from swallowing the economy ([Designer Notes #13](https://www.designer-notes.com/otc-designer-notes-13-black-market-part-i); summarising fetch). The buyer stays hidden, and Johnson describes the paranoia and wrong accusations that follow as a memorable part of play. The defensive item, Goon Squad, reveals the attacker when it catches a sabotage and hands the item back. It was added after months of play because players had no defence ([Designer Notes #13](https://www.designer-notes.com/otc-designer-notes-13-black-market-part-i); [Designer Notes #15, on a listing page](https://www.designer-notes.com/page/13); both summarising fetch). Capitalism Lab gives the supply-side version. The cost of acquiring a natural-resource site **rises sharply as more sites come into existence**, the premium shows on hover, and an AI company that pays a premium usually raises its selling price to recover the cost, so a rival's purchase becomes a later price rise the player can trace ([official Natural Resources page](https://www.capitalismlab.com/improvements/natural-resources/); summarising fetch). A community guide adds that AI firms think twice about the dearest deposit, so the player can buy the cheap ones and leave the expensive one as a deterrent ([Steam guide, community](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2969285443); summarising fetch).

**A saturation meter, and its warning.** In Big Pharma, competitors flooding a market with cheap drugs push saturation up and prices down. One player's single line of thirty pills drove saturation above 500% and the price to zero ([Steam thread, community, 2015](https://steamcommunity.com/app/344850/discussions/0/520518688951336366/); summarising fetch; the player cites the fan wiki). The warning comes in long games. Markets stayed saturated after a fourteen-year goal, and a developer from Twice Circled said the long-term saturation mechanics break down somewhat with three AIs and would be reviewed ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/344850/discussions/0/483367798515815515/); summarising fetch). A shared meter that rivals push but that never recovers turns into a dead market. This is the same lesson as Power Grid's fixed refill in the earlier research: prices must be able to come back down when buying slows.

**A visible list, dossier or calendar of what rivals will do.** Mad Games Tycoon 2 put a weekly top-ten chart on the main screen. Its Statistics menu lists every computer-run publisher and developer, sortable, with tooltips counting their games, and it lets the player hide companies that are not for sale. A 2022 rule locks companies from purchase for a set time ([First Year Anniversary post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4237325463675656030); ⚠️ keyword-filtered; [Company Acquisitions!](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4348798430274738151), read in full). Software Inc. built rival visibility patch by patch. It added a view of what competitors are researching in 2021 ([Alpha 11.7.62](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4032392655107365369)) and a full calendar of all upcoming releases and deadlines ([Alpha 11.7.43](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4059402835328033312)). In 2025 it began showing competitor products on the market triangle in the design window ([Beta 1.8.19](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1805431065498153)). ⚠️ All three are keyword-filtered lines matched to posts by date and title, and the researcher did not reopen each post to confirm the line sits in it. Most important for a no-dice game, Software Inc. added **a minimum six-month grace period before a new company spawns in an under-served market, to give the player reaction time** ([Publishers, piercings and project management](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/6339469370193392197), read in full). Scaling Laws, the closest design cousin to Game Night, gives each of its fourteen rival labs a dated history the player can open. The labs ship on the dates the real ones shipped and stall or collapse on their own schedule, not in reaction to the player's score. A dossier opened in 2023 cannot mention a 2024 collapse ([itch.io page](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws); [0.4.0 devlog](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws/devlog/1662867/040-the-best-strategy-in-my-game-was-cheating-and-i-only-found-it-by-measuring); both read raw in full). One named exception reacts: a single lab builds whatever the player is selling, so a specialism can be taken away ([0.2.0 devlog](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws/devlog/1654844/020-is-out-state-hands-you-a-country-your-rivals-hire-lawyers)). Its model creator has a "VS TOP MODEL" view that places the strongest rival model beside the player's projection. It shows capability only, because the developer refuses to invent rival costs that nobody publishes ([0.4.0 devlog](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws/devlog/1662867/040-the-best-strategy-in-my-game-was-cheating-and-i-only-found-it-by-measuring)). Text feeds are the cheapest channel and the noisiest. LLM Tycoon's "Chirp" feed, where rival CEOs hype their keynotes and mock the player's launches, needed a Mute button because notifications arrived too often ([0.8.0.0 post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1846651706690038)).

**A stake that counts toward control.** Several games make rival interest a slow, cumulative number. In Anno 1800, each island share is worth 6% of an island's balance. Holding every share of an island unlocks an expensive buyout that destroys all buildings except the trading post. The AI can do this to the player, so keeping one share of each island blocks it ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/916440/discussions/0/3767857515019307248); summarising fetch; no official source was found). In Railway Empire 1, each share purchase raises the marginal share price. A player guide quoted in a thread puts buying everything at once at about 150% of company value (the poster measured 160%), and merging buys the remainder at a 10% premium ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/503940/discussions/0/3799284366977733496); summarising fetch). In Capitalism Lab, one holder reaching **50% of your shares ends the game** in a hostile takeover ([official guide](https://www.capitalismlab.com/beginners-guide-gameplay-basics/); summarising fetch). Version 12.5 added a Holdings tree showing stakes and "effective interest" ([Version 12.5](https://www.capitalismlab.com/version125/); summarising fetch). Sid Meier's Railroads starts every player owning 50% of their own company and lets rivals buy the rest ([Shacknews preview](https://shacknews.com/article/44281/sid-meiers-railroads-preview); summarising fetch). Airline Tycoon 2 adds a neutral "Bad Bank" that buys shares from players and offers them to everyone, a market-maker that moves prices without a named rival ([Steam update notes](https://store.steampowered.com/news/7102/); summarising fetch). The earlier compute-deals research found that Offworld let players buy a rival's shares one at a time, so the remaining stock became a visible countdown, because players kept asking how close a rival was ([Designer Notes #17](https://www.designer-notes.com/otc-designer-notes-17-stock-market/), as reported in the earlier report).

**Rivals as counterparties for capacity are almost unexplored.** Only Scaling Laws lets the player propose to a rival lab, and one of its four proposals is **"buy their capacity"**. You pay to send a proposal, the answer comes days later, and a refusal does not refund the fee. Each card shows how likely that lab is to agree before you spend anything, and alliances step up at 90, 180 and 365 days and decay if neglected ([itch.io page](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws)). The stated odds are a dice roll, which the owner's rule forbids; the transferable parts are the price for asking and the delay. Only COLD AISLE advertises a rival's failure releasing hardware. When one of its 32 competitors becomes insolvent, the player can buy unknown lots of its hardware at liquidation ([Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5036820/?l=english); store text only, one user review). In Startup Company, merging a fully bought competitor takes seven days, and the player must prepare servers for the incoming users first. A rival deal therefore lands as a capacity demand ([Beta 16 post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2499943313644354101)).

## Supplier prices in tycoons climb over time and announce their changes

These games almost never use dice to move prices. They move them on a visible calendar, by generation, or by depreciation, and the direction is usually up for what the player has not yet bought.

**Game Dev Tycoon runs its whole supply side as a dated, announced timeline.** Each platform has a licence fee that rises by generation. Early platforms cost 50K to 200K, late ones 1M to 1.5M, and the PC has no licence fee at all ([Platforms](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Platforms); community wiki; ⚠️ for most platform pages only the fee and date fields were read). Release and retirement dates are fixed for each game length, and the game announces them as news in sequence. For the TES console the sequence runs from a rumour, to a confirmed release window, to a success report, to a dated discontinuation notice. The first console to leave, the G64, gets market-decline news, a bankruptcy item, a notice two months ahead that it will be withdrawn, and then an end-of-support item ([TES](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/TES); [Govodore G64](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Govodore_G64); community wiki, read in full). Market share follows a fixed table that the sales formula interpolates week by week, with no randomness ([Sales Algorithm](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Sales_Algorithm); community wiki; ⚠️ about 6,000 of 8,600 characters read). A small event shows "decline now, pay more later" without dice. An air conditioner is offered at 90K. Decline it and a later offer costs 180K. Decline that and the staff buy it themselves ([Random Events](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Random_Events); community wiki, read in full; the wiki marks the event's trigger as random). The developer itself warns that publisher offers often suggest poor combinations that lead to bad scores and penalties ([Greenheart Steam post, January 2024](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/5510783579247039069), read in full). The guide notes that players re-roll the publisher list by backing out of the screen or reloading ([Success Guide](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Success_Guild_updated_for_1.7.8); community wiki; ⚠️ keyword search). A hidden, rerollable board invites that exploit; a visible seeded board does not.

**Licences elsewhere get dearer, and moving prices get exploited.** Mad Games Tycoon 2 made IP licences much more expensive in 2021 and made them rise further over time in 2022. A global "loss of trust" event blocks new licences for a while ([Summer Update!](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4020014731791976970); [Update 2022.09.16A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4589754330749384797); [Patchnotes 2021.03.08A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4057152305727721555); ⚠️ all keyword-filtered). Software Inc. took the opposite step for a specific reason. In 2019 it switched software licences from a one-time fee to monthly payments during development ([Alpha 11 progress update](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2404270358325306210), read in full). In January 2020 it **fixed the licence price to stop a licence-fee exploit** ([Alpha 11.4](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2600199781650668700); ⚠️ keyword-filtered, and the note gives this line together with three neighbouring posts, so the exact post should be spot-checked). In 2021 it charged licences per employee per project ([Alpha 11.7.55](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4072920612516126302); ⚠️ keyword-filtered). The developer removed a moving price because players found a loophole in it. Any moving compute price in Game Night needs the same scrutiny.

**Scaling Laws prices hardware the way the real market does.** Renting costs cloud prices that lag the market by 180 days. Buying costs roughly a third as much per unit of compute, but you absorb the loss. **An accelerator bought on launch day is worth about half its price that same day and loses another quarter every time a better one ships.** Delivery delay is a priced option: the order window shows units, unit price, total and delivery date, and one slider lets you pay up to half again for up to six sevenths off the wait. The developer calls it the only place in the game where money buys calendar time ([itch.io page](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws)). Mad Games Tycoon 2 gives delay a cost by rule: pre-orders are cancelled if buyers wait too long, or if the price rises ([Update 2022.09.16A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4589754330749384797); ⚠️ keyword-filtered). That maps directly onto a Verde chip order that arrives months late. Capitalism Lab runs a four-stage boom and bust cycle shown on an economic-graphs page ([official macroeconomics page](https://www.capitalismlab.com/new-features/enhanced-macroeconomic-simulation/); summarising fetch). Its manual's own warehouse example is crude oil bought ahead of expected price rises, so stockpiling is a sanctioned bet ([official Warehouse page](https://www.capitalismlab.com/new-content/warehouse/); summarising fetch). Its seaports can stop supplying, depending on difficulty ([official guide](https://www.capitalismlab.com/beginners-guide-gameplay-basics/); summarising fetch).

**Developers who used dice moved away from them, and players asked for warnings.** Johnson changed Offworld's Pirates from a 50% roll per shot, which felt streaky, to a sure hit that takes half the cargo, and stopped Goon Squads randomly killing Power Surges after players called it a bad bounce. His stated goals include avoiding randomness after a player's choice ([Designer Notes #14](https://www.designer-notes.com/otc-designer-notes-14-black-market-part-ii/); [#15](https://www.designer-notes.com/page/13); summarising fetch). Scaling Laws keeps one roll but telegraphs it. A serious safety incident opens a file, the verdict comes five days later, and the developer says that delay is what separates a hard game from an arbitrary one ([itch.io page](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws)). Big Pharma shows what happens without the warning. Rival patents that lock the player out of a drug they already make arrive with no warning. A 55-comment thread asks for about three months' notice, a way to contest, or a limit to drugs not yet made ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/344850/discussions/0/520518053447819104/); summarising fetch; ⚠️ 15 of 55 comments seen). The workaround players found was switching delivery form, from a pill to a cream or syringe ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/344850/discussions/0/520518053442765849/); summarising fetch). RimWorld's Cassandra and Phoebe storytellers seed five possible caravan dates per settlement per year, at least six days apart, and orbital traders alternate seven active and eight inactive days. But each caravan date then passes a faction-relations check, which is a dice-like step on top of the seed ([RimWorld Wiki: Trade](https://www.rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Trade); community wiki; ⚠️ about 100,000 of 115,032 characters read). Several AI-lab games still roll dice: Startup Company's viral boosts and DDoS attacks ([Devlog #28](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2447035533305992625)) and Rise of Industry's contract offers. Those are contrasts, not models.

## Offer cards work at four facts, one penalty and a visible clock

The contract-board games converge on a compact card. They differ in where they put the strings and the rival pressure.

**Against the Storm keeps each trade offer to four facts.** Each card lists the goods wanted, the Amber payment, the travel cost and the travel time, and the player sets the quantity with arrow buttons in size steps. Offers reset every season and depend on the settlement's biome and on a five-level standing, where higher standing brings better offers and shorter travel ([Eremite, "Time is Money" post](https://eremitegames.com/time-is-money-update/); summarising fetch). Its "Timed Order" is a card with a countdown and a single fixed penalty: fail it and you lose the one reputation point it would have granted. The developers added it for excitement and to reward reckless risk-taking ([same post](https://eremitegames.com/time-is-money-update/); summarising fetch). Players still found the board weak. In 2022 they called trade routes boring and inconsequential, behind a half-hidden menu ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1336490/discussions/0/3547176090248233536); summarising fetch). After the rework, early offers felt like a loss, and replies said routes only pay at standing two to four ([Steam thread, community, March 2024](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1336490/discussions/0/4286936717520753825); summarising fetch; ⚠️ 15 of 31 comments shown). The lesson is that a lean card does not fix weak value. Against the Storm has no rival who takes offers; its pressure is the season timer.

**Football Manager keeps the card light by folding strings into one running total.** Clauses sit behind a menu, and a single line at the bottom carries the all-in future cost ([official FM26 image](https://cdn.footballmanager.com/site/inline-images/4.%20EN%5B1%5D_opt.jpg); viewed). The researcher counts about nine facts on the left pane, grouped into three labelled blocks. That is the researcher's own count from the screenshot. **RimWorld puts a clock on every visitor.** Orbital ships stay 40,000 ticks (16 in-game hours), and a notice says when one has left comms range ([RimWorld Wiki: Orbital trader](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Orbital_trader); community wiki; ⚠️ about 100,000 of 115,147 characters read). Quests separate an acceptance deadline from a completion timer, and missing either counts as failure ([RimWorld Wiki: Quests](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Quests); community wiki; ⚠️ about 100,000 of 128,592 characters read).

**Strings that change who wins come from relationships and performance.** Software Inc.'s 2024 publisher rework lets the player choose between deals. Each success improves the relationship, and the publisher then offers better deals. Publishers take 75% of a product's fans and hold a recoup target before lowering their royalty cut ([Publishers, piercings and project management](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/6339469370193392197), read in full). Earlier, companies began cancelling marketing and hosting deals when the player performed badly ([Alpha 10 post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2454911758748566987); ⚠️ keyword-filtered). Mad Games Tycoon 2 added the option to cancel exclusive publisher contracts in January 2022, then raised the cancellation penalties and tied them to difficulty. Exclusive games also have part of their development cost covered ([Company Acquisitions!](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4348798430274738151), read in full). COLD AISLE advertises customer contracts negotiated on price, duration, capacity, peak load, service level and penalties, with follow-up offers and ratings for measured reliability ([Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5036820/?l=english); store text only). Game Dev Tycoon's trade-show booth offers a size choice the player can read from a visible number: small, medium or large booths matched to fan-count bands of under 40,000, 40,000 to 100,000, and over 100,000 ([Convention](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Convention); community wiki, read in full).

**Capacity screens failed for want of a display, not a mechanic.** Startup Company's capacity is one number (compute units) set by the weakest of three server throughputs ([Steam thread, developer reply](https://steamcommunity.com/app/606800/discussions/0/1727575977542193508/)). Players still asked for years how the throughputs combine. The developer shipped a bottleneck alert and then a help system ([Beta 22 post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/3140604542246097950); [Devlog #59](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2609208878398734460)), and a top review still dismisses hosting as busy work ([Steam reviews](https://steamcommunity.com/app/606800/reviews/?browsefilter=toprated&filterLanguage=english); ⚠️ first page only). Scaling Laws documents three fixes that were all missing displays. In one measured campaign, serving load sat near 0% for eight years, with capacity 200 to 3,082 times demand and a fleet bill of $5.8M a day at 4% utilisation, and nothing on screen showed it. A 0.7.0 resale ladder now sells idle capacity at half its cost, and the developer shows that overbuying still never pays ($139.5M against $270.0M). In another campaign, three quarters of the audience left in a month because a better model was four times heavier to serve and nothing said so before the run was paid for. The scale page now prints how many people the fleet keeps served on the model being designed, in amber when that is fewer than today ([itch.io page](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws)). LLM Tycoon replaced compute-allocation sliders with typed values and added a message when there is not enough compute for research or training ([0.8.0.0 post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1846651706690038)). Mad Games Tycoon 2 cuts MMO sales when server space runs out, lets players reserve server rooms by use, and shows server costs as their own line in the finances ([Summer Update!](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4020014731791976970); [Sandbox mode 2.0](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4836332027673671222); [Official Release Date post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/5151600576584832658); ⚠️ all keyword-filtered). Game Dev Tycoon draws the opposite complaint: its game-history ledger omits licence fees and salaries, so a fee is paid and never itemised again ([Success Guide](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Success_Guild_updated_for_1.7.8); community wiki; ⚠️ keyword search).

## Conclusion

The research changes what "rivals felt in every option" has to mean. Game Night's banner already copies the pattern players rejected: a rival that acts at the round's end, on something the player was not watching. The games where rivalry lands do something different. They put the rival's effect on a number the player was already reading, such as a price, a saturation level or a share count, and they announce the rival's move before it happens: a six-month notice, a dated dossier, a counted interest line with a deadline. That points to a design rule that is cheap to apply to all seven options. Every deal card and every compute screen should show one rival-driven number and one rival-dated event. The evidence also moves where the risk sits. Moving prices are not dangerous in themselves, since nearly every game here moves them on a calendar. The danger is the loophole: Software Inc. froze a moving licence price to close an exploit, and Big Pharma's saturation never recovered. That makes O2's refill rule and O6's drift-back the parts to test hardest.

The second shift is that a contested deal is genuinely unexplored. Rise of Industry is the only tycoon that lets a rival take a contract, and it got there by slowing the game to speed 1 and accepting bankruptcies. That is a warning about the cost of contested deals and also proof they can run in real time. Football Manager shows that contest without bidding: a count, a date and a few buttons. Game Night can occupy a space no tycoon in this set fills, if each contest comes from a seeded schedule the player can read ahead of time.

## What the tycoon evidence does to the seven options

Each paragraph below covers which tycoon patterns strengthen the option, how rivals become felt in it, and whether its ranking changes. Where the paragraph proposes a rival element, that proposal is this report's inference from the cited patterns, not a sourced finding. The current recommendation in `docs/research/compute-deals/options.md` is O1, then O4, then O2, with O3 as the lighter alternative, O5 as the best add-on, and O6 and O7 as experimental.

### O1 · Free compute you can always see

**Strengthened, stays first.** Tycoon developers fixed capacity confusion by adding displays, which is O1's whole idea. Scaling Laws' served-people line came from a measured failure (three quarters of an audience lost), and its resale ladder came from a fleet at 4% utilisation that nothing on screen revealed ([itch.io page](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws)). Startup Company needed a bottleneck alert and a help system ([Beta 22](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/3140604542246097950); [Devlog #59](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2609208878398734460)). LLM Tycoon swapped sliders for typed values plus a "not enough compute" message ([0.8.0.0 post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/1846651706690038)), which backs O1's steppers over the two-handle bar. Game Dev Tycoon's ledger complaint ([Success Guide](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Success_Guild_updated_for_1.7.8), community wiki, ⚠️ keyword search) argues for itemising each contract's monthly cost. **How rivals become felt:** O1 as written has no rival element. Scaling Laws' "VS TOP MODEL" view, which puts the strongest rival beside the player's projection ([0.4.0 devlog](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws/devlog/1662867/040-the-best-strategy-in-my-game-was-cheating-and-i-only-found-it-by-measuring)), suggests a second headline line under "Free for training": the leading rival's next run and its start date, for example "OpenBrain starts a Large run on day 20". The "Get compute" button would then open a board where rival pressure is already visible.

### O2 · Size your own deal on a price ladder

**Strengthened, and the best vehicle for making rivals felt; stays third.** Capitalism Lab is a tycoon-scale version of the ladder. Resource sites get sharply dearer as more are bought, the premium shows on hover, and a rival that pays a premium passes it on in its selling price ([Natural Resources](https://www.capitalismlab.com/improvements/natural-resources/); summarising fetch). Railway Empire 1's rising marginal share price is the same curve applied to stakes ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/503940/discussions/0/3799284366977733496); summarising fetch). Against the Storm's arrow-button quantity steps show the size control can stay small on a card ([Time is Money](https://eremitegames.com/time-is-money-update/); summarising fetch). Two warnings shape the tuning. Big Pharma's saturation that never recovered ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/344850/discussions/0/483367798515815515/); summarising fetch) shows the refill must bring cheap steps back when demand slows. Software Inc.'s exploit-driven freeze ([Alpha 11.4](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2600199781650668700); ⚠️ keyword-filtered) says to probe the ladder for buy-sell loops. **How rivals become felt:** rivals' buys take the cheap steps, as O2 already says. Offworld adds the telegraph: show the step a rival just took as a price jump on the card the moment it happens, not only at the round's end ([Designer Notes #13](https://www.designer-notes.com/otc-designer-notes-13-black-market-part-i); summarising fetch). Capitalism Lab adds the trace: a rival that bought dear raises its own prices later (in Game Night terms, its model sizes or its spot resale).

### O3 · The ripening row

**Weakened; drops below O5.** No tycoon in these notes has offers that get cheaper the longer they wait. The direction is consistently the reverse. Game Dev Tycoon's air conditioner goes from 90K to 180K when declined ([Random Events](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Random_Events); community wiki). Mad Games Tycoon 2's licences rise over time ([Update 2022.09.16A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4589754330749384797); ⚠️ keyword-filtered). Scaling Laws' rental prices lag a rising market ([itch.io page](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws)). The only "cheaper with time" pattern is the depreciation of *older* hardware when a better chip ships, and that belongs to O5's announced chip launches, not to a sliding row. O3's sources were already weak (AI-written rule summaries), and the real 2026 market rose. **How rivals become felt:** O3's dated banner ("DeepThink signs this on day 18") is good and matches Football Manager's deadline line ([FM26 image](https://cdn.footballmanager.com/site/inline-images/4.%20EN%5B1%5D_opt.jpg)). But that banner works better inside the contested-deal option below (N1) than on a row whose prices fall. If O3 survives, flipping its direction, so that cards get dearer each round as rivals circle them, would match every tycoon source here. That is an inference, and it would make O3 close to N1.

### O4 · Forecast and commit

**Strengthened, stays second.** Scaling Laws states O4's tension in its own design notes: rent too little and a queue forms and people leave; rent too much and the bill eats the training run; no setting of that slider is correct. Its arithmetic that overbuying never pays, even with resale, is the kind of grade O4's era-end CFO line would give ([itch.io page](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws)). Software Inc.'s move from a one-time licence to monthly charges per employee per project ([Alpha 11 progress update](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2404270358325306210)) supports showing committed compute as a monthly bill on the strip. Rise of Industry's warning to accept only what you can finish ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/671440/discussions/0/3124866920644349038/); summarising fetch) is the downside of a firm commitment against a bad forecast. Software Inc.'s practice of cancelling deals after poor performance ([Alpha 10](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2454911758748566987); ⚠️ keyword-filtered) suggests that flexible terms can carry a supplier-side exit too. **How rivals become felt:** Software Inc.'s calendar of all upcoming releases and deadlines ([Alpha 11.7.43](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4059402835328033312); ⚠️ keyword-filtered) and Scaling Laws' dated rival histories suggest adding rival markers to the forecast strip: when each rival's next big run or fleet expansion lands on the seeded schedule. The player then reads their need line against the moments rivals will drain the shared market.

### O5 · Supplier weather: telegraphed shocks and strings that come due

**Strengthened most; rises into the recommended set, ahead of O3.** The tycoon evidence for announced, scheduled change is the most consistent in the set, and much of it is better sourced than the board-game rules O5 rested on. Game Dev Tycoon runs its entire supply side as a rumour, then a confirmed date, then a release, then a dated withdrawal notice ([G64](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Govodore_G64); community wiki, read in full). Software Inc. built a six-month notice before a new rival appears, explicitly for reaction time ([publishers post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/6339469370193392197), read in full). Scaling Laws makes the five-day warning the point of the design ([itch.io page](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws)). Big Pharma's players asked for about three months' warning on rival patents ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/344850/discussions/0/520518053447819104/); summarising fetch; ⚠️ 15 of 55 comments). Mad Games Tycoon 2's licence freeze and cancelled pre-orders ([Patchnotes 2021.03.08A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4057152305727721555); [Update 2022.09.16A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4589754330749384797); ⚠️ keyword-filtered) are ready-made string cards. Scaling Laws' depreciation rule (half on launch day, a further quarter per better chip) gives a concrete curve for "Rubin ships in three rounds: older chips drop". **How rivals become felt:** some of the weather should be rival-made and dated. Scaling Laws ties three rival collapses to the player's own exposures ([0.4.0 devlog](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws/devlog/1662867/040-the-best-strategy-in-my-game-was-cheating-and-i-only-found-it-by-measuring)), and COLD AISLE turns a rival's insolvency into hardware for sale ([Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5036820/?l=english); store text only). Announcements such as "Qilin's home-made chips enter volume in two rounds: Verde's queue shortens" or "OpenBrain's campus comes online in April: spot rises" keep the schedule visible and make rivals its cause. O5 also costs less to build than O2 and needs fewer changes to rival rules. That is why it now ranks with the three.

### O6 · The compute exchange

**Unchanged; stays experimental.** Offworld remains its main precedent, and nothing in the tycoon notes adds a second real-time shared market for an input. The notes add design parts and warnings. Airline Tycoon 2's neutral Bad Bank ([Steam update notes](https://store.steampowered.com/news/7102/); summarising fetch) and Capitalism Lab's always-open seaport ([official guide](https://www.capitalismlab.com/beginners-guide-gameplay-basics/); summarising fetch) are models for the market-maker that keeps the exchange liquid when no rival trades. Scaling Laws' resale at half price ([itch.io page](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws)) suggests idle compute should sell at a discount, not at the ticker price. The warnings are Software Inc.'s exploit freeze, Big Pharma's unrecovering saturation, and Capitalism Lab's advice to check seaport prices every two to three months because imports can rise or stop ([Steam guide, community](https://steamcommunity.com/sharedfiles/filedetails/?id=2969285443); summarising fetch). **How rivals become felt:** as written, through Offworld's anonymous price spikes, plus Offworld's Goon Squad idea that a defensive purchase can reveal who is pushing the price ([Designer Notes #13](https://www.designer-notes.com/otc-designer-notes-13-black-market-part-i); summarising fetch). The balance risk is still the highest of the seven.

### O7 · Sealed bids for the bottleneck

**Slightly strengthened; stays experimental.** Rise of Industry is the first real-time tycoon precedent for computer rivals bidding on the same contract. It also shows how to make bidding survivable in real time: slow the game to speed 1 when an auction opens ([patch-notes thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/671440/discussions/4/1815422173033520728); summarising fetch). Its problems are O7's risks. Players could not tell what they would earn before bidding ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/671440/discussions/0/5185299686169862094/); summarising fetch). A lost auction cancelled a signed contract and charged a penalty. Bankruptcies were accepted as intended. Sid Meier's Railroads' technology auctions, where bids get absurd and passing can cost a ten-year patent ([Shacknews preview](https://shacknews.com/article/44281/sid-meiers-railroads-preview); summarising fetch), warn against uncapped bids on a decisive asset. Football Manager's ultimatum to match the highest bid ([official FM page](https://www.footballmanager.com/features/deadline-day); ⚠️ search snippet only) is a one-step alternative to open bidding. **How rivals become felt:** directly, as bidders. O7's prepayment returned to losers keeps Rise of Industry's harshness out. The feed tells about appetite ("OpenBrain booked every spare GPU in Iowa") play the role Offworld's price bumps play. O7 still covers only the era 3 queue, so its reach is narrow.

## Two new options the evidence supports

### N1 · The contested deal (medium)

**The idea.** A few big cards each era (a Verde tranche, the Gulf campus, a large Azuria block) carry visible rival interest and a dated deadline. The card shows "2 labs interested" and one advisor line in plain words: "OpenBrain has asked Verde for an answer by day 18." The player can sign at the printed price, pay a fixed fee to stall (which moves the deadline by a stated number of days, once), or let it go. If the player passes or the deadline arrives, the named rival takes the card on that day, a "has left" notice appears, and the feed shows what the rival gained ("OpenBrain's fleet +40 units from April"). Which rival is interested, and on what day, comes from the seeded schedule, so nothing is rolled. A passing player could get a small consolation, such as first refusal on the rival's leftover capacity. This differs from today's banner because the interest is counted and dated ahead of the round's end. It differs from O3 because the price does not slide, and from O7 because nobody bids.

**Sources.** Football Manager's transfer-offer screen: a count of interested clubs, an advisor line giving the other side's deadline, a Stall button and a running total cost ([official FM26 image](https://cdn.footballmanager.com/site/inline-images/4.%20EN%5B1%5D_opt.jpg), viewed). FM27's offers that ramp up visibly as the window closes so that they do not surprise ([FM27 page](https://www.footballmanager.com/fm27/features/deeper-more-immersive-world); summarising fetch). Mad Games Tycoon 2's declined deals that go to a rival publisher ([Steam announcement](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4186652991113913673); ⚠️ keyword-filtered). Rise of Industry's per-region contracts that rivals can win, and its slowdown to speed 1 so the player can act ([patch-notes thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/671440/discussions/4/1815422173033520728); summarising fetch). RimWorld's "leaves in N" clocks and departure notices ([Orbital trader](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Orbital_trader); community wiki; ⚠️ partial read). Against the Storm's single fixed penalty on a Timed Order ([Time is Money](https://eremitegames.com/time-is-money-update/); summarising fetch). Software Inc.'s six-month notice before a rival moves ([publishers post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/6339469370193392197), read in full). The consolation for passing comes from For Sale, as reported in the earlier compute-deals research ([UltraBoardGames For Sale](https://ultraboardgames.com/for-sale/game-rules.php)). **Gap:** no source documents how Football Manager decides which clubs bid or how its deadlines are set. The seeded schedule has to be designed, not copied.

**Fit.** It fixes the banner problem directly and gives denial a cost the player chooses (signing early to keep a card from OpenBrain is a timed choice, not a fight). It also needs only one prompt per contest, which suits one human against bots in real time. It touches the same deal-board and rival-deal code as O3, and it could replace O3.

### N2 · Supplier stakes and rival fire sales (large)

**The idea.** The equity-for-compute card grows into stakes running both ways. The player can buy a supplier stake in steps (for example in CoreFlame or a Gulf campus operator), at a marginal price that rises with each step. Each step buys something concrete: a cheaper ladder, first refusal on that supplier's next tranche, or the right to block rivals from it. Rival labs hold stakes too, shown as one ownership bar per supplier, with a visible threshold at which the holder gets control. On seeded, announced dates a weak rival lab or a fragile neocloud sells off. Its compute appears on the board as a dated fire-sale lot, with its contents shown, and the labs with stakes in it get first call. Rivals become felt as co-owners and as sources of sudden supply on a known date.

**Sources.** Anno 1800's 6% island shares, the full-ownership buyout and the one-share block ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/916440/discussions/0/3767857515019307248); summarising fetch; no official source). Railway Empire 1's rising marginal share price, about 150–160% of value for a full buyout plus a 10% merge premium ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/503940/discussions/0/3799284366977733496); summarising fetch). Capitalism Lab's 50% takeover rule and Holdings tree ([official guide](https://www.capitalismlab.com/beginners-guide-gameplay-basics/); [Version 12.5](https://www.capitalismlab.com/version125/); summarising fetch), and its "Internal Sale" switch that keeps rivals off your supply ([official guide](https://www.capitalismlab.com/beginners-guide-gameplay-basics/)). Software Inc.'s share-by-share buying of rivals and its later rule that AI companies cannot dilute once one holder has more than 50% ([Beta 1.7.28](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/6300039510123701995); ⚠️ keyword-filtered, the note groups this line with two other posts). COLD AISLE's liquidation lots from insolvent rivals ([Steam store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/5036820/?l=english); store text only). Scaling Laws' "buy their capacity" proposal and its rival collapses tied to the player's exposures ([itch.io page](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws); [0.4.0 devlog](https://hcklab.itch.io/scaling-laws/devlog/1662867/040-the-best-strategy-in-my-game-was-cheating-and-i-only-found-it-by-measuring)). Startup Company's merger, which forces capacity preparation ([Beta 16](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/2499943313644354101)). Mad Games Tycoon 2's subsidiaries paying about $20 per sale against $8–12 from an unowned publisher ([Steam thread, community](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/6292161380826636698); ⚠️ summarising fetch, one player's claims at one difficulty).

**Warnings from the same sources.** Ownership features read as dull unless the payoff is clear early. Mad Games Tycoon 2 players found buyouts paid off only late. Railway Empire 2 players called the AI a money sink once they started buying it. Startup Company's rivals did nothing but wait to be bought. Software Inc. needed extra rules once someone passed 50%. COLD AISLE's lots are "unknown", which conflicts with the owner's no-dice rule, so Game Night's lots would need their contents shown. This option touches rival compute and late-game rival rules, which belong to `gn-improve`, so it is the larger and riskier of the two.

But most Mad Games Tycoon 2 and Software Inc. patch lines were found by keyword filtering (⚠️), some links were matched to posts by date and title without reopening them, and Steam store pages and forum threads came through a summarising fetch (⚠️). `contract_boards_and_offers.md`, `rival_firms_racing.md` and `supply_chain_business_sims.md` were read entirely through a fetch tool that returns a model-written digest, so "read in full" there means only that the digest covered the whole page. Game Dev Tycoon, RimWorld and Anno 1800 share rules rest on community wikis and player threads, not developer documents. No game was played, and no demo was tried. Four of the five notes state that no email address was used in any request (`rival_firms_racing.md` does not say), and none appears in this report.

**Limits.** The notes' coverage statements are the researchers' own claims and cannot be checked from outside. Any finding that will drive a design change, especially the Rise of Industry auction rules (probable developer replies, no official changelog) and the Anno and Railway Empire share numbers (community only), should be traced to its source before it is built on. The option rankings are this report's judgement from how directly each piece of evidence meets a Game Night problem; no mechanic here has been playtested.
