## 1. Power: queues, behind-the-meter gas, nuclear, curtailment-for-speed deals, equipment. What is new since September 2026?

### Takeaway
The biggest new fact is institutional. On 9 October 2026 FERC refused PJM's plan to let data centers connect early in exchange for being cut off first, and told the states to decide who gets curtailed. That leaves "accept being switched off to get power sooner" as a real but legally unsettled bargain. Everywhere else the pattern holds: the grid queue is enormous (ERCOT 474 GW), so labs build their own gas, and the government is now willing to commandeer data centers' backup generators in emergencies.

### Cited Findings

**A. Curtailment-for-speed (the "get cut off at peak, connect faster" bargain)**

- PJM filed on 13 August 2026 for an "Interim Resource Adequacy Service" (IRAS): loads of at least 50 MW entering after 1 June 2027 could connect without matching new generation if they agreed to be curtailed before PJM called on pre-emergency demand response; curtailed loads would be paid up to half of PJM's non-performance charge rate, and a customer could waive payment. ⚠️ the filing details are from a search-engine summary of PJM/Utility Dive/market-monitor documents, not read directly — [search summary citing PJM filing coverage](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ferc-pjm-large-load-interconnection-iras-byonc/832716/)
- **FERC order of 9 October 2026 (new):** approved PJM's 50 MW large-load definition and a large-load "registry" (location, ramp schedule, capacity supply); rejected IRAS and the "bring your own new capacity" rules; said IRAS put obligations on retail customers, chose which retail customers are curtailed first, set a retail rate, and appeared to discriminate against large loads, all of which belong to the states. Commissioner Rosner said states should set curtailment priority and that non-critical data centers should be cut before homes, hospitals and schools, but only after voluntary demand response is exhausted. Jefferies said the ruling means higher capacity prices for longer and a states-led version could return, with Ohio and Pennsylvania the ones to watch. ⚠️ Utility Dive page returned only its opening summary and excerpts (no docket number, vote count or full registry rules) — [Utility Dive, 12 Oct 2026](https://www.utilitydive.com/news/ferc-pjm-large-load-interconnection-iras-byonc/832716/)
- Same-day corroboration on the registry taking effect 12 October and docket ER26-3515. ⚠️ search snippet only — [TechTarget](https://www.techtarget.com/it-infrastructure/news/366652121/FERC-approves-PJM-load-registry-rejects-curtailment-plan)
- Texas: the large-load queue is more than 470 GW (474 GW, "421% higher than the current peak load record") as of July 2026. ERCOT's new "Provisional Controllable Load Resource" (PCLR) lets a load connect faster in exchange for being exposed to ERCOT-directed curtailment. Aurora's model (for Google and V-Wire) finds standalone PCLRs are curtailed in all five weather years: 50 to 241 hours a year in the central year, 35 hours in the mildest and 550 in the most extreme (0.35% to 3.47% of energy); a co-sited 4-hour battery with "front-of-meter netting" cuts curtailment hours 83%, versus 53% without netting. Source is a commissioned study by interested parties, stamped confidential though posted on ERCOT's site. ⚠️ the 182 KB slide deck was only read from its executive summary (about the first 9 KB of extracted text); the rest was not read — [Aurora/ERCOT LLWG deck, Sept 2026](https://www.ercot.com/files/docs/2026/09/10/Curtailment-Mitigation-for-PCLRs-Modeling-the-Effects-of-Front-of-Meter-Netting_LLWG.pdf)
- Texas SB 6: loads of 75 MW or more must disclose backup generation of at least half their size; ERCOT can order them to deploy it or curtail before or during an emergency; remote-disconnect authority; emergency-curtailment rule still being written. On 24 July 2026 the Texas PUC's first SB 6 test case reportedly held emergency curtailment of a co-located data center is not capped by the size of its paired behind-the-meter generator. ⚠️ search snippets of law-firm alerts, not fetched (White & Case returned HTTP 403) — [White & Case](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/puct-affirms-curtailment-authority-over-co-located-data-centers-first-net-metering); [Foley](https://www.foley.com/insights/publications/2026/03/public-utility-commission-of-texas-issues-proposed-rules-for-large-load-interconn/)
- Google signed "demand response" deals with five US utilities, announced 19 March 2026, making up to 1 GW of its data-center load curtailable at peak; the first two deals were Indiana Michigan Power and TVA in 2025. Commercial terms undisclosed. ⚠️ secondary coverage, search snippet — [Vozpopuli summary](https://www.vozpopuli.com/indux/en/google-is-signing-new-utility-deals-to-curb-data-center-power-use-during-peak-demand-and-the-ai-boom-is-now-forcing-big-tech-to-rethink-how-it-consumes-electricity/2862/); [Data Center Frontier, 2025](https://www.datacenterfrontier.com/energy/article/55316600/google-partners-with-utilities-to-ease-ai-data-center-grid-strain)
- Background to the FERC story: DOE Secretary Wright on 23 October 2025 directed FERC to open a large-load interconnection rulemaking (loads over 20 MW, 60-day studies for flexible loads, large loads paying 100% of network upgrades), with an April 2026 target. I could not find whether FERC finished that rule. ⚠️ law-firm alerts surfaced by search only — [Orrick](https://www.orrick.com/en/Insights/2025/11/FERC-Grapples-With-Surging-Reliability-and-Interconnection-Demands-From-Data-Centers); [Kirkland](https://www.kirkland.com/publications/kirkland-alert/2025/11/potential-game-changing-rulemaking-for-data-centers)

Decision tension: take the cheap, fast connection that comes with a "you are cut off first" clause (and risk your training run being switched off on the hottest day), or wait years for firm power.

**B. Government can commandeer data-center backup power**

- 18 to 20 May 2026: DOE Emergency Order 202-26-23 authorised PJM to direct backup generation at data centers and other large loads to run as a last resort before or during a Level 3 Energy Emergency Alert (the level where firm load is cut), requiring customers to meet environmental limits "to the maximum extent feasible." Similar orders went to ERCOT (24 Jan 2026, order 202-26-01), PJM (26 Jan, 202-26-06) and Duke in January 2026. Law firm reading: DOE is increasingly likely to let grid operators compel private backup power into service, and curtailment risk will show up in contracts. — [Gibson Dunn client alert, 21 May 2026](https://www.gibsondunn.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/doe-emergency-order-signals-new-role-for-data-centers-backup-power.pdf) (read in full via local PDF text extraction); January orders ⚠️ from search snippet — [DOE order page](https://www.energy.gov/ceser/federal-power-act-section-202c-pjm-interconnection-llc-pjm-order-no-202-26-23)
- About three weeks before 22 July, PJM issued its first-ever "Emergency Use of Backup-Generator Warning" during a record heat wave; it was not escalated and no generators were dispatched. — [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/fault-in-data-center-alley-triggered-3-gw-load-drop-on-pjm)
- 22 July 2026: a transmission-line fault in Ashburn, Virginia caused more than 3 GW of data-center load (about 3% of PJM demand) to drop off the grid within seconds. Dominion says the data centers' own protection systems switched to backup power; PJM saw a frequency change but no reliability impact; NERC is reviewing; a similar 2024 event dropped about 1.5 GW. An analyst suggests hyperscale campuses may need ride-through rules like large generators (a proposal, not a rule). — [Data Center Knowledge](https://www.datacenterknowledge.com/energy-power-supply/fault-in-data-center-alley-triggered-3-gw-load-drop-on-pjm) (read in full); one blog account of a tree strike and 1,800 MW conflicts with Dominion/Reuters-based accounts, treat as unverified — [remio.ai](https://www.remio.ai/post/a-3-gigawatt-grid-shock-exposes-an-ai-data-center-control-problem)

Decision tension: install big backup generators that can save your site in an emergency, knowing the government (and the neighbours) may then insist you run them when the grid is short.

**C. Price signal and politics of power**

- PJM's 2028/29 capacity auction (14 July 2026) cleared at the legislated cap of about $325 per MW-day for a third straight auction (uncapped it would have been $555), fell about 6,831 MW short of the reliability requirement (14.7% reserve margin against a 20% target); the market monitor attributed $6.3 billion of the cost to data centers and 46.2% of the price increase across the last four auctions to them. ⚠️ the Sierra Club page fetch returned empty; figures are from search summaries of the monitor and press — [Sierra Club NJ](https://www.sierraclub.org/new-jersey/blog/2026/07/pjm-auction-hits-price-cap-again-and-falls-short-projected-power-supply); [Sun-Gazette](https://www.sungazette.com/news/business/2026/07/future-regional-power-supply-down-but-not-out/)
- PJM says it will begin curtailing large (50 MW+) data centers in June 2027 as a late step; its 2027/28 procurement fell 6,600 MW (5.6%) short of the reserve target. ⚠️ blog summary of a TechCrunch report, not the original — [remio.ai](https://www.remio.ai/post/pjm-plans-data-center-power-cuts-as-grid-pressure-mounts). Note this plan is the one FERC just rejected (IRAS), so treat the June 2027 date as unsettled.
- White House "Ratepayer Protection Pledge," 4 March 2026: nonbinding commitment that hyperscalers "build, bring, or buy" their own new generation, pay for grid upgrades, negotiate take-or-pay style rates (pay whether they use it or not), invest locally, and make backup generation available in shortages. The page names no companies. ⚠️ signatories (Google, Amazon, Meta, xAI in March; 200+ later; 23 governors) come from a search summary only — [White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/presidential-actions/2026/03/ratepayer-protection-pledge/) (read in full); [signatory summary](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/?p=355014)

Decision tension: pay the full cost of your own power and grid upgrades to win public goodwill, or keep your costs lower and let households carry the bill (and the headlines).

**D. Behind-the-meter gas: speed against permission and trust**

- xAI ran 27 turbines in Southaven, Mississippi without permits from summer 2025 under a "temporary-mobile" exemption (up to a year); NAACP announced intent to sue on 13 February 2026; Mississippi's permit board unanimously approved a permit for 41 permanent turbines on 10 March 2026 after a hearing with unanimous local opposition. xAI said this unlocks 1.2 GW of self-generated power (the 1.2 GW is xAI's statement via a snippet; the article read in full gives no MW). — [Mississippi Free Press](https://www.mississippifreepress.org/mississippi-permit-board-grants-xais-request-for-41-southaven-gas-turbines-to-power-memphis-data-center/) (read in full); ⚠️ 1.2 GW and EPA 15 January 2026 turbine-standards claim from search snippet — [WDAM](https://www.wdam.com/2026/03/11/state-approves-xais-use-gas-turbines-southaven/)
- Cleanview counts 46 US data-center projects with 56 GW of planned behind-the-meter capacity, about 30% of tracked planned capacity, 90% announced in 2025. CBRE: grid power for existing projects is largely committed through 2030 in most US markets. Orders: Wärtsilä 412 MW of gas engines for an off-grid Ohio hyperscale site (operation targeted early 2028); Crusoe bought 13 PE6000 50-MW aeroderivative sets (650 MW); a three-year framework for 1.25 GW of Jenbacher engines; Nscale's West Virginia campus with 2 GW of Caterpillar engines tied to a Microsoft/NVIDIA letter of intent for up to 1.35 GW of compute. — [Power Engineering](https://www.power-eng.com/gas/data-center-power-crunch-lifts-engines-aeroderivatives-into-larger-role/) (read in full). Sidebar teasers there ("turbine prices up 195%," "sold out into 2029") were not substantiated in the page text.
- GE Vernova combined backlog and slot reservations 116 GW at end Q2 2026 and a heavy-duty turbine ordered now would not arrive until 2031; Bloom fuel cells with Oracle grew from 1.2 GW to a potential 2.8 GW with a system deliverable in about 55 days. ⚠️ market-research and trade snippets, not fetched — [search summary](https://www.power-eng.com/gas/data-center-power-crunch-lifts-engines-aeroderivatives-into-larger-role/); (builds on the earlier note's 100 to 116 GW figure).
- Meta Hyperion (Louisiana): on 27 March 2026 Meta and Entergy announced seven more gas plants (5.2 GW), making 10 plants and 7.5 GW of gas, plus 2.5 GW renewables; Meta pays about $11 billion for the 10-plant deal; the site grew from 2,250 to 3,650 acres; the seven new plants still need Louisiana Public Service Commission approval. Meta would pay the full annual revenue for the plants for 15 years; a commissioner warns of "generational risk" for ratepayers if Meta exits early (snippet). — [The Lens, 30 March 2026](https://thelensnola.org/2026/03/30/meta-triples-order-of-gas-fired-power-plants-for-its-ai-campus-in-louisiana/) (read in full); ⚠️ 15-year term, commissioner quote and "2 GW by 2030, 5 GW about 2032" are search-snippet only — [search summary](https://thelensnola.org/2026/03/30/meta-triples-order-of-gas-fired-power-plants-for-its-ai-campus-in-louisiana/)

Decision tension: build your own gas turbines in months and win the race, or wait for the grid and lose a year, while the neighbours, regulators and lawyers decide how much trust you have burned.

**E. Nuclear: slow, reputation-positive, and slipping**

- Palisades (Michigan, Holtec): the first US restart from decommissioning. Original target late 2025, then early 2026, then "end of February 2026," which passed; as of July, major projects done with more than 5,000 smaller activities left; fuel loading began 30 August 2026 and was paused in September after a fuel assembly tilted (The National says it tipped in August; the two accounts conflict on the month); NRC approved a license amendment 13 October 2026 to move it, retrieved by 22 October; NRC also found a "chilled work environment" in supply chain and quality assurance. CEO says restart "this year," ahead of a contract to supply power by March 2027; Holtec plans two SMR-300 reactors on site by 2031; a federal court dismissed an environmental suit. No restart date as of 22 October. — [ANS Nuclear Newswire, 8 July 2026 and linked later items](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-07-08/article-8187/palisades-restart-projects-holtec-ipo-lawsuit-dismissal-but-no-restart-date/); [The National, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.thenationalnews.com/future/technology/2026/10/22/palisades-power-plant-nuclear-controversy/) (both read in full)
- Crane (Three Mile Island Unit 1), with a 20-year Microsoft power agreement, is targeting 2027. ⚠️ search snippets, not fetched — [Public Power](https://www.publicpower.org/periodical/article/agreement-calls-restart-pennsylvanias-three-mile-island-unit-1)
- Meta nuclear package announced 9 January 2026: up to 6.6 GW by 2035. Vistra 20-year PPAs for 2,176 MW from operating Perry and Davis-Besse (starting late 2026) plus 433 MW of uprates; Oklo up to 1.2 GW in Ohio by 2034; TerraPower up to eight Natrium units (2.8 GW plus storage). No 2026 construction or approval progress found for the new reactors. ⚠️ search snippets — [ANS](https://www.ans.org/news/2026-01-12/article-7666/meta-strikes-deals-with-vistra-oklo-terrapower/)

Decision tension: sign for nuclear to earn trust and a clean story, knowing the "restart" can slip a year and you will need a bridge supply (gas) in the meantime.

### Inferences
- The two-sided curtailment bargain is the most game-ready mechanic: a card that says "connect 18 months sooner, but you are switched off first when the grid is short" has real-world backing (PJM IRAS, ERCOT PCLR, Google's 1 GW), and the FERC rejection gives a good event beat ("the regulator stripped your deal; the states decide").
- Nuclear restart slippage is now documented across three official-target misses, so "50% chance of slipping 1 to 2 turns" in Design 4 of the earlier report is, if anything, mild.
- Gas speed is gated by permission (state permit boards, lawsuits), not only by turbines; xAI shows "operate first, permit later" with a legal fight attached.

## 2. Sites: real campuses against announcements, local opposition, moratoria, tax fights

### Takeaway
One Stargate site is operating (Abilene, partly) out of seven that together plan about 9.3 GW; the gap between announced and online is the clearest legible tension. Opposition is now a measured second constraint: hundreds of groups, a state-level pause in New York, and rival trackers that disagree on the total.

### Cited Findings

**A. Announced versus online**

- Epoch AI (April 2026): seven US Stargate sites are in development, only Abilene operating; planned capacity about 9.3 GW across six sites plus under 0.3 GW at Lordstown. Abilene about 0.3 GW (revised down from an earlier 0.6 GW estimate), planned 1.2 GW by Q4 2026. Shackelford County TX 2.0 GW (on-site gas microgrid, first building late 2026, completion Q4 2028); Doña Ana County NM 2.2 GW (two gas microgrids, "Project Jupiter"); Milam County TX 1.2 GW (SoftBank affiliate, delivery expected by October per filings); Port Washington WI 1.3 GW; Saline Township MI 1.4 GW (DTE supplies 100%); Lordstown OH under 0.3 GW (mainly a server factory, land cleared, no large data center visible). OpenAI planned an Abilene expansion to 2.1 GW but reversed and redirected capacity elsewhere; Microsoft partnered with Crusoe on a separate adjacent 900 MW site. At least three sites plan on-site gas to avoid grid queues. — [Epoch AI, April 2026](https://epoch.ai/publications/openai-stargate-where-the-us-sites-stand) (read in full)
- Abilene by satellite (July 2026 estimate): about 421 MW of IT power and about 509,000 H100-equivalents operating, projected 843 MW by Q4 2026; buildings 1 and 2 operational Sept and Dec 2025, 3 and 4 estimated May 2026, buildings 5 to 8 roofed in July with fit-out ongoing and projected operational around November 2026. ### Inferences
- "Blocked or delayed" is not "cancelled"; a game should treat most opposition as a delay timer with a chance of withdrawal, which fits the source definitions.
- New York's 50 MW executive-order threshold, 20 MW bill threshold and Maine's 20 MW show thresholds are themselves political choices; a single game "size tier" is enough.

## 3. Sovereign and Gulf deals: Stargate UAE, HUMAIN, G42, others; strings and revocations

### Takeaway
The Gulf campus is real but is now a chain of strings: a conditional US license regime that on 10 July 2026 was loosened for named UAE entities but with an expiry clock about April 2027, a pending demand that G42 become a US-owned company, a Congress probe, and (new) a shooting war that has hit hyperscale data centers and forced a redesign. No formal revocation has happened.

### Cited Findings

**A. Stargate UAE and G42**

- Stargate UAE: planned 1 GW cluster inside a 5 GW campus near Abu Dhabi (about $30 billion), built by G42/Khazna with OpenAI, Oracle, Nvidia, Cisco, SoftBank; first 200 MW phase, about 100,000 GB300 chips. Target slipped between sources: Q3 2026 (earlier snippets), "end of 2026," and on 26 October 2026 "4Q 2026." ## 4. Export controls and government leverage over compute: licensing flips, tracking, pooling, priority, reserves

### Takeaway
Policy still flips every few months and is now transactional: allies get named-entity exceptions with expiry dates, China-linked subsidiaries abroad get closed off, and the government's real pooling tools are emergency orders over power and its own labs, not a Defense Production Act takeover of compute. I found no 2026 DPA move on lab compute.
