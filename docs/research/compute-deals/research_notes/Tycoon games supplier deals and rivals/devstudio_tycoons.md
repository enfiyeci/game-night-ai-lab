# Developer-studio tycoon games: supplier deals, licences, capacity and rivals (Game Dev Tycoon, Mad Games Tycoon 2, Software Inc.)

Purpose: material for redesigning "compute deals" in Game Night (deal board, sizes, moving prices, rivals felt through more than a banner; no dice, uncertainty from a visible seeded schedule). Date of research: 2026-10-26.

How to read the flags in this note:
- "(wiki, community)" = Fandom wiki, written by players, not the developer.
- "(Steam announcement)" = developer post pulled through Steam's public news API; the URL given is the API's own link to that post.
- ⚠️ = I read only part of that source, or read it through a summarising tool, or only have a search snippet. The reason is stated on the claim.
- General caveat for every Steam store page and Steam forum thread below: ⚠️ these were fetched with WebFetch, which returns a small model's summary of the page, not the raw page. Treat them as a summary of the page, not a full read.
- Everything quoted as "patch note lines" for Software Inc. and Mad Games Tycoon 2 was found by keyword-filtering many Steam announcements, so ⚠️ those posts were not read end to end unless I say so (see Coverage).

# 1. Game Dev Tycoon (Greenheart Games)

### Takeaway
Game Dev Tycoon has almost no "supplier board". The outside inputs are a fixed schedule of platform launches (each with a one-time licence fee you pay to make games for it), publisher contracts you pick from a re-rollable list, small contract jobs, and a few time-limited office/event offers. Rival companies are almost entirely news text: the console makers appear in an "Industry News" ticker that foretells each platform's launch and death, and players themselves call the competition fake. The most reusable idea is its dated, visible platform timeline (rumour, announcement, release, retirement notice).

### Cited Findings

**What the player buys from outside, and the terms**
- Platforms: each console/handheld has a "license" fee and a separate "development" cost in its wiki infobox. Examples read from the infoboxes: PC license N/A, development 5K; Govodore G64 license None, development 20K; TES license 80K, development 30K; Gameling 50K / 30K; TES 64 200K / 60K; Playsystem 200K / 60K; Playsystem 4 1M / 100K; MBox Next 1.5M / 200K; Swap 1.25M / 80K; OYA (microconsole) 10K / 40K. — [TES](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/TES), [Govodore G64](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Govodore_G64), [PC](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/PC) (wiki, community). Other platform values taken by pulling the infobox fields of each platform page through the wiki's API: [Platforms index](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Platforms) (wiki, community; ⚠️ for pages other than TES/G64/PC I read only the license/development/date fields, not the whole page).
- Licence cost rises as the game goes on: early platforms 50K-200K, late platforms 1M-1.5M (same infobox values as above). — [wiki infoboxes](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Platforms) (wiki, community)
- The licence behaves like a one-time per-platform unlock: a community guide advises choosing "a Platform(s) with a good market share and preferably one(s) you already have a license for". — [Success Guide updated for 1.7.8](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Success_Guild_updated_for_1.7.8) (wiki, community; ⚠️ read by keyword search of an 81,000-character page, not end to end). ⚠️ I found no page that states the payment flow outright (when the fee is charged, whether it is one-time); "one-time" is inferred from that sentence.
- The PC page says the PC has no licensing costs and stays the cheapest to develop for. — [PC](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/PC) (wiki, community)
- Multi-platform games and a "Multi-Platform Optimization" engine feature exist; the engine feature costs 2M and "decreases cost of multi-platform games". — [Custom Game Engine](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Custom_Game_Engine) (wiki, community; ⚠️ only the table rows around that feature were visible to me; page is 25,000 characters)
- Engine licensing: after building 10 custom engines you can research "License Game Engines", then a "Software Development Kit" engine part (100 RP research, 100K to add) that lets you sell the engine's licence for monthly income. — [R&D Lab](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/R%26D_Lab), [Custom Game Engine](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Custom_Game_Engine) (wiki, community)
- Publisher contracts: the game offers publishing deals for medium/large games; the guide says to take publisher deals until about 100K fans for medium games, then self-publish with marketing; deals can be re-rolled by backing out of the screen once, or by save/load. — [Success Guide](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Success_Guild_updated_for_1.7.8) (wiki, community; ⚠️ keyword read)
- The developer itself warns about publisher terms: "Be very careful what publishing contracts you take. Publishers often suggest not-so-ideal combinations which can lead to bad scores and you having to pay a penalty." — [Greenheart Steam announcement, Jan 2024](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/5510783579247039069) (read in full; the earlier-listed 44 Steam news items were only title-scanned)
- Contract jobs: unlocked after the third game via a character named Jason; small/medium/large; 24-week expiry; payment = 1000 x a base value that scales with the in-game year and a random range; penalty and deadline are also random ranges (formulas published). — [Contracts](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Contracts) (wiki, community; read in full). Note the wiki's formula uses random numbers; that is the opposite of the owner's "no dice" rule and is useful only as a contrast.
- Trade-show booths: yearly pop-up (prompt at the start of month 5 week 1) offering small 80K / medium 500K / large 1.5M booths; the right size depends on fan count (under 40,000 / 40,000-100,000 / over 100,000); closing the pop-up has no penalty beyond losing hype. — [Convention](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Convention) (wiki, community; read in full). This is a "size choice" example where the correct size is readable from a visible number.
- Marketing campaigns: guide mentions 500K and 50K campaigns and says to time them to development stage. — [Success Guild updated for 1.7.8](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Success_Guild_updated_for_1.7.8) (wiki, community; ⚠️ keyword read)
- Time-limited offers with a price that rises when you wait: the air-conditioner event offers 90K; if declined, a later offer costs 180K; if declined again the staff buy it themselves. — [Random Events](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Random_Events) (wiki, community; read in full)
- Rival-flavoured paid offers: "Industrial Life Topic" (5K-65K, gives a new topic, +1 evil) and "Industrial Sabotage" (50K-170K, "access to critical systems of one of your competitors"); a patent-troll "Legal Department" letter offers a licence at about 55x the original cost with a 50% discount for paying, or you can fight in court. — [Random Events](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Random_Events) (wiki, community). The wiki marks these "random", with conditions and ranges listed.
- Hardware: late-game Hardware Lab lets you build your own console (up to $100M+ to develop, support $30K-$3M a month, one console replaces the previous). — [Custom Console](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Custom_Console), [Hardware Lab](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Hardware_Lab) (wiki, community)
- Office capacity: moving to bigger offices is unlocked by events/conditions with price tags (150K technology upgrade, 8M technology park move, room for 6 employees). The 8M move offer "repeats until accepted". — [Random Events](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Random_Events) (wiki, community)

**Do prices or availability move over time, and how is that shown?**
- Platform availability follows a fixed calendar that scales with chosen game length: each platform page lists release and discontinue dates for 30-, 35- and 42-year games (for TES: release Y2 M1 W2 / Y2 M3 W2 / Y2 M6 W2; discontinued Y6 M6 W2 / Y7 M8 W2 / Y8 M8 W2). The G64 is the first to leave the market. — [TES](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/TES), [Govodore G64](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Govodore_G64) (wiki, community)
- The schedule is announced in the game as dated news items, in a sequence: rumour, confirmation with a release window, post-release "massive success" item, and a discontinuation notice with a date. For the TES: "According to rumours ... planning to launch its very own home gaming console" -> "has confirmed ... plans to release ... early next year" -> "has proven to be a massive success" -> "will discontinue the TES on June 14". For the G64: market-share-decline news at Y1 M7 W3 and Y3 M11 W2, a bankruptcy item at Y4 M12 W3, "In two months, the G64 will be withdrawn from the market!" at Y4 M11 W2, and "G64 is no longer supported" at Y5 M1 W2 (35-year game). — [TES](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/TES), [Govodore G64](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Govodore_G64) (wiki, community). Cited dates are those the wiki lists for the 35-year game.
- Platform market share is a lookup table of dated points that the sales formula interpolates between by week ("plot the previous and the next market points, draw a line"), so market size moves along a fixed, non-random curve. — [Sales Algorithm](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Sales_Algorithm) (wiki, community; ⚠️ I read the first 6,000 of about 8,600 characters; the raw table lives on a separate "Raw Data for Sales Algorithm" page I did not open)
- Licence fees rise with each platform generation (see infobox values above); I found no sign that a single platform's fee changes with demand. ⚠️ Absence is based on the pages I read, not a source stating it.

**How rival companies compete; how it is shown**
- The only named companies are the console makers (Govodore, Ninvento, Vena, Vonny, Mirconoft, Grapple, KickIT) and four publisher names (Active Visionaries, Electric Mass Productions, TQC, CapeCom); that is the entire "Companies" page. — [Companies](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Companies) (wiki, community; page tagged as a stub)
- Console makers compete with each other only in news text and market-share tables (e.g. Vonny's handheld "engineered by Vonny to reduce the hype made by Ninvento's GS"). — [Platforms](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Platforms) (wiki, community)
- Rivals cannot buy a deal out from under you, undercut you, or take a contract you wanted; the only company-level rival event is on your own bankruptcy, where a news item says "Electronic Mass Productions/Stygnya ... has purchased the remains of the company". — [Bankruptcy](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Bankruptcy) (wiki, community)
- Magazines rate your game against your own past best score, not against rivals: a long community guide says "You're not competing against the ever-growing video game industry, just with your own past best scores". — [Success Guild updated for 1.7.8](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Success_Guild_updated_for_1.7.8) (wiki, community; ⚠️ keyword read, but this line is the first paragraph)

**What players said**
- A Steam thread asking for rival companies: "feels kind of lonely"; a reply calls the current rivals "fake competition" and wants the ability to buy/shut down publishers or force them to make games only for your consoles; another asks for random market events so one console can outsell another instead of fixed timeline events. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/3048356136489099772) (⚠️ read through WebFetch summary, not the raw page)
- A community guide complains the game "has no profit/costs breakdown screen" and game history omits "marketing, Platform licences, building the new engine, and paying all your employees". — [Success Guild updated for 1.7.8](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Success_Guild_updated_for_1.7.8) (wiki, community; ⚠️ keyword read). This is a confusion point: the licence fee is paid but never itemised afterwards.
- Publishing offers can be "traps" (bad topic/genre/platform combinations the game asks you to accept); guide advises to inspect compatibility before taking them. — same source and the Greenheart post above.
- Steam review aggregate: Very Positive, 94% of 22,242 English reviews (Metacritic 68). — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/239820/Game_Dev_Tycoon/) (⚠️ WebFetch summary; I did not read review text, only the aggregate, so I have no review-based quotes about supplier/rival systems)

- Platform infobox fields exist in three versions (30/35/42-year) which suggests the schedule was rebalanced when game length options were added; I did not find the patch that did this.

### Inferences
- The strongest reusable idea is the announced timeline: a seeded schedule can surface as rumour -> confirmation with a date -> release -> retirement notice, so players plan around it with no dice. The game does this for platforms; the equivalent for Game Night would be chip generations, fab capacity coming online, and neocloud shutdown notices.
- Tiered fees that rise by generation (50K to 1.5M) let one mechanic (a one-time licence) stay interesting without a price-moving system; the cost is that the choice is mostly "can I afford it yet?".
- Re-roll by save/load of the publisher list shows the danger of a hidden, rerollable board: players game it. A visible seeded board removes that exploit.
- The wiki's price-doubling air-conditioner offer is a small, readable pattern for "decline now, pay more later" without randomness.
- Rivals as news-only is exactly what the owner dislikes about Game Night's banner; players here said it makes the world "lonely".

### Gaps
- Exact in-game name and layout of the platform-licence and publisher-contract screens; official images of them (none among the 7 Steam screenshots).
- Whether the platform licence is charged once per platform or per game, and exactly when.
- Developer postmortem/GDC talk on these systems: I ran only one general search for Greenheart commentary and found nothing relevant.
- Steam review text (only aggregates seen).
- Anything after the 2024-03 Steam news items (latest item I saw was "available on Netflix"; ⚠️ title only).

# 2. Mad Games Tycoon 2 (Eggcode)

### Takeaway
Mad Games Tycoon 2 is the closest of the three to "rivals you can feel": up to about 100 rival companies appear over the years, chart against you, license your engine, make exclusives for consoles, and can be bought. Outside inputs are dev kits and platform slots, IP licences, publisher exclusive contracts, engine licences, server rooms and staff searches. Players call contract work a poor trade, buying rivals marginal early and useful late, and the sharpest rival feel comes from charts and the publisher/developer statistics menu.

### Cited Findings

**What the player buys from outside; terms**
- The Steam page lists: license popular IPs (books, movies, toys, sports); build or license competitors' engines "earning a cut of games made with them"; work for publishers, independently, via contracts, or sign exclusive publishing deals; consoles with warranties and "developer incentives"; marketing campaigns; "Up to 100 rival companies. You can buy them, direct their output, or shut them down." — [Steam store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1342330/Mad_Games_Tycoon_2/) (⚠️ WebFetch summary)
- Dev kits: you must "buy the dev kit" before making games for a platform (shown in the thread about arcade cabinets: buy the Arcade Board/dev kit, research the workshop, then create the game and select the kit). The menu name is "Buy DevKits / Select Platforms for Game Development" (patch note, which also added filtering by platform type). — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/3171072251350400667) (⚠️ WebFetch summary); [Development Update, 2023-04-20](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/5133583099794633322) (⚠️ keyword-filtered lines of a 23,000-character post). ⚠️ I found no source giving dev-kit prices.
- Platform slots (capacity): "You can also unlock additional platform slots with contract games" (2021-03-08 patch notes); sandbox mode unlocks all slots and licences; "From start year 1995, platform slots are unlocked for game development" (2022-11-29); "You can now fill empty platform slots during development" (2022-09-27). — Steam announcements: [Patchnotes 2021.02.21A-2021.03.08A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4057152305727721555), [Sandbox mode 2.0](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4836332027673671222), [Update - BUILD 2022.09.16A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4589754330749384797) (⚠️ keyword-filtered lines). This is a rare example of capacity being earned by doing outside work.
- IP licences: they get "much more expensive" (Summer 2021), "become more expensive over time" and "have one suitable and one unsuitable genre" (2022-09), and licences are "now included in the game rating" (2023-04). — [Summer Update!](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4020014731791976970), [Update 2022.09.16A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4589754330749384797), [Development Update 2023-04-20](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/5133583099794633322) (⚠️ keyword-filtered lines)
- Exclusive publisher contracts: cancelling them was added in January 2022; cancellation penalties were then "increased and ... linked to the difficulty level" (2022-03-25); with an exclusive game "a portion of the development costs will now be covered" (2022-02-05). — [Company Acquisitions!](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4348798430274738151) (read in full; it carries the patch notes for these builds)
- Publisher terms players discuss: a five-star publisher pays less up front and less per copy but gets a larger share and boosts hype to roughly 80-100; "exclusive contract as soon as possible" is one player's advice. — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/3833172952657307613) (⚠️ WebFetch summary; anecdotal, one player's claims)
- Consoles you make: you can "set funding so that more NPCs develop exclusive games for your console" (2023-05-24), i.e. you pay to attract rivals' output; earlier "Player consoles are licensed much more often and NPCs pay additional attention to market strength" (2022-01-25). — [Official Release Date post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/5151600576584832658) (⚠️ keyword-filtered); [Company Acquisitions!](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4348798430274738151) (full)
- Engines you license out: you can see the number of licences sold and the revenue; "NPCs now pay more attention to the licensing price and profit sharing of player engines" (2021-12-22); the profit share is saved at the time of licensing so it cannot be changed later (2023-05-24). — [Patchnotes 2021.01.27](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4051520269926126446), [Merry Christmas! post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4224938027296499338), [Official Release Date](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/5151600576584832658) (⚠️ keyword-filtered lines)
- Server rooms (compute-like capacity): MMO and free-to-play games need server space; "When there is no more server space, MMO sales are reduced" (2021-09); you can "reserve server rooms for MMOs, F2Ps or Game Pass" (2022-11); "Server costs are now displayed separately" in the financial overview (2023-05). — [Summer Update!](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4020014731791976970), [Sandbox mode 2.0](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4836332027673671222), [Official Release Date](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/5151600576584832658) (⚠️ keyword-filtered lines)
- Pre-orders as a delay penalty: "Pre-orders of standard games are now cancelled if buyers have to wait too long" and "Pre-orders are cancelled due to a price increase" (2022-09). — [Update 2022.09.16A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4589754330749384797) (⚠️ keyword-filtered). Close analogue to a chip order that arrives months late.

**Do prices/availability move?**
- Licences rise in price over time (above). A global event "Loss of trust (no new licenses)" blocks new licences for a while (2021-03-08). — [Patchnotes 2021.02.21A-2021.03.08A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4057152305727721555) (⚠️ keyword-filtered)
- Platform availability follows historical dates and an option "Random Platform Popularity" (platform lifetime tied to sales; added 2021). Start year changes which companies and platforms exist. — [Steam announcements 2021-01-27, 2021-11-05](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4186652991113913673) (⚠️ keyword-filtered). Note: this random option is optional; default follows history.
- NPC company availability: "NPC companies are now locked for a certain amount of time until you can buy them" (2022-03-25). — [Company Acquisitions!](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4348798430274738151) (read in full)
- New rivals enter over time: "The earlier start date you pick, the more you will see new companies come into the market ... You may start with 10 or 20 competitors but by the end be competing against all 90+!" — [First Year Anniversary post, 2022-01-21](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4237325463675656030) (⚠️ keyword-filtered lines)

**How rivals compete and how it is shown**
- Charts: "mini weekly top 10 chart on the main screen" for PC & consoles, mobile, arcades and F2P downloads, toggled by a button next to your contract reputation (2022-01). Subsidiary games are colour-coded in the charts (2022-09). NPCs "compete for the results of Year End awards on the harder difficulty levels". — [First Year Anniversary post](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4237325463675656030), [Update 2022.09.16A](https://steamstore-a.akamaihd.net/news/externalpost/steam_community_announcements/4589754330749384797) (⚠️ keyword-filtered)
- Statistics menu: "Publishers & Developers" lists NPC companies, sortable by games developed/distributed (2022-01), with a tooltip that shows how many games each has published and developed (2021-12) and a "best list" by awards (2023-04); you can hide companies not for sale. ### Inferences
- Exposing rivals as a ranked, filterable list with a price tag (the Publishers & Developers menu plus a locked-until-unlocked timer) gives the player something to plan against; Game Night's rivals (DeepThink, OpenBrain) could appear as a table with model size, compute received and a "for sale / locked until month X" state.
- Declined offers going to NPC publishers is a clean, seeded-friendly way to make rivals take what you pass on, with no dice if the receiving rival is fixed by schedule.
- Capacity earned through a side job (platform slots from contracts) and capacity that stalls output when missing (no server space reduces MMO sales) are both direct analogues of serving/training capacity.
- Pre-orders cancelled when buyers wait too long is a model for a late chip order: a delay has a visible, rule-based cost.
- The rival-buying feature took several patches to feel useful and players judge it marginal early; the lesson is that a rival-ownership option needs a clear payoff curve.

# 3. Software Inc. (Coredumping)

### Takeaway
Software Inc. has the richest "outside supplier" model of the three and the best-instrumented rivals. You rent or buy land and rooms, lease software licences per need and per employee, take contract/support/marketing/hosting deals, outsource servers, take publisher deals, and trade stocks. AI companies specialise, enter under-served markets after a notice period, release products you can inspect, can be bought out (by you, by shares, and rarely by each other), and their research and products are visible on several windows. The studio's long-running 2026 "overhaul" adds buyable server hardware, AI-company CEOs and reworked pre-marketing, but it was still unreleased when last announced (2026-10-04).

### Inferences
- Software Inc.'s licence history is a direct lesson for pricing: charging monthly during development and per employee per project (instead of one up-front fee) tied cost to use, and the developer fixed the price after an exploit. For Game Night, a rental priced per compute-unit-month is easier to read than a lump sum.
- The 6-month grace before a new rival appears, the "what competitors are researching" window, and the release calendar give three examples of rival moves announced ahead of time, which suits a seeded schedule the player can read.
- Software Inc.'s hosting-deal and publisher-deal changes show the same pattern the owner wants: deals carry strings (quality targets, recoup targets, relationship that improves later deals, cancellation if you underperform), not just a price.
- Rivals' share of the same shared resource (patents, frameworks, stock) is what makes them feel present; supplier competition (one bidder taking a deal) is absent in all three games, so it would be new design for Game Night.
- Buying a rival's stock gradually (share by share, then majority) is a smooth alternative to a single buy button; the game also needed later rules (AI cannot dilute once someone has more than 50%).

# Cross-game inferences for Game Night's compute deals
(Inferences only; each rests on findings above.)
1. A visible timeline of "announce -> confirm with date -> arrive -> retire" (Game Dev Tycoon platforms) is the cleanest no-dice way to move availability and price.
2. Deals with strings and a relationship score (Software Inc. publishers: recoup target, better deals after success, cancelled if you fail) can make the cheapest card not always the best card.
3. Capacity should be earned or lost visibly: slots unlocked by contract work and server space that caps revenue (Mad Games Tycoon 2), server groups wired to processes (Software Inc.).
4. Rivals felt through the player's own screens: ranked lists with tooltips and charts (Mad Games Tycoon 2), calendars of rival releases and a "what they are researching" view (Software Inc.), and consequences for declined offers (MGT2: NPC publishers take games you decline).
5. Delay has a rule-based cost (MGT2 pre-orders cancelled if buyers wait too long); this maps to a late chip order.
6. None of the three has a rival that wins a specific supplier deal before you do; that mechanic would need to come from a design seed, not from these games.
