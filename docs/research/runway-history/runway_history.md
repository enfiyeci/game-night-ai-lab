# OpenAI and Anthropic: Cash, Burn, and Runway History (2015 – September 2026)

Compiled 2026-09-24. This is a fast-moving, heavily-leaked topic: most burn/loss/cash figures below come
from press reporting on private company data (The Information, Bloomberg, CNBC, leaked financials), not
audited public filings, until an IPO happens. Every figure is dated and sourced. Where I calculate
"months of runway" myself, it is explicitly labeled **MY ESTIMATE** with the inputs shown — these are

## PART 1 — OpenAI

### 1.1 Nonprofit era and the 2018 crisis (2015–2019)

**Takeaway:** OpenAI was pledged $1B at founding in 2015 but actually received only ~$130M by 2019.
Elon Musk's 2018 departure (and reneged future funding) combined with soaring compute costs created a
real cash crisis that forced the 2019 pivot from nonprofit to "capped-profit," which is what unlocked
Microsoft's first $1B.

- OpenAI was founded in December 2015 as a nonprofit. A total of $1 billion was pledged by Musk, Altman, Greg Brockman, Reid Hoffman, Jessica Livingston, Peter Thiel, AWS, and Infosys — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI); [datastudios.org funding history](https://www.datastudios.org/post/how-openai-was-funded-from-nonprofit-donations-to-billions-in-investments-and-global-partnerships).
- Actual capital collected badly lagged the pledge: only about $130–133 million had actually been received by 2019/2021 — [datastudios.org](https://www.datastudios.org/post/how-openai-was-funded-from-nonprofit-donations-to-billions-in-investments-and-global-partnerships).
- Musk's departure was announced February 20, 2018, after a dispute in which he proposed taking personal control of OpenAI (folding it into Tesla or taking a majority stake); the other founders refused. Musk then walked away and reneged on a large planned future donation — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2019-02-17/elon-musk-left-openai-on-disagreements-about-company-pathway); [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/business/how-openai-lost-musk-and-took-aim-at-something-magical/).
- This left Sam Altman "in dire need of cash and support," with existing backer Reid Hoffman stepping in with additional funds while combined AI-talent and compute costs kept rising — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/business/how-openai-lost-musk-and-took-aim-at-something-magical/). Altman said in 2019: "the amount of money we needed to be successful in the mission is much more gigantic than I originally thought" — [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/business/how-openai-lost-musk-and-took-aim-at-something-magical/).
- On March 11, 2019, OpenAI announced creation of a for-profit ("capped-profit") subsidiary specifically so it could raise money for compute; the cap restricted early investors to no more than 100x returns, with lower caps for later investors like Microsoft — [TechCrunch 2019 report cited via search summary](https://techcrunch.com/2019/07/22/microsoft-invests-1-billion-in-openai-in-new-multiyear-partnership/); [Big Think](https://bigthink.com/business/how-openai-lost-musk-and-took-aim-at-something-magical/).
- On July 22, 2019, Microsoft announced a $1 billion investment in OpenAI (roughly half delivered as Azure compute credits rather than cash) as part of a multi-year partnership — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2019/07/22/microsoft-invests-1-billion-in-openai-in-new-multiyear-partnership/).
- Musk v. Altman litigation (filed 2024, trial testimony through April–May 2026) surfaced internal 2015–2018 emails; Musk testified OpenAI leaders were "looting the nonprofit," and Microsoft's own 2018 internal doubts about OpenAI's path to AGI and fears of losing the startup to Amazon were entered as evidence — [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2026/04/29/nx-s1-5803833/elon-musk-testimony-openai-altman-day-2); [Winbuzzer](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/05/10/musk-v-altman-docs-microsoft-executives-had-reserv-xcxwbn/). ⚠️ I did not find a specific dollar figure for OpenAI's cash-on-hand at the low point of the 2018 crisis in any source — this is a gap, not an inference.

**Runway estimate (MY ESTIMATE):** Not calculable — no source states OpenAI's actual cash balance in 2018. The qualitative record (reneged pledge, "dire need of cash," restructuring specifically to raise money) supports treating 2018 as OpenAI's first near-zero-runway moment, but I cannot quantify months of runway from what's available. Flagged as a gap below.

### 1.2 2021–2022: scaling up, pre-ChatGPT burn

- Microsoft made a second investment round in OpenAI around 2021, reported at roughly $2 billion, following the initial 2019 $1 billion — [search-result summary, not independently confirmed by a single primary article](https://techcrunch.com/2023/01/23/microsoft-invests-billions-more-dollars-in-openai-extends-partnership/). ⚠️ I could not find one clean primary source stating the exact 2021 dollar figure; treat as an approximate, aggregator-derived number, not confirmed.
- OpenAI's 2022 expenses totaled $544.5 million: $416.45M on computing/data, $89.31M on staff, $38.75M other operating expenses — [search-result summary citing OpenAI's own disclosed 2022 figures](https://futurism.com/the-byte/openai-losing-money-chatgpt).
- The pending Microsoft mega-deal (ultimately ~$10B) was being negotiated through late 2022 with a targeted year-end 2022 close, but did not actually close until January 2023 — meaning OpenAI absorbed ChatGPT's November 2022 launch and its cost surge before the new capital arrived — [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/01/09/2023/microsoft-eyes-10-billion-bet-on-chatgpt).

### 1.3 January 2023: the ~$10B Microsoft deal

- Announced January 23, 2023. Microsoft invested (a reported) $10 billion as part of a broader round that valued OpenAI at $29 billion — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/01/23/microsoft-announces-multibillion-dollar-investment-in-chatgpt-maker-openai.html); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-01-23/microsoft-makes-multibillion-dollar-investment-in-openai).
- Deal structure: Microsoft entitled to 75% of OpenAI's profits until it recoups its investment, after which it holds a 49% stake — [search-result summary](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/01/23/microsoft-announces-multibillion-dollar-investment-in-chatgpt-maker-openai.html). This was the third phase of the Microsoft partnership (following 2019 and 2021) — same source.
- By February 2024, a secondary tender (led by Thrive Capital) reportedly valued OpenAI at approximately $80 billion — [Malay Mail, Feb 2024 citing media reports](https://www.malaymail.com/news/money/2024/02/18/media-openai-in-deal-valuing-it-at-us80b/118618). ⚠️ Seen only as a search-summarized snippet, not read in full; treat the $80B figure as reported-not-verified.
- OpenAI closed a private round including ARK Investment Management around April 2024 as part of what The Information described as "seven funding rounds totaling over $11 billion" to date — [The Information, via WebFetch summary, July 24 2024 report](https://www.theinformation.com/briefings/anthropic-projected-to-burn-more-than-2-7-billion-in-cash-this-year) (this OpenAI figure appeared as comparison context inside the same Information analysis I fetched for the cash-runway story below).

### 1.4 September–October 2024: OpenAI's most-reported near-cash-crunch moment

**Takeaway:** This is the best-documented "shortest runway" moment for OpenAI. The Information reported in July 2024 that OpenAI risked running out of cash within 12 months absent a new raise; by September 2024 OpenAI was projecting a ~$5B loss on $3.7B revenue for the year; the $6.6B round that resolved this closed October 2, 2024, with an unusual clawback clause.

- **The Information (July 24, 2024)**, based on "previously undisclosed financial data": OpenAI could lose as much as $5 billion in 2024, spending ~$7B on AI training and ~$1.5B on staffing (total burn ~$8.5B/year), and faces "risk of running out of cash within 12 months," needing to "close on yet another financing round within 12 months." Same report noted Anthropic's 2024 burn was projected far lower, at $2.7B — [Cointelegraph summary of The Information](https://cointelegraph.com/news/openai-could-lose-5b-this-year-and-run-out-of-cash-in-12-months-report) (I fetched and read this summary page in full).
- **CNBC / NYT, September 27, 2024**: OpenAI sees roughly $5 billion in losses for 2024 on $3.7 billion of revenue; ChatGPT alone expected to generate $2.7B of that revenue, ~$1B from other lines; monthly revenue hit $300M in August 2024, up 1,700% since the start of 2023 — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/09/27/openai-sees-5-billion-loss-this-year-on-3point7-billion-in-revenue.html).
- **October 2, 2024**: OpenAI closed a $6.6 billion round led by Thrive Capital (with Microsoft, Nvidia, SoftBank, Fidelity, Khosla Ventures, Altimeter, MGX, Tiger Global) at a $157 billion post-money valuation, plus a separate $4 billion revolving credit line — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/10/02/openai-raises-6-6b-and-is-now-valued-at-157b/); [SiliconANGLE](https://siliconangle.com/2024/10/02/openai-closes-record-6-6b-funding-round-157b-valuation/).
- **Clawback condition**: per Bloomberg, investors in this round could claw back their cash (i.e., the funding converts to debt) if OpenAI did not complete its conversion from nonprofit to for-profit within two years of the close — [NBC News](https://www.nbcnews.com/business/business-news/openai-funding-cut-10-billion-profit-conversion-doesnt-occur-year-end-rcna198915); reported also that the round itself could be cut by $10 billion if conversion didn't happen by year-end 2024, an earlier informal deadline that slipped — same source.

**Runway estimate at the July–September 2024 low point (MY ESTIMATE):**
- Inputs: reported annualized burn ~$8.5B/year (≈$708M/month) per The Information; explicit press framing that OpenAI risked running out of cash "within 12 months" absent new funding.
- No source states OpenAI's actual cash-on-hand figure at that moment. Working backward from the "within 12 months" framing at an $8.5B/year burn rate implies OpenAI's cash-on-hand in mid-2024 was on the rough order of $6–9B (i.e., roughly 8–12 months of burn) — **this is my own back-solved estimate, not a reported figure**, and should be treated as a rough game-balancing number, not a factual claim about OpenAI's actual balance sheet.

### 1.5 2025: SoftBank round, restructuring, and rising losses

- **March 31, 2025**: OpenAI finalized a $40 billion round led by SoftBank ($30B of it, with $10B from a syndicate including Microsoft, Coatue, Altimeter, Thrive) at a $300 billion post-money valuation — nearly double the October 2024 $157B mark. Structured in tranches: an initial ~$10B ($7.5B SoftBank + $2.5B syndicate) with a second ~$30B tranche by end of 2025 — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-03-31/openai-finalizes-40-billion-funding-at-300-billion-valuation); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/03/31/openai-raises-40b-at-300b-post-money-valuation/).
- **October 28, 2025**: OpenAI completed its restructuring into a nonprofit "OpenAI Foundation" (holding ~26%, ~$130B) overseeing a for-profit "OpenAI Group PBC" (Microsoft holding ~27%, ~$135B), removing the old 100x investor-return cap and the fundraising constraints tied to nonprofit status — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/28/open-ai-for-profit-microsoft.html); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-10-29/openai-s-public-benefit-corporation-plan-pbc-explained). This satisfied the two-year clawback deadline attached to the October 2024 round (with roughly a year to spare).
- **2025 full-year financials (leaked, reported June 2026)**: revenue $13.07B (up from $3.7B in 2024); net loss $38.5B (up from $5.09B in 2024, roughly 7.5x); total costs/expenses $34B (up from $12.48B in 2024) — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/16/openai-financials-leaked-losses-revenue-profit/); [Yahoo Finance / wheresyoured.at reprint](https://www.wheresyoured.at/exclusive-openai-financials/). ⚠️ These are leaked, unaudited figures reported by press, not an OpenAI financial statement I read directly.
- Anonymous-sourced caveat on the same leak: after excluding non-cash items (stock-based compensation etc.), OpenAI's actual out-of-pocket cash burn for 2025 was closer to $8 billion — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/17/openai-balance-sheet-mysterious-business/).
- **Cash on hand at year-end 2025**: OpenAI held just over $50 billion in assets, roughly half in cash (~$25B cash) — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/06/17/openai-balance-sheet-mysterious-business/) / [Yahoo Finance leak coverage](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/stocks/articles/openai-2025-financials-leaked-38-121508294.html).

### 1.6 2026: record valuation, and analysts warning of a later cash-out date

- **March 31, 2026**: OpenAI closed a funding round with $122 billion in committed capital (up from an initially announced $110B) at an $852 billion post-money valuation, anchored by Amazon ($50B), Nvidia ($30B), SoftBank ($30B), plus a16z, D.E. Shaw Ventures, and an undisclosed Microsoft contribution — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-03-31/openai-valued-at-852-billion-after-completing-122-billion-round). ⚠️ CNBC's corroborating figure came back blocked (403) on direct fetch during this research pass; used only via WebSearch snippet, cross-checked against Bloomberg's headline number.
- Reported 2026–2027 burn trajectory: cash burn projected at approximately $27 billion in 2026 and approximately $63 billion in 2027, up roughly 200% year-over-year; OpenAI CFO Sarah Friar reportedly told investors the company will not be cash-flow positive before 2028 at the earliest — [runtimewire.com summary](https://runtimewire.com/article/openai-278b-cash-burn-1-2t-valuation).
- Longer-horizon forecast: OpenAI expects roughly negative $278 billion in free cash flow from 2026 through 2030 as infrastructure spend (forecast ~$856B through 2030) outpaces even aggressive revenue targets (revenue projected to rise from ~$36B in 2026 to ~$350B in 2030) — same source.
- Separately, Deutsche Bank is cited estimating ~$143 billion in negative cumulative free cash flow for OpenAI between 2024 and 2029 before profitability, and other analysts cited losses surging to ~$74 billion by 2028 (about three-quarters of that year's projected revenue) — [eMarketer](https://www.emarketer.com/content/openai-forecast-143-billion-loss-raises-stakes-ai-monetization); [Yahoo Finance](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/financial-experts-warn-openai-may-113057515.html).
- **"Could run out of cash by mid-2027"**: multiple outlets (Tom's Hardware, citing an NYT-linked analyst read of OpenAI's finances; Windows Central) reported analysts, including Sebastian Mallaby (Council on Foreign Relations), warning OpenAI could exhaust cash by mid-2027 absent further capital, with one framing being "OpenAI might torch $14 billion in 2026" — [Tom's Hardware](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/big-tech/openai-could-reportedly-run-out-of-cash-by-mid-2027-nyt-analyst-paints-grim-picture-after-examining-companys-finances); [Windows Central](https://www.windowscentral.com/artificial-intelligence/openai-chatgpt/openai-might-torch-14-billion-in-2026). ⚠️ These are analyst projections/opinions, not company disclosures — labeled speculative in the sources themselves ("could," "may," "my bet is").
- As of this research (September 2026), OpenAI was reportedly in early talks for another private round at a valuation of at least $1.2 trillion, alongside a stated ambition for a ~$1 trillion IPO valuation — [runtimewire.com](https://runtimewire.com/article/openai-278b-cash-burn-1-2t-valuation).

**Runway estimate at year-end 2025 / into 2026 (MY ESTIMATE):**
- Inputs: ~$25B cash on hand at year-end 2025; ~$8B "true" cash burn reported for full-year 2025 (≈$667M/month); but 2026 burn is separately projected at ~$27B for the full year (≈$2.25B/month) as infrastructure spending ramps.
- Using the trailing 2025 burn rate: $25B ÷ $0.667B/month ≈ **37 months of runway** (MY ESTIMATE — naive, holds burn constant at the 2025 rate).
- Using the forward-looking 2026 burn projection instead: $25B ÷ $2.25B/month ≈ **11 months of runway** (MY ESTIMATE — much tighter, and arguably the more honest number given burn was reported rising sharply).
- The gap between these two estimates (37 months vs. 11 months) illustrates why "months of runway" is highly sensitive to whether you use trailing or forward-looking burn for a company whose spending is accelerating — worth building into the game's mechanic (see Transfer notes).

## PART 2 — Anthropic

### 2.1 Founding and early funding (2021–2022)

- Anthropic was founded in January 2021 by seven former OpenAI employees, including siblings Daniela and Dario Amodei — [Wikipedia-derived search summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropic).
- **May 2021**: $124 million Series A, funded almost entirely by tech founders/philanthropists rather than traditional VCs — lead investors included Jaan Tallinn, Dustin Moskovitz, and Eric Schmidt — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/news/145208-anthropics-124m-series-a-came-almost-entirely-from-tech-founders-not-vcs/).
- **April 2022**: $580 million Series B. Sam Bankman-Fried, via Alameda Research, was the lead investor, writing a $500 million check — 86% of the round — [Anthropic's own announcement](https://www.anthropic.com/news/anthropic-raises-series-b-to-build-safe-reliable-ai); [KuCoin summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/sbf-s-500m-anthropic-bet-from-ftx-collapse-to-380b-valuation). This $500M bought roughly an 8% equity stake — same source.

### 2.2 FTX collapse (November 2022) and what happened to the stake

- FTX collapsed into bankruptcy in November 2022. Its Anthropic shares (held via Alameda) became an asset of the bankruptcy estate rather than being "clawed back" from Anthropic itself — i.e., the stake was a thing FTX creditors could recover value from, not a liability against Anthropic — [Yahoo Finance / Forbes summary](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/ftx-owned-anthropic-solana-spacex-175433104.html); [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/josipamajic/2026/03/18/ftx-owned-anthropic-solana-and-spacex-then-had-to-sell-too-soon/).
- **March 25, 2024**: The FTX estate sold roughly two-thirds of its Anthropic stake for $884 million, with the largest buyer being ATIC Third International Investment Company (tied to Abu Dhabi's Mubadala sovereign fund), which alone bought $500 million of shares — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/03/25/ftx-estate-sells-majority-stake-in-startup-anthropic-for-884-million.html).
- FTX sold its remaining Anthropic shares (~$452 million) in mid-2024, bringing the estate's total realized value from the original $500M investment to about $1.3 billion — [KuCoin summary](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/sbf-s-500m-anthropic-bet-from-ftx-collapse-to-380b-valuation). Proceeds went to repaying FTX creditors, not back to Anthropic.
- At Anthropic's mid-2026 valuation (~$380–965B depending on the exact date), the same 8% stake, had it been retained, would have been worth on the order of $30 billion — same source. This is presented in coverage as one of the largest unrealized gains in VC history, illustrating that FTX's bankruptcy — not any weakness at Anthropic — is what ended this investor relationship.
- ⚠️ I found no source stating that Anthropic's own operations or cash position were destabilized by the FTX collapse; the disruption was entirely on the FTX/investor side. This is treated as "not found" for any Anthropic-side crisis, rather than inferred as "no impact."

### 2.5 Did Anthropic ever have a real near-bankruptcy moment?

**Takeaway:** I found no reported instance of Anthropic itself being described as close to running out of cash. The only "bankruptcy" language found is Dario Amodei's own hypothetical, industry-wide warning (that a single bad year of AI capex/demand forecasting could bankrupt any frontier lab, Anthropic included, in a future scenario) — this is forward-looking commentary about the industry's structural risk, not a report of Anthropic being in distress in September 2026.

- Dario Amodei has stated that without eventual industry revenue in the $800B–$1T range, even hedging could not prevent bankruptcy risk for AI labs generally, and that "you go bankrupt" if AI growth forecasts miss by even a year — [Winbuzzer, Feb 2026](https://winbuzzer.com/2026/02/14/anthropic-ceo-warns-ai-firms-risk-bankruptcy-over-timing-xcxwbn/); [Memeburn](https://memeburn.com/anthropic-ceo-bankruptcy-warning-ai-spending/).
- These warnings coincide with, rather than contradict, a period of Anthropic actually reporting rapid revenue growth ($65B annualized by mid-2026), an $965B Series H, above-80%-reported gross margins, and early profitable quarters — [search-result summary](https://memeburn.com/anthropic-ceo-bankruptcy-warning-ai-spending/).
- **Gap**: no source found describing a specific date or event where Anthropic was reported to be weeks/months from exhausting cash, unlike OpenAI's well-documented July–October 2024 episode. This is stated as "not found," not inferred as "never happened."

## PART 3 — Other labs: "game over" analogues (2024)

### 3.1 Inflection AI → Microsoft (March 21, 2024) — license-and-hire

- Microsoft hired Inflection's co-founders Mustafa Suleyman and Karén Simonyan plus most of its ~70-person staff, and separately paid Inflection AI $650 million: $620 million for a non-exclusive license to Inflection's AI models (which became available on Azure), and $30 million to waive legal claims related to the mass hiring — [Bloomberg via Yahoo Finance summary](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/microsoft-pay-inflection-ai-650-210933932.html); [DeepLearning.AI's The Batch](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/microsoft-pays-inflection-ai-650-million-hires-most-of-its-staff).
- Inflection used the licensing fee to return capital to investors (Greylock, Dragoneer, and others), reportedly at about 1.5x their original investment — same source.
- This was explicitly structured to avoid being a formal acquisition (an "acqui-hire" without an acquisition), and it drew regulatory attention: the FTC opened a formal investigation into the deal in June 2024, and UK regulators separately declined to open an in-depth probe (announced September 2024) — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-06-06/us-ftc-probes-microsoft-deal-with-ai-firm-inflection-wsj-says); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/09/04/microsoft-avoids-uk-probe-into-hiring-of-inflection-ai-employees.html).
- **Ending shape**: talent + license absorption, not liquidation — Inflection continued to exist afterward as a much smaller company, but its founders, core researchers, and effectively its frontier-model ambitions moved to Microsoft.

### 3.2 Adept → Amazon (June 28, 2024) — license-and-hire

- Amazon hired Adept's CEO David Luan (a former OpenAI VP of engineering) and most of its co-founders (Augustus Odena, Maxwell Nye, Erich Elsen, Kelsey Szot) plus additional staff, out of a roughly 100-person team, and licensed Adept's technology (including AI models and datasets) — [GeekWire](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/amazon-hires-founders-from-well-funded-enterprise-ai-startup-adept-to-boost-tech-giants-agi-team/); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/06/28/amazon-hires-execs-from-ai-startup-adept-and-licenses-its-technology.html).
- Investors in Adept were reportedly paid back as part of the arrangement — [Semafor](https://www.semafor.com/article/08/02/2024/investors-in-adept-ai-will-be-paid-back-after-amazon-hires-startups-top-talent).
- Around 20 employees remained at Adept afterward, with engineering head Zach Brock becoming the new CEO; the company continued in reduced form — [search-result summary](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/amazon-hires-founders-from-well-funded-enterprise-ai-startup-adept-to-boost-tech-giants-agi-team/).
- This deal explicitly mirrored the Microsoft/Inflection structure and drew the same kind of regulatory scrutiny (FTC inquiry into Amazon) — [search-result summary](https://www.geekwire.com/2024/amazon-hires-founders-from-well-funded-enterprise-ai-startup-adept-to-boost-tech-giants-agi-team/).

### 3.3 Character.AI → Google (August 2, 2024) — license-and-rehire

- Google signed a licensing deal valued at about $2.7 billion, granting Google a non-exclusive license to Character.AI's technology; co-founders Noam Shazeer and Daniel De Freitas (both ex-Googlers) returned to Google (Shazeer to Google DeepMind) — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/08/02/ex-google-engineers-from-characterai-re-join-company-with-ai-partnership-.html); [PYMNTS](https://www.pymnts.com/news/artificial-intelligence/2024/google-reportedly-spent-2-7-billion-to-rehire-character-ai-founder/).
- Existing Character.AI investors were reportedly cashed out at a valuation near $2.5 billion — [search-result summary](https://mlq.ai/news/characterai-explores-sale-or-new-funding-amid-rising-costs/).
- Underlying financial pressure: Character.AI ran on a high cost-per-message infrastructure where user growth directly scaled losses; the Google deal is reported to have eliminated the "burn-rate risk that had threatened the company's survival" and lowered inference costs going forward by reducing reliance on proprietary model-building — [search-result summary](https://em360tech.com/tech-articles/character-ai-scraps-building-llms-after-google-deal). ⚠️ I did not find a source with an explicit cash-runway figure (e.g., "X months left") for Character.AI before the deal — the "threatened survival" framing is qualitative, from aggregator commentary, not a hard reported number.
- The Character.AI story continued past the Google deal: by August 2025, reports had Character.AI "exploring a sale or new funding amid rising costs," suggesting the underlying cost pressure did not fully disappear even after the Google licensing cash injection — [MLQ News](https://techstartups.com/2025/08/20/character-ai-in-talks-to-sell-or-raise-new-funding-as-chatbot-costs-pile-up/). The DOJ was also reported investigating whether the deal's structure was designed to evade merger review — [Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/sy06wllflg).

### 3.4 Stability AI (2024 cash crisis and recovery)

- **Crisis state (as of ~October 2023, reported March 2024)**: Stability AI was spending an estimated $8 million/month, had just $4 million in the bank, was projected to make only $11 million in 2023 sales, while owing $99 million/year for cloud infrastructure (AWS, Google Cloud, CoreWeave) and $53 million/year in operating expenses/wages — [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/kenrickcai/2024/03/29/how-stability-ais-founder-tanked-his-billion-dollar-startup/).
- An October 2023 board meeting reportedly discussed a $95 million tentative funding lifeline pending due diligence, of which only a fraction was ultimately wired (with Intel investing $20 million of it) — same source.
- **March 22–26, 2024**: founder/CEO Emad Mostaque resigned amid a "hollowed out" company with mass executive departures; COO Shan Shan Wong and CTO Christian Laforte became interim co-CEOs — [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2024-03-26/stability-ai-ceo-emad-mostaque-resignation-what-happened); [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/03/22/stability-ai-ceo-resigns-because-youre-not-going-to-beat-centralized-ai-with-more-centralized-ai/).
- **April 18, 2024**: Stability AI laid off about 10% of staff (20+ employees) to "right-size" — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/04/18/ai-startup-stability-lays-off-10percent-of-employees-after-ceo-exit.html).
- **June 25, 2024 — the rescue**: Stability AI closed a new investment round (Greycroft, Coatue, Sound Ventures, Lightspeed, Sean Parker, Eric Schmidt, and incoming CEO Prem Akkaraju among the investors), which included forgiveness of over $100 million in debt and $300 million in future spending obligations (i.e., renegotiated-away cloud commitments). Sean Parker became Executive Chairman; Prem Akkaraju (former Weta Digital CEO) became CEO — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/06/25/stability-ai-lands-a-lifeline-from-sean-parker-greycroft) (reported investment size ~$80M cash injection alongside the debt/obligation forgiveness).
- **Recovery (Dec 2024 – 2025)**: CEO Akkaraju reported triple-digit growth rates and elimination of debt, with expansion into film/TV and enterprise licensing — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/12/09/stability-ai-new-ceo-prem-akkaraju-business-triple-digit-growth-greycroft-dana-settle-brainstormai/).
- **Ending shape**: this is the clearest "true near-zero cash" case in the set — $4M cash against ~$150M+/year in obligations — rescued not by acquisition but by a fresh equity round paired with the counterparties (cloud vendors, creditors) agreeing to forgive/restructure debt, i.e., a negotiated survival rather than a sale.

## Transfer notes: modeling near-bankruptcy in a 20-minute AI-lab game

Grounded in the timeline above, here is how I'd translate this history into game mechanics for calibrating a bankruptcy threshold.

1. **Model a "danger zone," not a hard cliff.** Every real case above (OpenAI 2024, Stability AI 2023–24) shows labs operating for months at single-digit months of runway while still functioning, raising, and even growing revenue. A single zero-cash-triggers-instant-game-over rule doesn't match reality — instead, trigger a "danger zone" state once estimated runway drops under a threshold (e.g., ≤6 months, matching OpenAI's July 2024 framing), during which the player faces escalating penalties (worse deal terms, forced emergency options) but isn't eliminated outright until a real deadline passes (e.g., payroll or a compute-vendor debt covenant).

2. **Give burn rate two speeds, deliberately.** The OpenAI 2025→2026 numbers show trailing burn (~$8B/yr) and forward-looking burn (~$27B/yr projected) can imply wildly different runway (37 vs. 11 months) from the *same* cash balance. A game mechanic where the player's displayed "runway" depends on whether they're scaling compute spend up (shortens it fast) or holding steady (looks safe) captures the real dynamic — and rewards/punishes over-committing to infrastructure spend the way OpenAI's 2026 trajectory does.

3. **Emergency options, each with a real modeled cost, drawn from what actually happened:**
   - **Strategic/compute-for-equity investment (OpenAI+Microsoft 2019 & 2023, Anthropic+Amazon/Google 2023–2026):** injects large cash/credits, but costs equity and gives the investor a profit-share or compute lock-in (e.g., Microsoft's 75%-until-repaid-then-49% term; Anthropic's $100B/10-year AWS spend commitment). Model this as: instant large cash infusion + a long-term recurring spend obligation to the same investor + reduced player control over future strategic choices (e.g., can't freely choose cloud vendor).
   - **Structure change to unlock capital (OpenAI's 1999→2019 pivot and 2025 PBC conversion):** doesn't inject cash directly but removes a fundraising cap, unlocking future rounds — with a real deadline risk attached (OpenAI's round could legally convert to debt if the conversion didn't happen in 2 years). Model as a one-time unlock with a ticking clock: fail to complete the structure change in N turns, and a prior "equity" round retroactively becomes a debt (cash-due) obligation.
   - **Emergency bridge round at a down-round or crisis valuation (Stability AI June 2024):** small cash amount relative to the hole, but paired with debt/obligation forgiveness from creditors — i.e., it works because it also shrinks the burn, not just because it adds cash. Model as: modest cash + a percentage reduction to ongoing burn (representing renegotiated vendor contracts), at the cost of a large dilution/valuation hit and possibly a leadership change (Stability's CEO exit).
   - **Acquihire/license offer (Inflection, Adept, Character.AI, all 2024):** the "game over, but softly" ending. The lab as an independent entity effectively stops pursuing frontier ambitions; investors get a fixed, sub-outsized cash payout (~1–1.5x); key staff and technology move to the acquirer. Model as an alternate ending state rather than a loss: player "exits" with a fixed payout instead of continuing, distinct from bankruptcy (zero payout).
   - **Revenue surge (Anthropic 2025–2026):** the only path in this dataset where a lab's own growth — not a rescue deal — closed the burn/revenue gap. Slower to trigger than a funding round, but doesn't cost equity or impose vendor lock-in. Model as a compounding but slow-acting lever the player can invest in (product/GTM) that pays off several turns later, contrasted against financing options that pay off immediately but cost more strategically.

4. **Calibrate the "true zero" threshold using Stability AI, not OpenAI.** OpenAI's worst documented moment (mid-2024) was still "~6–12 months of runway, per press framing" — a well-capitalized near-miss. Stability AI's October 2023 state ($4M cash against $150M+/year obligations, i.e., a matter of weeks) is the closest real-world analogue to true "about to hit zero." If the game wants a genuine bankruptcy-imminent state distinct from the "danger zone," Stability AI's ratio (cash-on-hand covering under a month of obligations) is a more honest calibration point than anything found for OpenAI or Anthropic.

5. **Don't give Anthropic a crisis it didn't have.** Unlike OpenAI, no source in this research shows Anthropic ever being reported near a cash-out date — its worst documented year (highest burn-to-revenue ratio) still coincided with its investors (Amazon, Google) continuing to commit tens of billions ahead of need. If the game features an "Anthropic-like" archetype, its intended difficulty curve should be "grows into profitability while still burning heavily," not "flirts with zero" — reserve the flirt-with-zero archetype for the "OpenAI-like" and "Stability-AI-like" labs.
