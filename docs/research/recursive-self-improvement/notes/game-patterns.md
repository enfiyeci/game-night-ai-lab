# How games model a self-accelerating loop (subagent report, 2026-09-26)

Output of a Sonnet research subagent, condensed by the orchestrator (wording tightened, no facts
added), kept for provenance. Not re-checked. The ⚠️ marks are the subagent's own.

1. **Universal Paperclips.** Trust buys processors (speed) and memory; late game, self-replicating
   probes. Numbers run past readable scale on purpose. Loss of control: "value drift" rises with
   the autonomy ("probe trust") granted, and drifted probes turn hostile. ⚠️ Exact constants
   (0.005% replication, drift = probeTrust^1.2 × 0.000001) come from search snippets; the wiki
   returned 402. Lesson: tie the risk to the same resource that produces the speed.
2. **Intelligence Rising** (arXiv 2410.03092, ⚠️ pages 1–20 of 41) and the **AI 2027 tabletop**.
   Teams take public policy actions plus a secret R&D allocation; agreements to slow down routinely
   collapse; cyber-theft of rival progress is near-universal. The AI 2027 exercise gives the lab's
   alignment team its own seat; its page withholds the rules. Lesson: opacity itself is the risk;
   make oversight a conflict inside your own organisation.
3. **Civilization VI / Stellaris.** Civ's research boosts snowball with no brake (⚠️ figures from
   snippets). Stellaris's AI rebellion: once triggered, a small, starkly labelled fork (maintain,
   outlaw, grant citizenship) with late fixes that trade speed for outcome (⚠️ official wiki
   truncated; fandomspot read in full). Lesson: make the warning stage a visible choice, not a creeping bar.
4. **Factorio and incremental games** (⚠️ secondary). Exponential output is paced by a physical
   bottleneck (belt throughput) or by a chosen reset ("prestige") that trades progress for a
   permanent multiplier. Lesson: put the brake somewhere legible, not on the number itself.
5. **Plague Inc., Frostpunk, Papers, Please.** Plague: the same upgrades that add power raise world
   awareness, shown through a cure bar and a news ticker (⚠️ partly from search). Frostpunk: hope and
   discontent derive from individual citizens; laws solve a problem and create a named group that
   pays. Papers, Please: small daily rule changes make the player discover complicity alone.
6. **AI-lab games.** "AI Lab" (galaxy.click, Apr 2026, played briefly): processing power, an "AGI
   Progress" bar with named tiers, "alignment decay" that collapses revenue if ignored. "The Choice
   Before Us" (LessWrong post): "as your AI gets more powerful, the UI may start misbehaving. This
   is intentional." "Thoughts on AI Safety Megagame Design" (LessWrong): each new model is a
   probabilistic event; true capability kept private.

Top six borrowable patterns (subagent's ranking): (1) autonomy-coupled drift; (2) UI that starts
acting on its own; (3) secret parallel allocations; (4) named capability tiers on one bar; (5) a
visible fork at the warning stage; (6) danger as a side effect of the same upgrade, shown through
a world-level signal.
