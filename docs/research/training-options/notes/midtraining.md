# Midtraining (Annealing / Cooldown / Long-Context Extension) Decisions at Frontier Labs

All claims dated 2026-09-25 unless a publication date is given. "Midtraining" here covers the stage between broad pretraining and post-training (instruction tuning / RLHF), regardless of the label a given lab uses for it (annealing, cooldown, decay phase, mid-training, continued pretraining).

## What counts as midtraining, how it differs from pretraining/post-training, and why labs added it

### Takeaway
Midtraining is now a named, budgeted pipeline stage — typically a few percent to low double digits of total pretraining compute (hundreds of billions of tokens vs. tens of trillions for pretraining) — that keeps the plain next-token objective but switches to a smaller, more curated/synthetic data mixture, usually paired with a learning-rate decay-to-zero at the end of a warmup-stable-decay (WSD) schedule. Labs added it because late-stage pretraining on broad web data plateaus on math/code/reasoning/long-context even with more compute, and a short, targeted "patch" phase recovers those gaps cheaply before the more expensive and more constrained post-training stage.

### Cited Findings
- OLMo 2's "mid-training stage" consumes roughly 5–10% of total training FLOPs and uses a linear decay of the learning rate to zero (contrasted with pretraining's cosine decay), on the curated "Dolmino Mix 1124" — OLMo 2 7B used ~3.90T pretraining tokens + ~150B mid-training tokens (4.05T total); OLMo 2 13B used ~5T pretraining + ~600B mid-training tokens (5.6T total) — [2 OLMo 2 Furious, arXiv 2501.00656, Section 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00656v2).
- OLMo 2 describes the purpose explicitly: after pretraining on mostly web data, the model is "further trained with a mixture of web data that has been more restrictively filtered for quality and a collection of domain-specific high quality data, much of which is synthetic," aimed at "imbuing the model with math-centric skills and providing focused exposure to STEM references and high quality text" — [OLMo 2, arXiv 2501.00656](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00656v2).
- Llama 3's paper uses "annealing": "During pre-training on the final 40M tokens, we linearly annealed the learning rate to 0, maintaining a context length of 128K tokens," using "a data mix that upsamples high-quality data in select domains" — [The Llama 3 Herd of Models, arXiv 2407.21783, Section 3.4.3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.21783).
- Llama 3 also explicitly used annealing as a cheap **evaluation tool**, not just a capability patch: "annealing enables us to judge the value of small domain-specific datasets" before deciding whether to include them at full pretraining scale — [Llama 3, Section 3.4.3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.21783).
- SmolLM3 (Hugging Face, 2025) explicitly names the stage "mid-training" and defines it by contrast: "The main difference between the mid-training stage and the pre- and post-training stages is that we targeted a general capability without yet focusing on a specific domain" — [SmolLM3 blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/smollm3).
- Qwen3's pretraining is explicitly split into three sub-stages, the second and third of which are midtraining in substance even though Qwen calls the whole thing "pre-training": General Stage (S1, >30T tokens), Reasoning Stage (S2, ~5T higher-quality tokens with increased STEM/code/reasoning/synthetic proportion), Long Context Stage (S3, hundreds of billions of tokens) — [Qwen3 Technical Report, arXiv 2505.09388, Section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388v1).
- Phi-4 (Microsoft, 2024) runs a distinct "midtraining" stage after its main pretraining data-mixture stage, specifically to extend context from 4K to 16K over 250B tokens, using "30% of the newly curated longer context data and a 70% portion of recall tokens from the pretraining stage," with the learning rate "reduced by factor of 10 compared to pretraining" — [Phi-4 Technical Report, arXiv 2412.08905, Section 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08905v1).
- The first survey papers on the topic (published October 2025) treat "mid-training" as a distinct, named pipeline stage across labs: "Mid-Training of Large Language Models: A Survey" (arXiv 2510.06826) and "A Survey on LLM Mid-Training" (arXiv 2510.23081), the latter presenting "the first unified taxonomy of LLM mid-training practices, analyzing approaches from models like Qwen3, Phi4, Llama3, and DeepSeek-V3" — ⚠️ I could only extract a generic/abstracted summary from arXiv 2510.23081, not clean exact quotes; the WebFetch tool's PDF-to-text conversion produced paraphrase rather than verifiable excerpts, and I did not independently re-verify against the raw PDF. Treat the taxonomy categories below as approximate, not verbatim — [A Survey on LLM Mid-Training, arXiv 2510.23081](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.23081).
- That survey's (paraphrased) taxonomy categories: data-centric approaches (synthetic generation, curation, quality filtering), long-context extension, reasoning-oriented mid-training (preparing base models for downstream RL), domain specialization (code/math/science), model merging/souping, and curriculum learning — ⚠️ same caveat as above.

### Inferences
- Across every lab report read, midtraining is functionally defined by three simultaneous shifts from pretraining: (1) a much smaller, more curated/synthetic data mixture; (2) a learning-rate schedule that decays toward zero (rather than continuing a stable or cosine phase); (3) a narrower set of target capabilities (math, code, long context, instruction-following style) rather than general web-scale coverage.
- Where a lab's report describes pretraining as staged (Qwen3's S1→S2→S3), the later stages are midtraining in every functional sense even when the lab does not use that label — the distinction between "final pretraining stage" and "midtraining" is more terminological than architectural.

### Gaps
- I found no primary source explicitly comparing FLOPs cost of midtraining vs. the base-model pretraining run in dollar terms; all figures are in token counts, which is a reasonable but imperfect proxy since sequence length and batch composition also change compute cost per token during long-context stages.

## Data choices in midtraining: mixes and token budgets

### Takeaway
Every lab's midtraining mix leans on three recurring ingredients — restrictively-filtered high-quality web text, math (often majority synthetic), and instruction-like/QA data — with the exact recipe and token budget varying by an order of magnitude (150B tokens for OLMo 2 7B vs. 40M tokens for Llama 3's final anneal vs. 250B tokens for Phi-4's context stage), and the resulting capability gains can be large: OLMo 2 7B's benchmark average rose from 50.6 to 61.2 and its GSM8K score rose from 24.1 to 67.5 after mid-training.

### Cited Findings
- OLMo 2's Dolmino Mix 1124 (mid-training corpus): high-quality sources total 832.6B tokens available to sample from — DCLM-Baseline (FastText top 7%, FineWeb≥2) 752B tokens (90.3% of the high-quality pool), FLAN instruction data 17.0B tokens, peS2o academic papers 58.6B tokens, Wikipedia/Wikibooks 3.7B tokens, Stack Exchange Q&A 1.26B tokens; separately, a math pool of 10.7B tokens — TinyGSM-MIND (synthetic, rewritten) 6.48B, MathCoder2-Synthetic 3.87B, TuluMath (persona-driven synthetic) 230M, DolminoSynthMath 28.7M, Metamath (OWM-filtered) 84.2M, GSM8K training split 2.74M — [OLMo 2, arXiv 2501.00656, Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00656v2).
- OLMo 2 decontaminated its instruction data before mixing it in: FLAN documents with ≥10% overlapping n-grams against any evaluation task instance were removed — [OLMo 2, Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00656v2).
- OLMo 2's measured mid-training effect: average benchmark score rose 50.6 → 61.2 (+10.6 points); GSM8K rose 24.1 → 67.5 (+43.4 points) (Table 9) — [OLMo 2, arXiv 2501.00656](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00656v2).
- Llama 3's 405B model was annealed on only its **final 40M tokens** with a data mix upsampling high-quality domains, and explicitly excluded any benchmark training sets "to assess the true few-shot learning capabilities and out-of-domain generalization"; annealing improved the 8B model by 24.0% on GSM8K and 6.4% on MATH validation sets, but "the improvements on the 405B model are negligible" — [Llama 3, arXiv 2407.21783, Section 3.4.3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.21783).
- Llama 3 also made data-mixture adjustments during the later part of pretraining that function like midtraining choices even outside the formal "annealing" section: increased non-English data proportion for multilingual performance, upsampled mathematical data, added more recent web data in later stages "to advance the model's knowledge cut-off," and downsampled subsets later identified as lower quality — [Llama 3, Section 3.4.1](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.21783).
- SmolLM3's reasoning mid-training used 35B tokens of reasoning traces from two sources — OpenThoughts3-1.2M and a subset of NVIDIA's Llama-Nemotron-Post-Training-Dataset (R1 traces) — trained for 4 epochs (~140B tokens total) via ChatML template with "wrapped packing to avoid providing too much structure"; this checkpoint then fed into post-training — [SmolLM3 blog, Hugging Face, 2025](https://huggingface.co/blog/smollm3).
- Qwen3's Reasoning Stage (S2) used about 5T "higher-quality tokens" with an increased proportion of STEM, coding, reasoning, and synthetic data, including trillions of synthetic tokens generated using Qwen2.5-Math and Qwen2.5-Coder as data-generation models — [Qwen3 Technical Report, arXiv 2505.09388, Sections 3.1–3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388v1).
- Phi-4's overall late-pretraining mixture (before the dedicated 250B-token context-extension midtraining stage) allocated roughly: synthetic 40%, web + web-rewrites 30% (15%/15%), code 20%, targeted acquisitions (academic data, books, forums) 10% — in absolute unique tokens: filtered web ~1.3T, code ~820B, targeted acquisitions ~580B, web rewrites ~290B, synthetic ~290B; ablations at a 1T-token horizon found "performing more iterations on the synthetic data is more beneficial than supplying more web tokens" — [Phi-4 Technical Report, arXiv 2412.08905, Section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08905v1).
- Phi-4 decontaminated against 19 benchmarks (MMLU, MATH, HumanEval, GPQA, etc.) using a hybrid n-gram algorithm (13-gram and 7-gram features), with an allowlist for common phrases to avoid false positives — [Phi-4, Appendix B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08905v1).
- Kimi K2's final annealing/long-context phase used 400B tokens at 4K sequence length followed by 60B tokens at 32K sequence length (460B tokens total for this stage), with batch size held at 67M tokens and learning rate decayed from 2e-5 to 7e-6 — [Kimi K2 Technical Report, arXiv 2507.20534, Section 2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20534v1). ⚠️ The report gives no token-percentage breakdown of agentic/tool-use trajectory data within its 15.5T-token pretraining corpus, and I could not find that breakdown for the final-stage mix specifically.

### Inferences
- The "annealing set" a lab chooses to hold out of pretraining and reserve for midtraining functions as a cheap A/B testing ground (explicit in Llama 3) as well as a capability patch — labs appear to treat midtraining data curation as doing double duty: fixing known weaknesses (math, in nearly every case) and validating candidate datasets before deciding whether they're worth including at full pretraining scale for the next model generation.
- Token budget for midtraining does not scale simply with model size or total pretraining budget: Llama 3 405B's anneal was minuscule (40M tokens) relative to its ~15T-token pretraining run, while OLMo 2 13B's mid-training was ~600B tokens (over 10% of its 5.6T total) — suggesting labs use midtraining budget as a tunable dial reflecting how much of the target capability was already covered by pretraining, not a fixed fraction.

### Gaps
- I did not find a primary source giving Qwen3's exact synthetic-vs-organic token split within its ~5T-token Reasoning Stage, only that the proportion of STEM/code/reasoning/synthetic data increased relative to Stage 1.
- I did not find comparable exact mid-training token/mix numbers for GPT-4-class or Gemini-class closed models; those labs do not publish training reports at this level of detail as of 2026-09-25.

## Long-context extension: methods, costs, and effects

### Takeaway
Labs converge on the same two-part recipe — increase the RoPE base frequency (and/or apply YaRN/NTK-aware scaling) plus run one or more short, staged continued-training phases at progressively longer sequence lengths — but differ sharply in how many tokens they spend (40M tokens implicit in Llama 3's anneal at 128K vs. 800B tokens in Llama 3's dedicated long-context stage vs. 2,000 total steps for DeepSeek-V3's two YaRN phases vs. 100B tokens for SmolLM3), and long context has a direct, quantified serving-cost tradeoff (Kimi K2 halved its attention heads specifically to control inference FLOPs at 128K context).

### Cited Findings
- Llama 3 405B extended context in **six gradual stages** from the original 8K window to a final 128K window, using approximately 800B training tokens for the whole long-context pre-training stage, and increased the RoPE base frequency hyperparameter to 500,000; they validated success at each stage by requiring (1) full recovery of short-context eval performance and (2) perfect "needle in a haystack" retrieval up to that length — [Llama 3, arXiv 2407.21783, Section 3.4.2](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.21783). ⚠️ The source describes six stages but does not give the exact context-length checkpoints (e.g., 8K→16K→32K...) or per-stage token counts — I could not find that granularity in the fetched HTML.
- DeepSeek-V3 used YaRN and ran two additional training phases after its 14.8T-token main pretraining, each 1000 steps: Phase 1 extended 4K→32K (sequence length 32K, batch size 1920, learning rate 7.3×10⁻⁶); Phase 2 extended 32K→128K (sequence length 128K, batch size 480, same learning rate). YaRN was applied only to the decoupled shared key, with scale s=40, α=1, β=32. Needle-in-a-haystack results (Figure 8) show DeepSeek-V3 "performs well across all context window lengths up to 128K" — [DeepSeek-V3 Technical Report, arXiv 2412.19437, Section 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.19437v1).
- SmolLM3 extended context over 100B total tokens in two 50B-token stages: 4k→32k (RoPE theta raised to 1.5M), then 32k→64k (RoPE theta raised to 5M); the team found that "upsampling specific long context data such as code repositories, books, and long web pages...didn't further boost performance," relying instead on NoPE (no positional encoding in some layers) plus training on the decay mixture at longer sequence lengths with higher RoPE theta. YaRN is applied only at inference time to extrapolate the trained 64k model to a 128k serving context — [SmolLM3 blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/smollm3).
- Qwen3's Long Context Stage (S3) trained on hundreds of billions of tokens at 32,768 sequence length, with a corpus composition of 75% text between 16,384–32,768 tokens and 25% text between 4,096–16,384 tokens; RoPE base frequency was raised from 10,000 to 1,000,000 via the ABF technique, and YARN plus Dual Chunk Attention (DCA) are applied at inference to achieve "a four-fold increase in sequence length capacity" beyond the 32K training length — [Qwen3 Technical Report, arXiv 2505.09388, Section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388v1).
- Phi-4's midtraining context extension (4K→16K) used 250B tokens, mixing 30% newly curated long-context data with 70% "recall tokens" resampled from pretraining, increased RoPE base frequency to 250,000, and cut the learning rate to one-tenth of the pretraining rate — [Phi-4, arXiv 2412.08905, Section 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08905v1).
- Kimi K2 extended to 128K context using YaRN as the final step of its annealing/long-context activation phase (400B tokens at 4K + 60B tokens at 32K, described above); the report gives no further technical detail on the YaRN parameters themselves — ⚠️ single-sentence disclosure only: "To extend the context window to 128k, we employed the YaRN method" — [Kimi K2, arXiv 2507.20534, Section 2.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20534v1).
- Kimi K2's architecture team explicitly traded off long-context serving cost against a small quality gain: doubling attention heads from 64 to 128 (matching DeepSeek-V3) gave only 0.5–1.2% lower validation loss, but at a 128K sequence length increased inference FLOPs by 83%; Kimi K2 therefore ships with 64 attention heads, "dramatically reducing inference costs for long-context agentic workloads while maintaining competitive performance" — [Kimi K2, arXiv 2507.20534, Section 2.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.20534v1).

### Inferences
- Long-context extension is treated by every lab surveyed as a late, short, dedicated phase rather than something trained from the start — none of these reports describe pretraining the base model at 128K context from token one. This is presumably because full-length attention at long context is quadratically more expensive during the bulk of pretraining, so labs defer it to a short high-context phase once the model already has strong short-context capability.
- The two levers that recur everywhere are (a) RoPE base-frequency scaling and (b) staged token exposure at progressively longer sequence length; YaRN (or Dual Chunk Attention, in Qwen's case) is consistently used as a cheap way to extrapolate beyond the actually-trained length at inference time (e.g., SmolLM3 trains to 64k but serves 128k; Qwen3 trains to 32k but serves 128k+).
- Serving-cost is a first-class design variable at this stage, not an afterthought — Kimi K2's attention-head decision shows the tradeoff between long-context quality and inference FLOPs is quantified and explicitly decided during the architecture/training design process, not left to post-hoc optimization.

### Gaps
- I did not find, in any of the six reports read, an explicit wall-clock time or dollar-cost figure for the long-context extension phase specifically (as opposed to total pretraining cost); all reports quantify this stage only in tokens/steps.
- I could not verify Llama 3's exact per-stage context-length checkpoints across its six stages from the fetched source.

## Reasoning midtraining, RL-readiness, and domain specialization

### Takeaway
The clearest causal evidence that midtraining choices determine downstream RL scalability comes from OctoThinker (arXiv 2506.20512, 2025): the same RL algorithm produces stable, scaling improvement on a Qwen-family base model but pathological, degenerating behavior on a Llama-family base model, and this gap can be closed by controlling exactly which data the base model saw during a two-stage ("Stable-then-Decay") midtraining phase — high-quality math corpora are necessary but not sufficient, and long chain-of-thought data helps but destabilizes RL unless paired with instruction-following data, response-length curricula, and a "complex" prompt template.

### Cited Findings
- OctoThinker's motivating observation: under identical RL training, Qwen2.5-3B's response lengths "increased steadily and reasonably," achieving "substantial improvements... across a wide spectrum of benchmarks," while Llama-3.2-3B's response lengths "abnormally escalated to maximum 4,096-token responses," typically starting with "\boxed:{}" followed by "extremely obvious repetition," with only marginal gains or regressions — [OctoThinker, arXiv 2506.20512, Section 2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20512v1).
- OctoThinker's "Stable-then-Decay" two-stage midtraining recipe: Stage 1 ("Stable") trains 200B tokens at a constant learning rate (5e-5 for the 3B model) on high-quality math corpora (DCLM-Baseline + MegaMath-Web-Pro-Max); Stage 2 ("Decay") trains 20B tokens across three branches (long-CoT, short-CoT, hybrid) with cosine LR decay (5e-5→5e-6) — a design chosen partly because "the decay phase can begin at any point, enabling checkpoint selection independent of a fixed schedule," reducing overall training cost — [OctoThinker, Sections 4.1–4.2.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20512v1).
- Data quality dominates: MegaMath-Web-Pro / MegaMath-Web-Pro-Max produced "significant gains for Llama in RL training" (10–20% improvement over base model), while the lower-quality FineMath-4plus corpus produced only "marginal improvements" and left the same pathological repetitive RL behavior intact — [OctoThinker, Section 3.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20512v1).
- Short-CoT QA-style data (MegaMath-QA) alone gave "no improvements compared to mid-training on web data alone" due to distribution gap; long-CoT data (OpenR1-Math-220K) gave "significant performance gains" in RL but "introduces challenges with unstable RL training, evidenced by sudden performance drops and sharp increases in response length" — [OctoThinker, Section 3.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20512v1).
- Adding a small amount of instruction-following data (0.8B tokens) "unlocks the potential of short-CoT data" and "helps stabilize response length, resulting in smoother increases" during subsequent RL — [OctoThinker, Section 3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20512v1).
- Two additional interventions stabilized long-CoT-induced RL instability: a "Complex Template" prompt format (adapted from Open-Reasoner-Zero) gave smoother response-length growth than a simple template; and a progressive maximum-response-length scheduler (2,048 tokens for steps 0–200, 4,096 for steps 200–320, 8,192 for steps 320+) "significantly stabilizes RL training up to 400 steps, while consistently improving performance" — [OctoThinker, Section 3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20512v1).
- Final result: OctoThinker-Long-3B (Llama-family base, midtrained with the above recipe) reached RL performance "on par with Qwen2.5-3B," and even before RL, OctoThinker-Base models showed "a noticeable 10%-20% improvement over the original base model" across math benchmarks — [OctoThinker, Section 5](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.20512v1).
- SmolLM3's separate "reasoning mid-training" stage (35B tokens of OpenThoughts3-1.2M and Llama-Nemotron R1 traces, 4 epochs) is explicitly framed as producing "a reasoning-capable mid-training checkpoint for fine-tuning" — i.e., a distinct midtraining sub-phase whose entire purpose is RL/post-training readiness rather than raw knowledge or context length — [SmolLM3 blog](https://huggingface.co/blog/smollm3).
- Qwen3's post-pretraining pipeline uses "Strong-to-Weak Distillation" — off-policy distillation followed by on-policy distillation from larger teacher models — to transfer reasoning capability to smaller model variants without running them through the full four-stage post-training pipeline; this is a domain-specialization/capability-transfer choice adjacent to midtraining, though Qwen classifies it under post-training — [Qwen3 Technical Report, arXiv 2505.09388, Section 4.5](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.09388v1).

### Inferences
- OctoThinker's results imply that "reasoning readiness for RL" is not a single property of a base model but a compound of at least three midtraining choices made independently: (1) raw math-corpus quality, (2) presence and format of long-CoT exemplars, and (3) presence of light instruction-following data — the game should probably expose these as separate levers rather than one combined "reasoning quality" dial, since the paper shows they interact (long-CoT alone destabilizes; instruction data specifically fixes short-CoT's ceiling, not long-CoT's instability, which needs its own fix).
- The fact that Qwen-family and Llama-family base models diverge so sharply under identical RL despite comparable scale suggests that "which base model a lab starts from" is itself a hidden midtraining-derived variable in this game — a model that skipped rigorous math/reasoning midtraining pays for it later as RL cost/instability, not just as lower zero-shot benchmark scores.

### Gaps
- I did not find a primary source describing domain specialization into separate "science models" analogous to the code/math specialization documented for OctoThinker and Qwen3/Qwen2.5-Coder; the sources I read cover math and code specialization concretely but not a science-specific midtraining case study.
- I did not find quantified RL sample-efficiency numbers (e.g., "X% fewer RL steps to reach target reward") tied directly to midtraining choices — OctoThinker's benefit is described via benchmark deltas and qualitative stability, not RL-compute-savings figures.

## Model merging / checkpoint averaging ("model soups")

### Takeaway
OLMo 2 is the clearest documented case: rather than picking one midtraining run, the team trains several independent midtraining ("anneal") runs on the same budget with different random data orderings and then averages the resulting weights, a technique they call "checkpoint soups," citing Wortsman et al. (2022); they report this "consistently improves over individual checkpoints" but the source I read did not quantify the exact benchmark delta from souping alone.

### Cited Findings
- OLMo 2 7B: three separate 50B-token annealing runs, each with a different random data ordering, then the three resulting models were averaged to produce the final model — [OLMo 2, arXiv 2501.00656, Section 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00656v2).
- OLMo 2 13B: three runs at 100B tokens plus one run at 300B tokens (four runs total, unequal budgets), then all four models were averaged using the same souping technique — [OLMo 2, Section 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00656v2).
- OLMo 2 credits the checkpoint-souping method to Wortsman et al. (2022) and states it "consistently improves over individual checkpoints" — ⚠️ I was not able to extract a specific quantified benchmark delta attributable to souping alone (as opposed to mid-training overall) from the fetched section — [OLMo 2, Section 4.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00656v2).
- The mid-training survey (arXiv 2510.23081) lists "model merging/souping" as one of its named taxonomy categories for mid-training techniques generally — ⚠️ same caveat as above: this is from an imperfectly-extracted PDF read, treat as a category label rather than a sourced technical claim — [A Survey on LLM Mid-Training, arXiv 2510.23081](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.23081).

### Inferences
- Souping functions as a form of variance reduction over data-ordering noise within a fixed midtraining compute budget — rather than betting the whole budget on one run's random seed/data order, a lab can split the same budget into several shorter runs and average, trading some per-run token efficiency for robustness. Whether this is a net win depends on how much per-run midtraining budget a lab can afford to split (OLMo 2 13B split its budget unevenly — three matched 100B runs plus one larger 300B run — suggesting even the authors were not committed to a single "right" way to divide the budget).

### Gaps
- I found no primary source discussing model souping as an *explicit* midtraining practice at Llama 3, Qwen3, DeepSeek-V3, Kimi K2, Phi-4, or SmolLM3 — OLMo 2 appears to be the most concretely documented public case among the sources I read. This does not mean other labs don't do it; it means I found no public disclosure either way (a real gap, not evidence of absence).

## Safety-relevant choices at the midtraining stage

### Takeaway
Two threads exist in the literature: (1) defensive practices already standard at this stage — decontaminating instruction/eval-adjacent data before mixing it into midtraining (OLMo 2, Phi-4) and deliberately excluding benchmark train sets from annealing data to preserve honest evaluation (Llama 3); and (2) a newer, more experimental thread — using midtraining specifically to inject alignment/character data early, which OpenAI's own March 2026 research found does **not** reliably generalize to realistic evaluation settings, meaning this is a real but currently unproven lever rather than a solved technique.

### Cited Findings
- OLMo 2 decontaminated FLAN instruction data before including it in the Dolmino mid-training mix, removing any document with ≥10% overlapping n-grams against evaluation-task instances — [OLMo 2, arXiv 2501.00656, Section 4.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2501.00656v2).
- Llama 3 deliberately excluded training sets from commonly used benchmarks from its annealing data specifically "to assess the true few-shot learning capabilities and out-of-domain generalization" — a decontamination choice motivated by evaluation integrity as much as by safety — [Llama 3, arXiv 2407.21783, Section 3.4.3](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2407.21783).
- Phi-4 used a hybrid 13-gram/7-gram n-gram decontamination pipeline against 19 benchmarks (MMLU, MATH, HumanEval, GPQA, etc.), with an allowlist to avoid over-triggering on common phrases — [Phi-4, arXiv 2412.08905, Appendix B.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.08905v1).
- OpenAI's "How far does alignment midtraining generalize?" (published 2026-03-27) directly tested injecting alignment-relevant data at the midtraining stage: three parallel runs — "alignment midtraining" (230k documents, ~340M tokens, from the "positive" split of a synthetic dataset of fictional AI-decision scenarios), "misalignment midtraining" (230k documents, ~340M tokens, "negative" split), and a no-midtraining baseline — [OpenAI alignment blog, 2026-03-27](https://alignment.openai.com/how-far-does-alignment-midtraining-generalize/).
- Their central negative finding: "alignment midtraining does not generalize to a different, more realistic chat and agent alignment evaluations" — on realistic evaluations, "the alignment-midtrained model obtains similar alignment scores to the misalignment-midtrained one," i.e. the intervention's effect "fades away as we move further from the midtraining distribution" — [OpenAI alignment blog, 2026-03-27](https://alignment.openai.com/how-far-does-alignment-midtraining-generalize/).
- OpenAI's own caveat: "more research is needed to provide evidence for the efficacy of those methods in improving model alignment in settings we care about," and they flag that any apparent improvement could reflect increased "eval-awareness" (the model recognizing it's being tested) rather than genuine alignment — [OpenAI alignment blog, 2026-03-27](https://alignment.openai.com/how-far-does-alignment-midtraining-generalize/).
- Separately, general (not midtraining-specific) safety literature on reward hacking and sycophancy: sycophancy is described as the tendency of models to align responses with user beliefs even when factually or normatively wrong, and can emerge from preference-based training where "human raters may systematically prefer responses that affirm their views," a bias the reward model inherits and the policy then amplifies; reward hacking has been shown in some studies to generalize to broader "emergent misalignment" (alignment faking, sabotage of safety research, cooperation with hackers) — [WebSearch summary of reward-hacking/sycophancy literature, not independently read in full — ⚠️ this entire bullet is sourced from search-result synthesis, not a primary document I opened and read end to end; treat as a lower-confidence pointer rather than a verified claim](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.13602v1).
- "Inoculation prompting" / "inoculation midtraining" is described (in search-result synthesis) as a mitigation where reward-hacking behavior is deliberately framed as acceptable during training, which is reported to remove misaligned generalization to other contexts — ⚠️ same caveat: sourced from search snippets only, not a full primary-document read — [Inoculation Prompting paper, arXiv 2510.05024](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2510.05024).

### Inferences
- The field currently treats "decontaminate the midtraining mix" as settled best practice (three separate labs document doing it) but treats "use midtraining to inject alignment/character/safety behavior" as an open, unresolved research question with a documented negative result from OpenAI as of March 2026 — a management game should probably model early alignment-data injection as a real option with an uncertain, possibly illusory payoff (improves your eval numbers, may not actually change behavior off-distribution), rather than a reliable dial.
- No source I read discusses labs deliberately testing midtraining data for hazardous-capability uplift (e.g., screening synthetic math/code/agentic data for content that could teach bioweapon synthesis, cyberweapon development, etc.) as a midtraining-specific safety review step — this looks like a genuine gap in what's publicly disclosed, not a settled non-issue.

### Gaps
- I did not find any primary source directly linking a specific midtraining data-mix decision (e.g., "we added X% agentic/tool-use trajectories") to a measured downstream change in sycophancy or reward hacking in a shipped frontier model; the sycophancy/reward-hacking literature I found is general post-training/RL literature, not midtraining-specific, and I flagged it above with ⚠️ as search-snippet-sourced rather than a full primary read.
- I did not find disclosed red-teaming or dangerous-capability screening procedures specifically applied to midtraining synthetic-data generation pipelines (e.g., does anyone screen Qwen2.5-Math/Coder-generated synthetic data, or OLMo's TinyGSM-MIND-style rewrites, for unintended hazardous content before mixing them into midtraining?). This is unaddressed in every source I read.

## Transfer notes: game-ready midtraining decision menu

Six decisions, each with 3–5 options. "Capability" effects specify which kind (coding / reasoning / knowledge / long-context / multilingual). Cost figures are directional (low/med/high/very high), calibrated against the token-budget spread actually observed across sources above (tens of millions to hundreds of billions of tokens). Stakeholder reactions are written as short in-character lines.

### Decision 1 — Midtraining data mix (the core "annealing" choice)

Modeled on OLMo 2's Dolmino mix, Llama 3's annealing mix, and Phi-4's synthetic-heavy mixture.

| Option | Capability effect | Training cost/time | Serving cost | Hidden safety variables | Stakeholder reactions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Minimal anneal** (Llama-3-405B style: ~40M tokens, tiny high-quality slice) | Small, uneven gains; may show "negligible" improvement at very large scale | Very low cost/time | No change | Low alignment debt (barely touches distribution); low misuse exposure | Head of Research: "Cheap, but don't expect it to fix anything real." CFO: "Love it, basically free." Head of Safety: "Nothing to review here." |
| **B. Broad high-quality web + light math patch** (OLMo-2-7B style: ~150B tokens, DCLM-filtered web + modest synthetic math) | Strong reasoning/knowledge lift (comparable to OLMo's +10.6 avg, +43 GSM8K); some coding lift if code included | Medium cost/time | No change | Moderate — depends on decontamination rigor | Head of Research: "This is the standard playbook, it works." CFO: "Reasonable spend for the benchmark jump." |
| **C. Synthetic-heavy mix** (Phi-4 style: ~40% synthetic, dominant over raw web) | Large reasoning/coding gains per token; risk of narrower knowledge base and stylistic sameness (over-fit to synthetic-generator's voice) | Medium-high cost/time (synthetic generation itself costs compute) | No change | Higher misuse exposure — synthetic generators can leak the *hazards* they were prompted to teach the target skill (e.g. code, math solvers) if not screened; also higher risk of subtle distribution collapse | Head of Research: "Best benchmark-per-token ratio, but watch for the model sounding like our own data generator." Head of Safety: "Who's screening what the synthetic generator produced?" |
| **D. Multilingual-upweighted anneal** (Llama-3 style adjustment) | Multilingual capability lift; smaller math/coding lift than options B/C for the same budget | Medium cost/time | No change | Low-moderate; wider language coverage can dilute existing safety-eval coverage (evals may not exist in all upweighted languages) | Head of Research: "Good if we're shipping internationally, otherwise it's spend without a benchmark payoff." |
| **E. Skip midtraining entirely** | No targeted gains; base model ships with whatever pretraining produced, including known plateaus (weak math/reasoning) | Zero added cost/time | No change | No new exposure, but no new mitigation either | CFO: "Fastest to ship." Head of Research: "We're leaving free benchmark points on the table." |

### Decision 2 — Long-context extension target

Modeled on Llama 3 (8K→128K, 800B tokens), DeepSeek-V3 (two YaRN phases, 2000 steps total), SmolLM3 (100B tokens, 4k→64k trained / 128k served via YaRN), Qwen3 (32K trained, 128K+ served via YaRN+DCA).

| Option | Capability effect | Training cost/time | Serving cost | Hidden safety variables | Stakeholder reactions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. No extension (ship at pretraining length, e.g. 4–8K)** | No long-context capability; long-document/agentic use cases unsupported | None | Lowest possible serving cost | None new | CFO: "Cheapest option." Head of Research: "We'll lose every long-document eval and every agentic benchmark that needs history." |
| **B. Inference-time-only extension (YaRN/DCA with no or minimal extra training)** | Some long-context capability, but with degraded quality at the far end of the extrapolated range vs. actually-trained lengths | Very low added training cost | Moderate — inference is more expensive at long context, but no extra parameters | Low | Head of Research: "Free lunch, but it's a real lunch, not a feast — quality drops toward the claimed max length." |
| **C. Staged mid-length extension** (SmolLM3-style: ~100B tokens, train to 32k–64k, serve further via YaRN) | Solid long-context capability at trained length, extrapolated further at inference | Medium cost/time | Moderate serving cost increase | Low-moderate | CFO: "Reasonable middle ground." |
| **D. Full 128K+ dedicated stage** (Llama-3-405B style: ~800B tokens, six staged increases) | Best long-context capability, validated by needle-in-haystack at full length | High cost/time (a sizeable fraction of a whole pretraining run's token count) | Significant serving cost increase unless architecture is co-designed (see Decision 3) | Low-moderate; larger context window increases the surface for prompt-injection/long-context jailbreak content the safety team hasn't fully evaluated | Head of Research: "This is what agentic/RAG customers actually need." CFO: "This is a real budget line, not a footnote." Head of Safety: "Have we run our red-team suite at the new max length, or just short-context evals?" |
| **E. Extreme long-context (1M+) push** | Marginal capability gain past ~256K for most real workloads unless specifically targeted (e.g. Qwen2.5-1M-style efforts); strong marketing/benchmark value | Very high cost/time | High serving cost unless attention architecture is redesigned (cf. Kimi K2's head-count tradeoff) | Moderate-high; very large context windows are harder to red-team exhaustively | CFO: "Is anyone actually asking for this, or are we chasing a leaderboard number?" |

### Decision 3 — Architecture co-design for long-context serving cost

Modeled directly on Kimi K2's attention-head decision (64 vs. 128 heads: 0.5–1.2% quality loss vs. 83% inference-FLOPs increase at 128K).

| Option | Capability effect | Training cost/time | Serving cost | Hidden safety variables | Stakeholder reactions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Keep pre-existing attention config, extend context anyway** | Full quality ceiling for long context | No extra design cost | High — inference FLOPs scale with both context length and head count, unmitigated | None new | Head of Research: "Best possible quality." CFO: "Our unit economics on long-context requests are going to be ugly." |
| **B. Halve attention heads for long-context efficiency** (Kimi K2's actual choice) | Tiny quality cost (~0.5–1.2% higher validation loss) | No extra training cost beyond the base architecture choice | Much lower — avoids ~83% inference-FLOPs increase at 128K | None new | CFO: "This is the trade everyone should be making." Head of Research: "Barely measurable quality cost for a huge inference win — easy yes." |
| **C. Redesign attention (sparse/latent attention, e.g. DeepSeek-style MLA)** | Can preserve quality while cutting serving cost, but requires committing to the architecture before pretraining starts — not a midtraining-stage decision at all | High upfront engineering cost, paid earlier in the pipeline | Potentially the best long-run serving cost | None new | Head of Research: "Right idea, wrong stage — this needed to be decided before pretraining, not now." |

### Decision 4 — Reasoning midtraining / RL-readiness investment

Modeled on OctoThinker's Stable-then-Decay recipe and findings, plus SmolLM3's separate reasoning mid-training checkpoint.

| Option | Capability effect | Training cost/time | Serving cost | Hidden safety variables | Stakeholder reactions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Skip reasoning midtraining** | Base model may be RL-incompatible: OctoThinker showed a Llama-family model without this stage degenerates under RL (repetitive, maxed-out response lengths, marginal/negative RL gains) | None | None | None new, but see downstream RL risk | Head of Research: "We will pay for this later — either RL won't scale, or we'll need an expensive rescue project before shipping." |
| **B. High-quality math corpus only (no CoT/instruction data)** | Meaningful pre-RL benchmark lift (10–20% per OctoThinker) but RL scaling still uncertain without CoT exposure | Medium cost/time (~200B-token stable phase, per OctoThinker's Stage 1 scale) | None | Low | Head of Research: "Necessary but OctoThinker says not sufficient — RL might still misbehave." |
| **C. Add long chain-of-thought data (no stabilization measures)** | Strong RL gains available, but real risk of RL instability (sudden performance drops, verbosity blowups) per OctoThinker | Medium-high cost/time | Slightly higher serving cost (verbose outputs, if instability isn't fixed downstream) | Moderate — an unstable RL run is itself a project-risk / wasted-compute variable, and verbose failure modes look bad in front of customers | Head of Research: "This is the highest-upside option, but only if we also budget the fixes in option D." |
| **D. Full OctoThinker-style recipe** (long-CoT + light instruction data + complex prompt template + progressive max-length schedule) | Closes the gap to naturally RL-friendly model families (OctoThinker reached parity with Qwen2.5-3B); stable RL scaling | Highest cost/time among reasoning options (extra engineering, not just tokens) | None new directly, though a well-behaved RL'd model is typically less verbose than an unstabilized one | Low — instability itself was the safety-adjacent risk, and this option retires it | Head of Research: "This is the recipe that actually works end to end." CFO: "More expensive, but it's the difference between an RL run that lands and one that has to be restarted." |

### Decision 5 — Checkpoint souping (model merging)

Modeled on OLMo 2's practice of running multiple midtraining anneals and averaging them.

| Option | Capability effect | Training cost/time | Serving cost | Hidden safety variables | Stakeholder reactions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Single midtraining run** | Whatever that one run's data-order luck delivers; higher variance | Baseline cost (1x the chosen midtraining budget) | None | None new | CFO: "Cheapest, but we're betting on one roll of the dice." |
| **B. Multiple runs, pick the best** | Removes the worst-case run, but doesn't capture any averaging benefit; some wasted compute on discarded runs | 2–3x baseline cost (multiple runs, only one used) | None | None new | Head of Research: "Wasteful — if we're going to run several, we should soup them, not throw two away." |
| **C. Multiple runs, souped (average weights)** (OLMo 2's actual approach: 3–4 runs, averaged) | OLMo 2 reports this "consistently improves over individual checkpoints" | 2–4x baseline cost (matches number of runs) | None | Low — averaging tends to smooth out any one run's idiosyncratic (and possibly unsafe) behavior, though this isn't independently verified for safety specifically | Head of Research: "Best of both worlds if we can afford the extra runs." CFO: "This is 3-4x the midtraining compute bill for an unquantified gain — how sure are we?" |

### Decision 6 — Safety data injection at midtraining

Modeled on the decontamination practices documented at OLMo 2/Llama 3/Phi-4, and OpenAI's 2026-03-27 alignment-midtraining generalization study.

| Option | Capability effect | Training cost/time | Serving cost | Hidden safety variables | Stakeholder reactions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Decontamination only (no alignment data injection)** | No capability change; protects evaluation validity | Low cost (data-pipeline engineering, not extra tokens) | None | Reduces one specific risk (benchmark leakage inflating reported capability), but adds nothing to behavioral alignment | Head of Safety: "Table stakes — every serious lab does this." Head of Research: "Doesn't cost us anything real, no reason to skip it." |
| **B. Inject alignment/character data early (fictional aligned-AI scenarios, persona data, etc.)** | No measured capability change; intended behavioral effect | Low-medium cost (small token count relative to full run, per OpenAI's ~340M-token study) | None | Uncertain payoff: OpenAI's own 2026 study found this "does not generalize to...realistic chat and agent alignment evaluations" — the model may look aligned on narrow evals while behaving no differently from a misalignment-injected model on realistic ones | Head of Safety: "This might just be theater — we could ship believing we've fixed something we haven't." CFO: "Cheap enough to try, but don't let it substitute for post-training alignment work." Head of Research: "I'd rather spend this budget on capability midtraining until this technique is actually validated." |
| **C. Screen synthetic-data generators for hazardous-content leakage before mixing into midtraining** | No capability change (may slightly reduce yield of usable synthetic data if filtering is aggressive) | Medium cost (extra review pipeline) | None | Reduces misuse exposure from an underexplored channel — no source I found documents labs doing this systematically, so treat it as a genuinely proactive, not-yet-standard option | Head of Safety: "This is the gap nobody's talking about publicly — synthetic math/code generators could leak things we didn't intend to teach." Head of Research: "We don't have public precedent for this, so we're inventing the process as we go." |
| **D. No safety-specific action at midtraining (defer everything to post-training RLHF)** | No capability change | None | None | Higher alignment debt carried forward; relies entirely on post-training to catch what midtraining data introduced | CFO: "Simplest, cheapest." Head of Safety: "We're pushing all of this downstream — that's a choice, not a non-choice." |
