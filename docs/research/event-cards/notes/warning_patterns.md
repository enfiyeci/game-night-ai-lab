# Warning Patterns in Management/Strategy Games: Pre-Event Signals and Escalation

## Advisor warnings (Civilization, Democracy 4, Tropico, Two Point Hospital/Theme Hospital, Prison Architect)

### Takeaway
Advisor-delivered warnings work by attaching a persona (a named advisor, a PA voice, an almanac page) to a monitoring dashboard: the persona restates a metric qualitatively so the player gets a nudge without a raw number, and the "warning" is really a standing readout the player can check at will rather than something that pushes itself at them. Prison Architect is the exception among these five — it uses staff/system alerts tied to a visible danger meter rather than a named advisor.

### Cited Findings
- In Tropico 5, the in-game Almanac has dedicated tabs for Politics (approximate vote share for the player and their opponent before an election, plus a "Popular Support" indicator described as the second most important stat after Balance), a rebel/threat tab (more rebels, or more dissatisfied rebels, means more frequent attacks), a Foreign Relations tab (relations with neighboring countries, which telegraphs invasion risk from trade routes/constitution/diplomacy), and a Happiness tab broken into Food, Housing, Job, Religion, Crime, Safety, and Healthcare — letting the player see which faction is about to turn hostile before a revolt happens — [gamepressure.com Tropico 5 Almanac guide](https://www.gamepressure.com/tropico5/6-almanac/zf642c).
- ⚠️ The gamepressure page fetched did not mention advisors directly in connection with the Almanac (I read this page in full — the omission is the page's, not a partial read on my part); a companion Steam Community source found only in search snippets says rebel counts are checked via "foreign section, then threat level in the almanac" — [Steam Community discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/245620/discussions/0/540742667573194626/) (snippet-only, not fetched in full).
- ⚠️ (snippet-only, not fetched in full) In Civilization VI, the Advisor is an on-screen entity organized into five recommendation categories (Generic, Culture, Science, Faith, Military), each with its own icon, used mainly to suggest what to build/research rather than to warn of specific incoming threats — [Civilization Wiki: Advisor (Civ6)](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Advisor_(Civ6)) (page returned HTTP 402 on fetch; this is the WebSearch summary only). I could not confirm from a full read whether Civ6's advisors issue explicit pre-crisis warnings (e.g. of impending war or unhappiness) as opposed to build suggestions — flagged as a gap below.
- Cliff Harris (Positech Games) added a "media reports" layer to Democracy 4 specifically because Democracy 3 let players enact extreme policies without any felt consequence. Media reports are plain-text, journalistic-style stories (e.g., implying a specific citizen will suffer or die because of a policy) that are deliberately given **no mechanical effect** on the simulation — their only job is emotional/moral feedback, distinct from the mechanical Situations system (credit rating changes, factory closures, etc.) — [Cliffski's Blog: "Consequences in Democracy 4"](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2020/04/11/consequences-in-democracy-4/) (read in full).
- A media report in Democracy 4 is gated by conditional triggers on policy settings and outcome stats — e.g., a story about business closures only fires when labour laws are set to 65–100% intensity **and** GDP is still below 60% — so the warning text only appears when it is actually relevant, avoiding a false alarm during a boom — [Cliffski's Blog](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2020/04/11/consequences-in-democracy-4/) (read in full).
- Reports are populated with procedurally generated citizen names, which Harris frames as mimicking real media's preference for human-interest stories over abstract economic data — the same design habit the AI Lab game could use to make a warning feel diegetic rather than statistical — [Cliffski's Blog](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2020/04/11/consequences-in-democracy-4/) (read in full).
- ⚠️ (snippet-only) In Democracy 3/4, bad Situations (e.g., inner city riots) are shown to the player as an on-screen exclamation-mark icon — orange in Democracy 4, red in Democracy 2/3 — layered over the affected policy/stat on the main simulation map — [Democracy Wiki: Situations](https://democracygame.fandom.com/wiki/Situations) (page returned HTTP 402 on fetch; snippet only) and [Wikipedia: Democracy (video game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_(video_game)).
- Two Point Hospital's PA/Tannoy system delivers warning-style lines in a deadpan comedic voice while a problem is live in the hospital (e.g., a line telling patients not to slip on a puddle that hasn't been cleaned, or "Warning! Seismic activity detected"), performed by voice actor Jayne Webley — this is a diegetic audio layer riding on top of the mechanical simulation rather than a UI element — [Two Point Hospital Steam discussions](https://steamcommunity.com/app/535930/discussions/0/1761356057412293038/) and [TV Tropes: Funny/Two Point Hospital](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Funny/TwoPointHospital) (snippet-only; the dedicated wiki page on the Tannoy, [Two Point Hospital Wiki: Tannoy](https://two-point-hospital.fandom.com/wiki/Tannoy), returned HTTP 402 and could not be fetched).
- Prison Architect (the original) shows a large "RIOT UNDERWAY" banner across the screen once a riot starts, naming the number of participating inmates, and guards will "radio in" to alert the player as soon as they spot trouble — this is a reactive/in-progress alert rather than a pre-crisis warning — [Prison Architect Steam discussions via search synthesis](https://steamcommunity.com/app/233450/discussions/0/1729828401668290537) (snippet-only).
- ⚠️ (snippet-only) Prison Architect 2 (per Paradox's own Feature Highlight, referenced in search results but not independently fetched) adds a visible "Frustration Bar" that fills up before a riot, plus Admin staff who "pop up and warn" the player of brewing trouble, and a soundtrack that becomes more tense as danger rises — a genuine pre-crisis, escalating, multi-channel (visual + character + audio) warning stack. I could not fetch the Paradox Feature Highlight page directly to verify wording; this is inferred from the WebSearch synthesis of [Feature Highlight #10 - Riots](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/prison-architect-2/news/feature-highlight-riots).
- I did fetch the official Prison Architect (original) wiki's Riots page in full: it documents the escalation ladder from individual prisoner unhappiness → "irritated" → "angry" → property destruction/attacks on staff → 10 or more prisoners rioting simultaneously triggers prison-wide "riot status," and an uncontrolled long-lasting riot is an explicit Failure Condition in Sandbox mode. The page as fetched does **not** describe any dedicated warning UI (bar, icon, or advisor pop-up) preceding the riot in the original game — that pre-riot warning layer appears to be new to Prison Architect 2 — [Prison Architect Wiki: Riots](https://prisonarchitect.paradoxwikis.com/Riots) (read in full) and [Failure Conditions](https://prison-architect.fandom.com/wiki/Failure_Conditions) (title only, not fetched).

### Inferences
- Across these games, the "advisor" pattern splits into two sub-patterns worth distinguishing for the AI Lab design: (1) a **standing readout** the player must proactively check (Tropico's Almanac, Democracy's Situations panel) — good for a phone/feed object the player glances at — versus (2) a **pushed, in-character line** that interrupts or narrates (Two Point Hospital's PA, Prison Architect 2's Admin staff pop-up) — closer to what the brief's advisor "!"/"!!" head-markers are already doing.
- Democracy 4's split between mechanical Situations (which have real effects and escalate) and flavor-only media reports (which never do) is a reusable idea for Game Night: AI Lab — the feed phone could carry emotionally resonant, powerless flavor text, while the advisor "!"/"!!" markers and the event-card system carry the actual mechanical warning, keeping the numbers hidden in both places but for different reasons.

## News tickers and feeds as early warning (Plague Inc, Frostpunk, Cities: Skylines Chirper)

### Takeaway
Plague Inc.'s news ticker is the clearest verified example of a diegetic feed that foreshadows a specific mechanical consequence (a government countermeasure) before it hits, and it deliberately dilutes real warnings with jokes/random noise early on, then lets warning content crowd out the noise as the crisis deepens — a direct model for the planned "feed phone." Cities: Skylines' Chirper is, by contrast, explicitly closer to ambient flavor/noise than to an actionable warning system, based on the (snippet-level) sources found. Frostpunk's warning layer for discontent/hope is verified as a countdown-based ultimatum, but the specific pre-ultimatum "ticker" or notification wording could not be fetched in full.

### Cited Findings
- Plague Inc.'s News screen shows headlines, some of which affect the plague's spread/lethality and others which are purely cosmetic/random ("Tower of Pisa leaning the wrong way," "Fidget spinning considered war crime") — [Plague Inc. Wiki: News](https://plagueinc.wiki.gg/wiki/News) (read in full).
- As a plague progresses, plague-relevant headlines crowd out the random/joke ones, "to the point of them becoming the only news displayed" late-game — meaning the signal-to-noise ratio of the feed itself is a diegetic urgency cue: a feed dominated by on-topic warnings reads as "things are dire" even before the player checks any stat — [Plague Inc. Wiki: News](https://plagueinc.wiki.gg/wiki/News) (read in full).
- The News ticker foreshadows specific upcoming obstacles the player will have to play around, in a rough sequence: early detection ("New disease spotted in <country>," "Scientists concerned over <plague name>") → escalating countermeasures ("Airplanes use new sterilisation air filters," "<country> starts cure research") → targeted counters to the player's own strategy ("Scientists exploit [symptom] targeted") → endgame headlines ("There are no healthy people left in the world") — [Plague Inc. Wiki: News](https://plagueinc.wiki.gg/wiki/News) (read in full).
- This means the ticker's warnings are not generic flavor — they specifically preview a change to the rules the player is optimizing against (e.g., cure research starting, or a symptom becoming a research target), giving an experienced player a window to adapt before the mechanical effect (a real cure, a new travel restriction) actually lands. This directly matches the AI Lab brief's "warning first, event card next turn" structure.
- ⚠️ (snippet-only, not fetched in full — the two dedicated Frostpunk Events wiki fetch attempts both returned HTTP 402) Frostpunk's ultimatum mechanic: if Hope drops critically low or Discontent maxes out, the game triggers an ultimatum event giving the player roughly 2–3 days (sources vary between "2 days" and "2-3 days") to move the offending stat by about 15%, or by getting Discontent back under a 75% threshold even momentarily; failing this ends the settlement/game — [gamepressure.com: "What happens when Discontent or Hope reach critical values?"](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk/what-happens-when-discontent-or-hope-reach-critical-values/zfad7c) and multiple Steam Community discussion threads, e.g. [this thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/323190/discussions/0/677329263114903855/) (all snippet-level from WebSearch synthesis; the sourced numbers — "2 days" vs "2-3 days," "15%" — conflict slightly between the summarized sources and should be treated as approximate, not exact).
- ⚠️ (snippet-only) Cities: Skylines' Chirper is described in secondary sources as "the mascot" and in-game social-media parody (a reference to X/Twitter); Chirps "occasionally reflect upon the moods and opinions" of citizens and "can provide insights on problems that can be resolved," but the same sources frame it primarily as a cosmetic/comedic layer (its appearance is a coplayer customization option; it can be turned off entirely in Cities: Skylines 2) rather than a mechanically load-bearing warning system — [Cities: Skylines Wiki: Chirper](https://skylines.fandom.com/wiki/Chirper) (fetch attempt returned HTTP 402; this is WebSearch's synthesis only) and [GameSkinny: "How to Turn Chirper Off"](https://www.gameskinny.com/tips/cities-skylines-2-how-to-turn-chirper-off/) (title/snippet only).

### Inferences
- Plague Inc.'s "noise-to-signal ratio as urgency cue" is directly reusable for the planned feed phone: early game, the feed can carry mostly unrelated office/meme content; as alignment/misuse pressure rises, warning-flavored posts can organically increase their share of the feed without ever showing a number, which double-encodes urgency (more warnings, and a higher proportion of them).
- Chirper's fate (openly framed by its own developers as toggle-able noise, and treated by players as comic relief) is a useful cautionary example: a feed that never ties into consequences trains players to ignore it. If the feed phone is meant to be an early-warning surface (as the brief implies), it likely needs to behave more like Plague Inc.'s ticker (specific, escalating, tied to a real upcoming event) than like Chirper (ambient, decorative, dismissible without cost).

The ultimatum's timing and threshold numbers above are corroborated across multiple secondary/community sources but not a single authoritative primary source, and they conflict slightly (2 days vs 2–3 days).
- Could not fetch Cities: Skylines Chirper's dedicated wiki page (402) or its Paradox wiki equivalent, ChirpX (page not found at the URL tried) — so I cannot confirm from a primary source how mechanically connected individual Chirps are to actual city problems versus being randomly generated flavor text.

## Letter/alert stacks (RimWorld, Stellaris, Oxygen Not Included, Factorio)

### Takeaway
RimWorld's split between one-shot color-coded Letters and persistent, non-dismissible Alerts is the cleanest verified model for "a warning that can be dismissed vs. one that can't until you actually fix the underlying problem" — directly relevant to deciding whether the AI Lab's warning card should be swattable or should linger. Stellaris shows how a single problem can be represented as one persistent, trackable object (a Situation with a progress bar and discrete named stages) rather than a stream of separate messages, which is a strong alternative to a card-per-turn system. Factorio cleanly separates short-lived event alerts (something just happened) from condition-based warning icons (something is still wrong), which maps well onto "warning now vs. event card next turn."

### Cited Findings
- In RimWorld, most events generate a **Letter** — an envelope icon on the screen's right edge — and "the color of the envelope will suggest the severity of the event": blue = good, grey = neutral, yellow = bad, red = direct threat. Letters can be right-clicked to dismiss, or removed from the notification stack via a hotkey. Some events skip the letter system entirely and instead pause the game with a modal choice window (i.e., the full "event card" already interrupts play, no different from a letter) — [RimWorld Wiki: Events](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Events) (read in full).
- RimWorld distinguishes Letters from **Alerts**: Alerts "cannot be removed the same way as letters" (i.e., they are not one-shot dismissible; a persistent list of current Alerts is viewable via the L key) — meaning an Alert stays up for as long as its underlying condition (e.g., a colonist is starving) remains true, functionally different from a Letter's one-time, closeable notification — [RimWorld Wiki: Events](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Events) (read in full).
- RimWorld event/letter titles are often dynamic text mentioning specific locations, pawn names, faction names, or buffs/debuffs, and some explicitly show a countdown for time-based events — [RimWorld Wiki: Events](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Events) (read in full).
- In Stellaris, a "Situation" is a gradually-developing mechanic (as opposed to an instant event) tracked via a 0–100 progress bar in the Situation Log and the game's Outliner sidebar; it advances through discrete, named Stages (example given for a shortage situation: Minor Deficit → Major Deficit → Severe Deficit → Catastrophic Deficit), each stage both entering new mechanical penalties and (per the wiki) potentially firing its own narrative event — [Stellaris Wiki: Situations](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Situations) (read in full).
- Stellaris deliberately avoids showing the player a raw number for how bad a Situation is; the wiki's description implies urgency is instead communicated through the named stage label itself (e.g., "Severe" vs. "Catastrophic") plus visibly mounting side effects (reduced ship damage, lower research/output, lower stability) — a qualitative label plus a felt mechanical cost, not a number — [Stellaris Wiki: Situations](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Situations) (read in full).
- Stellaris lets the player pick different "approaches" to a Situation, each of which changes its monthly progress rate — i.e., the player has agency to slow, accelerate, or resolve the Situation before it reaches its worst stage, rather than only being able to watch a countdown — [Stellaris Wiki: Situations](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Situations) (read in full).
- Stellaris also surfaces an update to the Situation Log by making the log glow orange, an unread-notification-style cue layered on top of the progress bar — [Stellaris Wiki: Situations](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Situations) (read in full).
- Factorio has two visually and functionally distinct notification layers: (1) **Alerts**, which appear to the right of the quickbar, use a distinct icon per type (e.g., entity destroyed, turret out of ammo, train out of fuel), optionally play a sound, and — critically — are time-limited, lasting only 5 to 30 seconds depending on type (train_out_of_fuel = 5s, entity_destroyed = 30s) before disappearing regardless of whether the underlying problem is fixed; and (2) **Warning icons**, which appear directly on the affected entity/building and persist for as long as the underlying issue exists (e.g., low power, no ammo) — [Factorio Wiki: Alerts](https://wiki.factorio.com/Alerts) (read in full).
- Factorio alerts can be clicked to jump the camera to the offending entity in remote view, and hovering one enlarges its map icon and draws directional arrows at the screen edges pointing toward the source — a lightweight "diegetic waypointing" pattern — [Factorio Wiki: Alerts](https://wiki.factorio.com/Alerts) (read in full).
- Players can individually toggle or mute each of Factorio's ~16 alert types from a dedicated configuration panel above the minimap, i.e., alert-fatigue mitigation is handed directly to the player as an opt-out control — [Factorio Wiki: Alerts](https://wiki.factorio.com/Alerts) (read in full).
- ⚠️ (snippet-only; the Oxygen Not Included wiki pages for Red Alert and Notification Systems both returned HTTP 402 on fetch, and the WebSearch synthesis states the Notification Systems wiki page "appears to be a stub article with limited content") Oxygen Not Included has a "Red Alert" mode toggled via an exclamation-point icon next to the cycle-time controls; when active, the edges of the screen glow red and notifications appear highlighted in red, and it forces Duplicants to prioritize work over their own basic needs — this functions as a player-triggered, persistent, high-visibility, screen-wide state rather than a single popped-up message — [Oxygen Not Included Wiki: Red Alert](https://oxygennotincluded.fandom.com/wiki/Red_Alert) (snippet-only) and [Automated Notifier](https://oxygennotincluded.fandom.com/wiki/Automated_Notifier) (snippet-only: a buildable device that can be wired to sensors to fire a customizable notification when a condition is met — i.e., ONI lets players build their own condition-based warning triggers rather than relying solely on developer-authored ones).

### Inferences
- The RimWorld Letter/Alert split maps almost exactly onto the AI Lab brief's stated two-stage delivery: the initial diegetic warning (advisor "!"/"!!" head marker, feed-phone post) behaves like a RimWorld Alert — it should probably persist as long as the underlying hidden risk is elevated, not be a one-shot toast — while the resulting event card, once triggered, behaves like a RimWorld Letter/pop-up: a one-time, resolvable choice.
- Stellaris' single persistent "Situation" object (one thing that develops through named stages, rather than a new message every tick) is a strong alternative pattern to a stream of separate warning cards for the same underlying problem — it would let the AI Lab game show one evolving thread (e.g., in the situation log or on the advisor) that upgrades its urgency wording as the hidden score climbs, rather than spamming new individual warnings.
- Factorio's split between short "something just happened" alerts and persistent "something is still wrong" icons on the object itself is a good model for placing the "!" and "!!" markers over advisors' heads: "!" could behave like a Factorio warning icon (persists as long as the advisor's concern is live) while a triggered event card is closer to a one-off alert.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch Oxygen Not Included's Red Alert or Notification Systems wiki pages directly (HTTP 402 both times); the WebSearch synthesis itself flags the Notification Systems page as a likely stub, so I cannot report ONI's design in the same depth as RimWorld/Stellaris/Factorio, and I did not verify whether the Red Alert state has any pre-warning "graduated" step before it can be toggled.

## Escalation: warnings that become harder events on a deadline

### Takeaway
Frostpunk's ultimatum and Stellaris' staged Situations are the two clearest verified deadline/ladder mechanics: one is a hard countdown with a binary pass/fail outcome, the other is a graduated ladder with continuously visible mechanical cost at every stage. Democracy 4's escalation is softer and continuous (a Situation's icon and severity shift color/label as the underlying stat drifts) rather than a hard deadline. I found no primary-sourced material in this pass on CK3 event deadlines specifically (see Gaps).

### Cited Findings
- Frostpunk's ultimatum is a deadline mechanic layered directly on top of the Hope/Discontent stats: crossing a critical threshold on either stat triggers an ultimatum event with a short real/in-game-day countdown (sources describe this as roughly 2 to 3 days) during which the player must move the stat back by a described margin (approximately 15%, per one secondary source) or the settlement ends — [gamepressure.com](https://www.gamepressure.com/frostpunk/what-happens-when-discontent-or-hope-reach-critical-values/zfad7c) (snippet-only, not fetched in full — flagged above already).
- Stellaris' staged Situations (Minor → Major → Severe → Catastrophic Deficit, in the shortage example) are a graduated escalation model: each stage is both a warning about the next stage and a real, live mechanical penalty in its own right, so "ignoring the warning" doesn't just risk a future event — it is already costing the player something at every stage along the way — [Stellaris Wiki: Situations](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Situations) (read in full).
- ⚠️ (snippet-only) Democracy 3/4 represents a bad Situation as a persistent on-screen icon (an exclamation mark, orange in Democracy 4 / red in Democracy 2-3) overlaid on the relevant policy or stat; the icon's presence is itself the warning, and (per the earlier Cliff Harris blog post, read in full) the mechanical Situation is distinct from and can co-occur with narrative "media report" flavor text that has no mechanical effect — [Democracy Wiki: Situations](https://democracygame.fandom.com/wiki/Situations) (snippet only) plus [Cliffski's Blog](https://www.positech.co.uk/cliffsblog/2020/04/11/consequences-in-democracy-4/) (read in full).
- Prison Architect's riot escalation ladder (verified via the full wiki read) is: individual unhappiness → "irritated" → "angry" prisoners → property destruction/attacks → 10+ simultaneously rioting prisoners triggers prison-wide riot status → an uncontrolled, long-lasting riot is a Failure Condition — a ladder of worsening states with a hard failure state at the top, similar in shape to Frostpunk's ultimatum but reached through many small steps rather than one hard stat threshold — [Prison Architect Wiki: Riots](https://prisonarchitect.paradoxwikis.com/Riots) (read in full).

### Inferences
- The two verified escalation shapes — Frostpunk's "single hard threshold + short countdown + binary fail" versus Stellaris'/Prison Architect's "graduated ladder with real cost at every rung" — are usefully different models for the AI Lab's warning-to-event-card pipeline. The brief's stated design (a warning this turn, a full event card with costed choices next turn if ignored) is structurally closer to Stellaris' staged model (something already gradually worsening, made explicit) than to Frostpunk's hard countdown, since the "event card" is itself just the next stage of the same underlying problem, not a binary game-over check.

### Gaps
- I did not find or fetch primary-sourced material in this pass specifically on CK3 (Crusade Kings III) event deadlines, despite it being named in the brief's key questions — no search was run for it directly, and I ran out of budget before covering it; note this explicitly to the report writer as an uncovered example, rather than presenting a guess.
- The exact Frostpunk ultimatum timing/threshold figures are corroborated across several community/secondary sources but were never confirmed against Frostpunk's own wiki or a 11 bit studios developer statement (both attempted fetches of the Frostpunk Wiki returned HTTP 402).

## Alert fatigue and techniques to avoid warning noise

### Takeaway
General UX/design writing converges on the same handful of remedies for alert fatigue — prioritize by actionability, aggregate related alerts, let players mute/configure by type, and use restrained/tiered sensory design rather than uniform loud interruptions — and Factorio's own alert system (verified in full) is a working, shipped example of several of these techniques at once (typed icons, time-limited transient alerts vs. persistent condition icons, per-type mute controls). I was not able to fetch a full primary design article (GDC talk or Gamasutra/Game Developer piece) on this specific topic in this pass; the findings below rest on WebSearch's synthesis of secondary UX-writing sources, not a full read of any single article, so treat the general design principles below as ⚠️ lower-confidence/secondary compared to the game-specific, fully-read findings elsewhere in this note.

### Cited Findings
- ⚠️ (all snippet-only synthesis, no full article fetched) "Alert fatigue" is described in general UX writing as a state where an overwhelming number of low-priority, false-positive, or non-actionable alerts causes users to tune out all alerts, including important ones — [Meister: "Notification fatigue: the silent productivity killer"](https://www.meistertask.com/blog/notification-fatigue-the-productivity-killer-explained) and [NN/g: "Alert Fatigue in User Interfaces"](https://www.nngroup.com/videos/alert-fatigue-user-interfaces/).
- ⚠️ (snippet-only) Recommended mitigations in this literature include: prioritizing alerts by severity/impact and eliminating ones that don't require action or are redundant; using restrained visual indicators (a small dot or icon) instead of loud pop-ups for lower-priority information, reserving stronger interruption for genuinely urgent items; and, for game UI specifically, building sonic intensity gradually for recurring alerts rather than using a single startling sound repeatedly, and assigning distinct sonic character by priority tier (sharp/metallic for high priority, soft/rounded for low priority) — [The Designer's Field Guide / UX Collective, "How to design to alert users without overwhelming them"](https://uxdesign.cc/how-to-design-to-alert-users-without-overwhelming-them-4bb41feda9f0) and [SFX Engine: "Best Practices for Game UI Sounds"](https://sfxengine.com/blog/best-practices-for-game-ui-sounds) (both snippet-only; direct fetch of the first was blocked with HTTP 403).
- Factorio's shipped alert system is a concrete, fully-verified instance of several of these mitigation techniques operating together: type-specific icons so different problems are visually distinguishable at a glance, short automatic expiry (5–30s) for transient alerts so the notification tray doesn't pile up, a separate persistent-icon channel reserved for conditions that are still true, and a per-type mute/toggle panel that hands the player direct control over which alert types they want to see at all — [Factorio Wiki: Alerts](https://wiki.factorio.com/Alerts) (read in full).

### Inferences
- The single most game-tested anti-fatigue technique across the sources here is **separating "this just happened" (transient) from "this is still true" (persistent)** — Factorio does it explicitly (Alerts vs. warning icons), RimWorld does the same thing under different names (Letters vs. Alerts). For the AI Lab game, this suggests the advisor "!"/"!!" markers should be the persistent channel (as long as the hidden risk is live, the marker stays), while the warning-turns-into-event-card transition is the transient, one-shot "something just changed" channel.
- The generic UX advice to reserve strong interruption for actionable items supports the brief's existing plan (a quiet warning first, a costly choice-bearing card only once the risk has actually matured) — it is itself a two-tier priority system, which the secondary literature treats as good practice.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch a full primary article, developer blog post, or GDC talk transcript specifically about alert fatigue in game design in this pass — the one promising secondary article found via search (UX Collective / Designer's Field Guide) returned HTTP 403 on fetch, and no Gamasutra/Game Developer article surfaced in the two searches run. All alert-fatigue findings above beyond the Factorio case study should be treated as secondary-source synthesis rather than fully verified primary claims.

## Hinting at severity without showing hidden numbers

### Takeaway
Every game covered in this note that hides its underlying math still communicates severity through some qualitative channel — a named stage label (Stellaris), a color-coded envelope (RimWorld), a screen-wide color wash (ONI's red alert glow), a proportion-of-feed effect (Plague Inc.'s ticker), or in-world prose that dramatizes a consequence without stating a number (Democracy 4's media reports) — which together form a small toolbox directly transferable to the AI Lab's hard "no numbers" constraint.

### Cited Findings
- RimWorld's four-color Letter system (blue/grey/yellow/red) communicates severity with color alone, no stat shown — [RimWorld Wiki: Events](https://rimworldwiki.com/wiki/Events) (read in full).
- Stellaris' named Situation stages (e.g. "Severe Deficit," "Catastrophic Deficit") communicate escalating severity through wording alone, backed up by the player's own felt experience of mounting side effects (less output, lower stability) rather than a number on the stage itself — [Stellaris Wiki: Situations](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Situations) (read in full).
