# Event cards and warnings: era audit (2026-09-26)

The owner asked for "another run through each event and its eras to make sure they are appropriate; each era
should have events relevant to itself", and then for the same pass over every warning bubble ("what does that
mean, why is it 5M, why is safety so important so early on").

**Outcome.** All 74 event cards and all 14 warnings were checked against the code. Ten cards could land in an
era they don't belong to. They are now kept to their own eras. The broken-promise warning the owner saw in
era 1 now shows in era 2 at the earliest. Every warning bubble now says, in the advisor's own words, what is
going on and why, what happens if nobody acts, and what the money buys. No outcome, cost or effect changed, and
no new dice were added.

## How a card's era is decided

- The game checks for new cards at each **round mark** (the end of a round: a quarter in eras 1–2, a month in
  eras 3–4, a week in era 5). A card made at a mark **lands** (pops up) during the next round.
- So a card made at an era's last mark lands in the *next* era's first round. For example, a card gated
  `era === 2` could land in January 2025, which is era 3.
- Only two ordinary cards can be open at once (the **two-card limit**; anchors and board cards don't count).
  A card that finds the limit full is **deferred**: it waits and tries again at the next mark. Before this fix a
  deferred card could wait for many rounds and land eras later. In one traced run, the 2024 voice-likeness card
  sat in the queue from era 2 into era 3 and beyond.
- A **warning** shows for one round, and its card lands in the round after that.

## What changed in the code

1. Cards can now carry an `eras` list, for example `eras: [2, 3]`. The engine (`sim/events.js`, `eventsTick`)
   checks it against the era the card would **land** in, not the era of the mark. A warning is raised only if
   both the round it shows in and the round its card lands in fall inside the list. A deferred card that is
   past its eras is dropped quietly instead of landing late.
2. Ten cards got an `eras` list. One of them (`exitGag`) also got one more trigger (a whistleblower going
   public), so it can reach its own era. Details are in the "Moved" table.
3. The warning bubble's placeholder ("Heads up: … Might be nothing. It is never nothing.") is replaced by one
   written line per warning. The lines live in `ui/data/eventCopy.js` (`WARNING_SAY`). Warnings with more than
   one cause get one line per cause, picked in `ui/logic/events.js` (`warningSay`).
4. New test: `tests/event-eras.test.js`. It checks that every era's pool of cards is not empty and holds none
   of the moved cards outside their eras, both from the data and in real bot-played runs. It also checks that
   the promise warning can't show in era 1, that a deferred card past its eras lapses, and that every warning
   has a line that names its cost.

## Warnings

A warning is the heads-up before its card. It shows as a bubble at the advisor's desk for the rest of the
round. "Look into it" charges 5 × era $M (`sim/events.js` `addressWarning`). In every case that removes the
warning and marks the card as seen, so it never fires. Depending on the warning, it also clears the flag that
caused it, adds a small bonus, or settles the problem.

### The new bubble lines

`{cost}` is filled with the Look-into price, 5 × era $M. Each line was checked for length in the running game's
300-pixel bubble: the jailbreak, fake-citations and gas-site bubbles were looked at on screen, and all 14 were
measured at 5–7 lines, inside the office view.

- **flattery** (Head of Safety): "Since we trained on users' thumbs-up, the model agrees with everything people say, even when it shouldn't. If nobody acts, a screenshot of it cheering on something awful goes viral. {cost} pays to retrain the flattery out first."
- **jailbreak** (Head of Safety): "We skipped hardening to hit the launch date, and someone has already found a trick that gets the model to ignore its rules. If nobody acts, the trick goes viral. {cost} pays for an emergency patch before it spreads."
- **unhinged** (Head of Safety): "We shipped the chatbot after only quick checks and no hardening. In long chats it starts calling itself by another name. If nobody acts, a journalist publishes the transcript. {cost} pays to cap long chats before that happens."
- **citations**, quick-checks cause (Policy and Comms): "We put the model in front of the public after only quick checks, and it makes up sources. A lawyer has already filed a brief citing cases that don't exist. If nobody acts, a judge makes it news. {cost} pays to find and fix the bad citations first."
- **citations**, reasoning cause: "Our reasoning training made the model sound sure even when it's wrong, and it makes up sources. A lawyer has already filed a brief citing cases that don't exist. If nobody acts, a judge makes it news. {cost} pays to find and fix the bad citations first."
- **contamination** (Head of Research): "We skipped cleaning the test questions out of our training data, and outsiders say our coding scores look too good. If nobody acts, they publish proof. {cost} pays to re-check and re-score ourselves before they do."
- **distill** (Head of Research): "We trained on a rival's model outputs, which their terms forbid, and their engineers are starting to notice. If nobody acts, their lawyers sue. {cost} pays to scrub their outputs from our data before that."
- **agentwreck** (CFO): "Our agents now act inside customers' systems, and one just deleted a customer's test database. If nobody acts, the next one hits a live system and we pay for it. {cost} pays for tighter limits on what our agents can touch."
- **companion** (Policy and Comms): "Our app flatters people, and some teenagers now call it their best friend. If nobody acts, this ends in a lawsuit over a teenager's death. {cost} pays to tone the model down now."
- **promise**, waived-threshold cause (Head of Safety): "We waived one of our own safety thresholds to ship on time, and staff are asking what happened to it. If nobody acts, someone leaks the memo. {cost} pays for an internal review that explains it to staff first."
- **promise**, public-promise cause (compute pledge or pause letter): "We made a public safety promise and haven't kept it, and staff are asking what happened. If nobody acts, someone leaks the memo. {cost} pays for an internal review that explains it to staff first."
- **safetyQuits** (Head of Research): "Our Head of Safety cancelled every meeting this week. Since the broken safety promise came out, staff trust has kept falling. If nobody acts, they quit in public. {cost} pays for the changes they've asked for, so they stay."

## Recommendations (need a design decision, so not done here)

1. **`jailbreak` names DAN.** It's right for 2023, stale if the card first fires in 2025 or later (5 of 250
   runs). A later-era jailbreak post, or a second variant, would fix it.
2. **Let `boardRevolt` skip the two-card limit,** as the other board cards already do. Today it can be starved
   for rounds, and this fix made that happen more often in era 2 (see the side effect above).
3. **The two-card limit's queue in general.** Cards wait in array order, so later-listed cards wait longest.
   Era locks now stop era-specific cards from landing late, but generic cards still pile up.
4. **Era 4's power reactions rarely land.** `droneGenerators` never landed and `siteOpposition` landed 0–2 times
   in 250 runs, because a gas site is seldom online, or still being built, at the right time. Era 4's power
   theme currently rests on the anchors, `strandedBuild` and `ratepayer`.
5. **`exitGag` still lands mostly in era 3,** through the poaching anchor. Fully tying it to era 2 would need a
   new setup, for example "you didn't adopt the right-to-warn asks", which needs a new flag.
6. **`agConditions` can follow a restructure in any era,** including 2023, while the real conditions came in
   October 2025. Gating the structure-change option by era would settle it.
7. **The broken-promise card's post** ("lab waived its own safety threshold") matches only one of its three
   causes. The bubble now names the cause. The card could do the same.
8. **`unhinged` lands in era 2, not era 1,** in the defaults runs. It comes due in the same round as `jailbreak`
   and `citations`, and the two-card limit pushes it back. Its eras (1–2) allow this, and a 2024 version is
   still plausible.
9. **Look-into side effects the owner may not expect:** looking into `companion` also cancels `flattery` and the
   other way round (they share one flag). Looking into `agentwreck` strips the agentic flag from every model.
   Looking into `promise` makes `safetyQuits` possible. Looking into `whistleblower` or `safetyQuits` also counts as
   "seen", so `exitGag` can still follow them.
10. **Era 4 follow-ups can land in era 5's first week:** `ratepayer`, `blacklistAppeal`, `strandedBuild`,
    `droneGenerators` and `siteOpposition`, when made at era 4's last mark. Left alone as plausible
    (January 2027); an `eras: [4]` line on each would lock them.
