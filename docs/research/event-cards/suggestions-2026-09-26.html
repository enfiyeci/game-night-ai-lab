Decision 1 of 7

Where does a warning show up?

A warning is a small feed post, like "@tired_parent: it agrees with everything I say. everything.", with one button to act on it. The plan puts it in a toast under the HUD. You have since picked the feed phone on the CEO's desk.

Research. RimWorld and Factorio split one-shot notices from persistent alerts, so the player can tell them apart at a glance. Plague Inc.'s news ticker hints at trouble before it lands, and the hints crowd out the jokes as the danger grows. You have picked the in-office option every time something lives in the office during play.

Why C is costly: warnings are not linked to advisors in the data, so all 14 would need an owner assigned. It would also give the "!" marker a second meaning.

Decision 2 of 7

What does an event card look like?

The card is a new kind of screen, so this one gets a spread. Choose which to mock up; you can tick more than one. Each mockup would use a real card from the game, such as "Flattery blowup".

Research. Nearly every game surveyed keeps its early signs inside the world but pauses for a proper window when a decision is due. Game Dev Tycoon uses one pop-up for everything. Crusader Kings 3 stages the people involved inside its event window. Your compute dialogs, with the Team panel of opinions on the left, were accepted first time.

The bubble lines in C are my placeholders; in the game you would write them. Why C is costly: the bubble lines need writing or templating from the backer tags. Also, four of the eight tags (Product, Government, Security, Staff) have no desk in the office.

Decision 3 of 7

Can the player put a card aside?

The plan does not say. The shared dialog closes with Escape, so as things stand a closed card would be forgotten until it resolves itself at the end of the turn.

Research. Game Dev Tycoon lets less urgent notices wait in a sidebar, while a few types always pop up. Crusader Kings 3 lets events wait, but not forever.
Decision 4 of 7

What does the player see when a card is ignored?

Every card has a do-nothing choice (for "Flattery blowup" it is "Defend it"; for "Jailbreak goes viral" it is "Deny it"). The sim picks it silently when a card goes unanswered.

Research. Crusader Kings 3 auto-picks the first option after four in-game months, and no surveyed game shows the player that default in advance. Showing it would be our own idea. It turns the silent fallback into a choice the player can read.
Decision 5 of 7

What happens when two or three cards arrive at once?

Normally there are two at most; pooling can make it three. A President promise call is also a card and takes a slot.

Research. Crusader Kings 3 keeps a queue the player opens on demand ⚠️ (details from search summaries only). RimWorld and Factorio keep urgent things apart from everything else.
Decision 6 of 7

Should crisis cards look different?

Four cards are crises: "Weights stolen by a foreign state", "Weights copied to an outside server", "A whistleblower goes public" and "Your Head of Safety quits publicly". Answering one of them puts the board in crisis mode.

Research. RimWorld colours its letters by threat, from blue to red. Stellaris names its severity stages ("Severe", "Catastrophic") instead of showing a number. Oxygen Not Included turns the screen edge red in a red alert ⚠️ (search snippets only).
Decision 7 of 7

What do the advisors' "!" marks mean?

Today "!" means uneasy and "!!" means alarmed, based on each advisor's private reading of the lab. Nothing happens when the player clicks an advisor.

Research. The writing on alert fatigue (players tuning out a flood of alerts) says to give each channel one job ⚠️ (general writing reached only through search summaries). Cities: Skylines' Chirper feed became noise because it mixed jokes with real news ⚠️.

Build notes found on the way

  • A warning's text is not handed to the UI directly. The screen must read it back from the feed post the sim made (tagged warning).
  • setField replaces a whole field. Answering two cards must merge the answers first, or the second answer erases the first.
  • The "This turn" summary drops event results today; decision 4 adds them.
  • The president demand card and the President meeting are different systems. Keep them as separate screens.
  • The investors' card repeats its title as its post text ("Drop your strictest hard line to win enterprise deals" twice). Its post needs its own line.
