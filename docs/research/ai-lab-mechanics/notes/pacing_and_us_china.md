# Pacing the AI Frontier, and US–China AI Dynamics (through September 2026)

## Pause and slowdown proposals (FLI letter, CAIS statement, 2025–2026 calls, lab framework conditional pauses, September 2026 "pacing" remarks)

### Takeaway
Three waves of public pause/slowdown asks (March 2023, May 2023, October 2025) each drew large signatory lists but produced no enforced pause; by September 2026 the debate shifted from "stop entirely" to "pace" (slow but keep going), and even that softer ask from a sitting lab CEO collapsed into renewed competitive releases within about ten days.

### Cited Findings
- The Future of Life Institute's "Pause Giant AI Experiments: An Open Letter" (published March 2023) called on "all AI labs to immediately pause for at least 6 months the training of AI systems more powerful than GPT-4," citing risks including AI-generated propaganda and loss of control; it was signed by more than 30,000 people — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pause_Giant_AI_Experiments:_An_Open_Letter), [FLI](https://futureoflife.org/open-letter/pause-giant-ai-experiments/).
- No lab adopted the proposed six-month pause; as of the letter's six-month anniversary (September 2023) FLI itself was calling for regulation rather than claiming a pause had happened — [FLI, "As Six-Month Pause Letter Expires"](https://futureoflife.org/ai/six-month-letter-expires/), [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/next/2023/09/21/six-months-ago-experts-called-for-a-pause-to-ai-experiments-where-are-we-now).
- The Center for AI Safety's "Statement on AI Risk," released May 30, 2023, is a single sentence: "Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear war." It was signed by over 100 AI professors including Turing laureates Geoffrey Hinton and Yoshua Bengio, and by lab leaders Sam Altman (OpenAI) and Dario Amodei (Anthropic), plus Bill Gates — [CAIS statement](https://aistatement.com/), [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_on_AI_Extinction_Risk).
- In October 2025, FLI published a "Statement on Superintelligence" calling for "a prohibition on the development of superintelligence, not lifted before there is broad scientific consensus that it will be done safely and controllably, and strong public buy-in." Over 850 people signed, including Geoffrey Hinton, Yoshua Bengio, Steve Wozniak, Richard Branson, and — notably crossing usual political lines — Steve Bannon and Meghan Markle — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/10/22/800-petition-signatures-apple-steve-wozniak-and-virgin-richard-branson-superintelligence-race.html), [statement site](https://superintelligence-statement.org/), [Time](https://time.com/7327409/ai-agi-superintelligent-open-letter/).
- Anthropic published Responsible Scaling Policy (RSP) v3.0, effective February 24, 2026 (per Anthropic's own dated page) / announced around April 1, 2026 per secondary coverage — sources disagree on the exact effective date, see Gaps. The v3 policy **removed** a prior hard "if-then" commitment not to scale potentially dangerous models if competitors did so first, replacing it with "competitor-contingent commitments": mitigations Anthropic pursues regardless of competitors, plus a broader map of what mitigations the whole industry should adopt if others reciprocate — [Anthropic RSP v3.0](https://www.anthropic.com/responsible-scaling-policy/rsp-v3-0), [GovAI analysis](https://www.governance.ai/analysis/anthropics-rsp-v3-0-how-it-works-whats-changed-and-some-reflections), [Let's Data Science](https://letsdatascience.com/news/anthropic-revises-responsible-scaling-policy-v3-25732fab).
- Anthropic's stated rationale for loosening unilateral pause triggers: "From a societal perspective, what matters is the risk to the ecosystem as a whole. If one AI developer paused development to implement safety measures while others moved forward training and deploying AI systems without strong mitigations, that could result in a world that is less safe" — [GovAI](https://www.governance.ai/analysis/anthropics-rsp-v3-0-how-it-works-whats-changed-and-some-reflections).
- RSP v3 introduces "Frontier Safety Roadmaps" (lab-specific safety goals) and "Risk Reports" (quantified risk across deployed models) as its new accountability mechanism instead of a hard capability ceiling — [GovAI](https://www.governance.ai/analysis/anthropics-rsp-v3-0-how-it-works-whats-changed-and-some-reflections).
- On September 12, 2026, Anthropic CEO Dario Amodei published the essay "We Must Pace the Frontier," arguing the industry should deliberately slow the rate of capability gains (not stop) so safety work can catch up, citing recursive self-improvement risk and (per secondary summary) an "OpenAI–Hugging Face incident" as a proximate trigger — [darioamodei.com](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier), [Zvi Mowshowitz summary](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/we-must-pace-the-frontier).
- Amodei's three-step framework: (1) Anthropic unilaterally commits to give embedded, employee-level third-party evaluators (e.g., METR-type organizations) ongoing access — office space, tool permissions, and rights to publish findings without editorial control — to verify safety practices and assess alignment of training pipelines, not just finished models; (2) frontier AI companies in democratic countries coordinate on common safety standards and capability limits, with government backing so the coordination survives antitrust/legal challenge; (3) the US and democratic allies attempt safety-testing and capability-growth coordination with China — [darioamodei.com](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier), [AgentBreaking summary](https://agentbreaking.com/blog/amodei-pace-the-frontier-embedded-evaluators/).
- Amodei explicitly rejected unilateral pausing as strategically dangerous, warning that slowing down without international coordination risks ceding ground to China, and that "defecting from such an agreement by evading monitoring could radically shift the balance of global power" — [darioamodei.com via WebFetch summary](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier).
- Sam Altman (OpenAI) responded publicly, "I agree with Dario that we need to pace the frontier," and Elon Musk (xAI) said "Dario is right" — [hpcwire/AIwire](https://www.hpcwire.com/aiwire/2026/09/23/the-ai-race-is-moving-too-fast-its-leaders-warn-will-anyone-slow-down/).
- ⚠️ According to a Substack commentary piece (not a primary lab announcement), the pledge broke down fast: xAI released Grok 4.7 with aggressive pricing on September 21, 2026 (nine days after the essay); Anthropic — the lab whose CEO wrote the pledge — released Claude Opus 5.5 the next day (September 22, "in exactly ten days" per the piece's framing from the Sept 12 essay), and OpenAI countered with GPT-6 "Sol" and "Luna" models roughly 90 minutes later. The piece states a coordinated pricing structure "collapsed in under two days" once the first lab broke rank — [promptledproduct.substack.com](https://promptledproduct.substack.com/p/three-ai-ceos-promised-to-slow-down). This is a single secondary/opinion source; treat model names, exact hour, and the "90 minutes" detail as reported-but-not-independently-corroborated in this research pass (see Gaps).
- Independent commentary (Zvi Mowshowitz, a widely-cited AI policy blogger) and a critical response ("Who Should Pace the Frontier? Not Dario Amodei," Tech Policy Press) both treat the September 2026 essay as a real, closely-watched proposal but dispute whether Anthropic — as a commercially interested party — should be the one setting industry pacing norms — [thezvi.substack.com](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/we-must-pace-the-frontier), [Tech Policy Press](https://www.techpolicy.press/who-should-pace-the-frontier-not-dario-amodei/).

### Inferences
- The pattern across three pause/slowdown efforts (2023 pause letter → 2023 extinction statement → 2025 superintelligence statement → 2026 "pacing" essay) is escalating signatory breadth and declining ambition: from "stop for 6 months" to "prohibit until consensus" to "just slow the rate of improvement" — each step more achievable in principle but each also failing to bind actual lab behavior once proposed.
- Anthropic's own trajectory (RSP v3 loosening unilateral pause commitments in early-to-mid 2026, then Amodei publicly asking the industry to pace in September 2026) suggests the lab concluded unilateral restraint doesn't work and shifted toward asking for reciprocal/coordinated restraint instead — the same "someone worse will do it" logic that critiques of pause proposals raise (see race-theory section).
- The reported ~10-day collapse of the pacing pledge (if the secondary account holds up) would be a concrete, game-relevant data point: even a voluntary, publicly announced slowdown among the very CEOs who proposed it did not survive one lab's ordinary product release cycle.

### Gaps
- The exact effective/announcement date of Anthropic RSP v3 is inconsistently reported: Anthropic's own page is titled "Effective February 24, 2026" while secondary coverage (Let's Data Science, Winbuzzer) describes an April 1, 2026 publication. This research pass did not resolve the discrepancy — it may reflect a drafting/board-approval date vs. a later public release date.
- The September 2026 "pledge collapse" narrative (Grok 4.7, Claude Opus 5.5, GPT-6 Sol/Luna, the "90 minutes" detail) rests on one Substack analysis piece; it was not cross-checked against a wire-service or lab press release in this pass. Flag as ⚠️ single-source and not independently verified here.
- Could not verify whether any government (US, UK, EU, or Chinese) has formally responded to or endorsed Amodei's September 2026 pacing proposal.

## Theory of the race (race-to-top vs. race-to-bottom, prisoner's dilemma framing, compute thresholds in law, AI 2027 scenario work, critiques)

### Takeaway
The "if we don't, someone worse will" argument is the dominant justification labs give for not slowing down, and it is formalized in scenario work like AI 2027 (a warning) and its 2026 sequel AI 2040: Plan A (a prescriptive counter-scenario built around a verified US–China deal); meanwhile the one concrete compute-based legal threshold in US policy (10^26 FLOP reporting under Executive Order 14110) was rescinded in January 2025 and not replaced with an equivalent binding rule.

### Cited Findings
- Executive Order 14110 (Biden, October 2023) required developers of "dual-use foundation models" trained above 10^26 integer or floating-point operations to report training runs, model-weight security, and red-team safety testing results to the federal government — [Wikipedia, EO 14110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_14110).
- President Trump rescinded EO 14110 within hours of taking office on January 20, 2025; replacement orders (EO 14179 and later EO 14179/14365-series orders) did not reinstate an equivalent compute-based reporting requirement, leaving the status of any federal compute-reporting threshold uncertain through 2026 — [Wikipedia, EO 14110](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_14110), [CASRAI summary of EO 14179/14365](https://casrai.org/guides/trump-ai-executive-orders-eo-14179-eo-14365).
- At the Seoul AI Safety Summit (May 21–22, 2024), 27 countries plus the EU agreed to jointly develop shared "risk thresholds" for frontier AI — capability lines (e.g., ability to help create weapons of mass destruction, or to evade human oversight) that would trigger extra mitigations — but this was an agreement to develop thresholds, not a binding threshold itself — [DeepLearning.AI](https://www.deeplearning.ai/the-batch/ai-summit-in-seoul-achieves-safety-commitments-from-companies-and-governments/), [Wikipedia, AI Seoul Summit 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Seoul_Summit_2024).
- The February 2025 Paris AI Action Summit was intended to be where those risk thresholds ("red lines") got defined, but the outcome declaration lacked that substance; the UK declined to sign the Paris declaration, saying it didn't provide enough practical clarity on global governance — [Time](https://time.com/7221384/ai-regulation-takes-backseat-paris-summit/), [Freshfields](https://www.freshfields.com/en/our-thinking/blogs/technology-quotient/seoul-summit-sees-further-developments-in-international-governance-of-frontier-ai-102j8cj).
- AI 2027 (Daniel Kokotajlo and the AI Futures Project, released April 2025) is a detailed forecast/scenario in which fully autonomous AI agents surpass humans at "everything" around end-2027, leading in the scenario's branch endings to either human extinction or irreversible concentration of power — [80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/daniel-kokotajlo-ai-2040-plan-a/), [Wikipedia, AI Futures Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Futures_Project).
- The same team published a follow-up, "AI 2040: Plan A," in July 2026 — explicitly a normative recommendation, not a prediction — depicting the US and China reaching a verified deal to ban runaway "intelligence explosions," delaying safe/verified superintelligence to 2040 instead of the next few years, via a decade of alignment research, wide power-sharing, and reversibility — [80,000 Hours](https://80000hours.org/podcast/episodes/daniel-kokotajlo-ai-2040-plan-a/), [Kokotajlo on X](https://x.com/DKokotajlo/status/2075251618728292464), [Lawfare podcast](https://www.lawfaremedia.org/article/scaling-laws--daniel-kokotajlo-on-ai-2040--plan-a).
- Amodei's September 2026 essay explicitly frames unilateral pausing as strategically self-defeating relative to China, and frames "pacing" (industry-wide, then international, coordination) as the only viable middle path between "race flat out" and "unilaterally disarm" — [darioamodei.com](https://darioamodei.com/post/we-must-pace-the-frontier).

### Inferences
- The rescission of EO 14110's compute-reporting threshold, combined with the Paris summit's failure to define "red line" capability thresholds, means that as of September 2026 there is no binding, government-enforced compute threshold anywhere in US law — the only surviving thresholds are voluntary lab-internal ones (Anthropic's RSP capability/safety-level framework) and the "we agreed to agree" language from Seoul. This is a real, citable gap between rhetoric and enforceable law that a game could dramatize (players propose a threshold; it gets watered down or reversed at the next political transition).
- AI 2027 functions in public discourse as the "race-to-the-bottom, race wins, everyone loses" warning case, while AI 2040: Plan A functions as the "coordination is possible but requires a verified US–China deal" counter-scenario — together they map cleanly onto a game's two end-state archetypes (uncontrolled race vs. negotiated pacing).

### Gaps
- Could not find, within this pass, a dedicated rigorous critique of AI 2027's methodology (e.g., forecasting-community rebuttals disputing its timelines) beyond general awareness that such critiques exist; this would need a further targeted search citing named critics (e.g., from LessWrong, AI Impacts, or academic forecasters) to include with sources.
- Did not find confirmation of any jurisdiction (EU, China, or a US state) currently enforcing a binding numeric compute threshold in force as of September 2026; the EU AI Act's own compute-based "systemic risk" threshold (10^25 FLOP, from the EU AI Act, not directly searched in this pass) was not verified end-to-end and is flagged here rather than asserted.

## Coordination mechanisms (summits, AI Safety Institute network, treaty/verification proposals, agreed vs. proposed)

### Takeaway
Government coordination on frontier AI has moved from a single UK-convened summit (Bletchley, 2023) to a recurring, geographically rotating series (Seoul 2024, Paris 2025, India 2026) plus a standing International Network of AI Safety Institutes launched at Seoul — but binding verification mechanisms (compute tracking, on-chip attestation, a "CERN for AI" or "IAEA for AI") remain proposals in academic/think-tank literature, not agreed government commitments.

### Cited Findings
- The first AI Safety Summit was convened by the UK at Bletchley Park in November 2023 — [Wikipedia, AI Safety Summit 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Safety_Summit_2023).
- The second summit, the AI Seoul Summit, was held May 21–22, 2024, co-hosted by South Korea and the UK; participating companies signed the "Frontier AI Safety Commitments," and 27 countries plus the EU agreed to develop shared risk thresholds. The summit also launched the International Network of AI Safety Institutes, coordinated by the UK, linking national AI safety/security institutes for cross-border technical capability evaluations — [Wikipedia, AI Seoul Summit 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Seoul_Summit_2024), [CFG.eu explainer](https://cfg.eu/the-ai-safety-institute-network-who-what-and-how/), [CSIS](https://www.csis.org/analysis/ai-safety-institute-international-network-next-steps-and-recommendations).
- The third summit was held in Paris in February 2025 (rebranded the "AI Action Summit," reflecting a shift in framing from risk to opportunity/adoption); the US pushed back against regulation-heavy framing, and the UK declined to sign the closing declaration over lack of substance — [Time](https://time.com/7221384/ai-regulation-takes-backseat-paris-summit/).
- The fourth summit was the India AI Impact Summit 2026 (New Delhi), which shifted framing again, this time explicitly from "risk" to "impact." 91 countries and international organizations adopted the summit's declaration; the summit produced at least 15 deliverables, six "global AI casebooks" cataloguing over 170 deployed AI use cases, and over US$200 billion in announced AI-related investment commitments (infrastructure, foundation models, hardware, applications). India also joined the US-led "Pax Silica" initiative on semiconductor/electronics supply-chain resilience — [ORF](https://www.orfonline.org/expert-speak/from-dialogue-to-deployment-strategic-outcomes-of-the-india-ai-impact-summit-2026), [PIB India](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleasePage.aspx?PRID=2234343&reg=3&lang=1), [Wikipedia, India AI Impact Summit 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_AI_Impact_Summit_2026).
- Proposals for a "CERN for AI Safety" (a pooled, science-focused international lab for AI safety research, modeled on CERN) and for treaty-verification mechanisms (compute tracking, hardware-level/on-chip governance, audits overseen by an empowered International Network of AI Safety Institutes with authority to pause development) exist in the think-tank/academic literature (The Future Society, arXiv papers on hardware governance and nuclear-arms-control analogies) but have not been adopted as binding government commitments as of September 2026 — [The Future Society](https://thefuturesociety.org/international-ai-verification), [arXiv, hardware-level governance](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04712), [arXiv, verification methods](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.16074).
- IDAIS (International Dialogues on AI Safety), run by the Safe AI Forum, is a Track-2 (non-governmental, scientist-to-scientist) channel that has convened dialogues including in Shanghai (2025, bringing together Geoffrey Hinton, Andrew Yao, and other global AI scientists to discuss misalignment risk) and most recently London, April 17–19, 2026 — [Safe AI Forum blog](https://blog.biocomm.ai/2025/10/31/idais-international-dialogues-on-ai-safety/), [idais.ai dialogues page](https://idais.ai/dialogues/).

### Inferences
- The summit series shows a clear framing drift away from "risk"/safety (Bletchley, Seoul) toward "opportunity"/adoption and investment (Paris, India) as more governments and more commercial interests joined; a game could represent this as a dial the player/governments can push between "safety framing" and "growth framing," with consequences for whether coordination commitments have teeth.
- No coordination mechanism to date has produced an enforceable, verified cross-border commitment (e.g., mutual compute audits with consequences) — the concrete outputs are voluntary corporate pledges (Frontier AI Safety Commitments), a technical evaluation network (AISI network), and non-binding declarations. This is the realistic ceiling a game's "coordinate" path should reflect: coordination raises trust and information-sharing, but doesn't reliably stop a defector.

### Gaps
- Could not confirm within this pass whether the International Network of AI Safety Institutes includes China as a member, or whether China has ever participated in AISI technical evaluations; this materially affects how "real" a US–China verification regime could be in a game and should be checked further before writing that mechanic as historically grounded.
- Did not find a primary summit declaration text for India 2026 in this pass (only secondary analyses); the specific "15 deliverables" and investment figures are relayed from ORF/PIB secondary coverage rather than read from the declaration itself.

## Capability gap over time (DeepSeek R1 and successors; Qwen/Kimi/GLM/MiniMax; where China leads)

### Takeaway
DeepSeek R1 (January 2025) triggered a lasting narrowing of the US–China frontier-model gap; by mid-to-late 2026 credible estimates converge on roughly 3–9 months of US lead in raw frontier capability (down from an earlier 18–24 month estimate), while China has moved into leadership on cost-efficiency, open-weight release volume, and share of global model usage — figures that are themselves contested across sources.

### Cited Findings
- DeepSeek-R1 reached 1,400 points on the Chatbot Arena leaderboard in February 2025, trailing OpenAI's o1 by only about 0.4% — [search-synthesized from CSIS-cited data, via WebSearch result](https://theaicronicle.com/en/news/geopolitics/china-narrows-ai-gap-csis-report).
- By March 2026, Anthropic's Claude Opus 4.6 reportedly led the strongest Chinese model by only about 2.7% on the same class of benchmark — [as above, CSIS-cited via secondary aggregation](https://theaicronicle.com/en/news/geopolitics/china-narrows-ai-gap-csis-report).
- A CSIS report cited in coverage states the US–China capability gap, "once estimated at 18 to 24 months, has effectively shrunk to less than six months in critical domains like coding, mathematics, and reasoning" as of 2026 — [The AI Chronicle](https://theaicronicle.com/en/news/geopolitics/china-narrows-ai-gap-csis-report).
- DeepSeek's own V4 model documentation/commentary "acknowledges it trails state-of-the-art frontier models by approximately 3 to 6 months," a figure roughly consistent with an estimate of "a seven-month" US lead — [CFR](https://www.cfr.org/articles/deepseek-v4-signals-a-new-phase-in-the-u-s-china-ai-rivalry).
- A US-government-backed evaluation is reported to claim China's leading model lags the frontier by eight months, but critics say the analysis relied on partly private, unverifiable datasets and compared only to one US model — contested claim, both sides represented in coverage but the critique is from unnamed "critics" in secondary reporting — [Grafa](https://grafa.com/en/news/crypto/us-china-ai-gap-deepseek-debate).
- Google DeepMind CEO Demis Hassabis has been quoted characterizing China as "months" behind the US — [Vertu, secondary aggregation of Hassabis remarks](https://vertu.com/lifestyle/the-global-ai-race-why-china-is-just-months-behind-the-us-according-to-deepminds-ceo/); ⚠️ this is a secondary aggregator site, not the original interview transcript, so treat the exact wording as unverified.
- Where China is assessed to lead: cost/efficiency (Chinese labs are reported to be "tightening the gap with the US by just being more efficient" despite less compute and fewer training tokens, per Fortune, September 13, 2026) and open-weight release volume/global deployment share (see Open Weights section below) — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/13/china-us-ai-models-efficient/), [Fortune, Moonshot/Z.AI/DeepSeek cost](https://fortune.com/2026/07/26/china-moonshot-deepseek-zai-kimi-challenging-us-ai-cost/).
- As of July 2026, the top tier of Chinese frontier models includes DeepSeek, Qwen, Kimi, Doubao, GLM, and ERNIE; named current-generation models include Kimi K3, DeepSeek-V3 (and successors), Qwen 3.6, GLM 5.2, and MiniMax — [Digital Applied / Medium-sourced roundup](https://medium.com/coinmonks/chinas-top-ai-models-in-2026-deepseek-qwen-kimi-doubao-and-the-new-ai-race-08083866ac5a).

### Inferences
- The size of the US–China gap is genuinely contested rather than settled: figures in circulation as of September 2026 range from "a few months" to "eight months" to a claim the gap has "effectively" shrunk to under six months, and each figure comes from a different methodology (leaderboard scores, government-backed benchmark evaluation, lab self-assessment). A game's "gap" statistic should be presented as a fuzzy, disputed number rather than a fixed value the player can perfectly observe — that mirrors reality and creates a genuine information-uncertainty mechanic (do you race based on a gap estimate you can't fully trust?).
- Chinese labs' emphasized strengths (cost, efficiency-under-compute-constraint, open release) are consistent with the plausible effect of US export controls: constrained compute access pushed Chinese labs toward efficiency gains and toward open-weight distribution as a market/geopolitical strategy rather than closed frontier-only competition.

### Gaps
- Did not independently verify the underlying CSIS report's methodology (only saw it through a secondary "AI Chronicle" summary); flag the "18–24 months to under 6 months" claim as ⚠️ sourced through an aggregator, not the CSIS report itself.
- Could not confirm exact benchmark methodology behind the "Claude Opus 4.6 vs. strongest Chinese model, 2.7% gap" figure; it was relayed via a secondary aggregator rather than a primary benchmark leaderboard page.

## Policy levers (US export controls, AI Diffusion Rule, China's responses, AI Action Plan, China's governance rules)

### Takeaway
US chip export policy has swung repeatedly — tightening under Biden, briefly tightening then reversing under Trump on H20, and abandoning the broad three-tier "AI Diffusion Rule" for country-by-country deals — while China has escalated its own leverage (rare-earth export controls, an antitrust case against Nvidia, and direct orders to Chinese firms to stop buying Nvidia's China-market chip) and built out a fairly comprehensive domestic AI governance regime (content labeling, generative-AI rules) alongside its internationally-facing Global AI Governance Action Plan.

### Cited Findings
- The Biden administration published the "Framework for Artificial Intelligence Diffusion" (the AI Diffusion Rule) on January 15, 2025, dividing the world into three tiers of GPU export access — [USSC](https://www.ussc.edu.au/the-us-ai-diffusion-rule), [IISS](https://www.iiss.org/publications/strategic-comments/2025/12/the-us-pivot-on-regulating-ai-diffusion/).
- The Trump administration formally moved to rescind the AI Diffusion Rule on May 13, 2025, two days before it was due to take effect, replacing the global tiered framework with country-by-country negotiation and case-by-case licensing — [BIS press release](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-announces-rescission-biden-era-artificial-intelligence-diffusion-rule-strengthens), [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/bis-rescinds-its-ai-diffusion-rule-and-issues-compliance-guidance-regarding-advanced-computing-items).
- On the H20 chip specifically: the Trump administration extended export restrictions to cover Nvidia's H20 on April 15, 2025, citing China AI-capability concerns, then reversed course on July 17, 2025, with Commerce confirming Nvidia's H20 export license applications to China would be approved — [WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20250515-us-export-controls-on-ai-diffusion-officially-paused-but-new-guidance-elevates-risk-for-ai-related-exports) (dates as reported in aggregated search summary; see Gaps for a caveat on this specific pair of dates).
- On July 23, 2025, the White House released "Winning the AI Race: America's AI Action Plan," with 90+ policy actions across three pillars — accelerating AI innovation, building AI infrastructure, and leading in international AI diplomacy/security — alongside three executive orders, including one to streamline permitting for AI infrastructure (data centers, chip fabs) on federal land — [Wilson Sonsini](https://www.wsgr.com/en/insights/white-house-releases-americas-ai-action-plan.html), [White House PDF](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/07/Americas-AI-Action-Plan.pdf).
- China published its "Global AI Governance Action Plan" on July 26, 2025, via the Ministry of Foreign Affairs, presented by Premier Li Qiang at the World AI Conference; the 13-point plan calls for "unleash[ing] the potential of AI while ensuring its safety, reliability, controllability, and fairness," and backs establishing two new UN-framework mechanisms: an Independent International Scientific Panel on AI and a Global Dialogue on AI Governance — [PRC Ministry of Foreign Affairs, primary text](https://www.fmprc.gov.cn/mfa_eng/xw/zyxw/202507/t20250729_11679232.html).
- China's Cyberspace Administration domestically requires AI-generated content labeling under the "Measures for Labeling of AI-Generated Synthetic Content" (finalized March 14, 2025, effective September 1, 2025), plus the mandatory national standard GB 45438-2025, and separately layers this atop the 2023 Interim Measures for Generative AI Services — [Inside Privacy / CASRAI aggregation](https://casrai.org/guides/china-ai-regulation-explained).
- China's rare-earth export controls (expanded April 2025 to cover seven critical minerals, later broadened) have been used as explicit trade leverage: US Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick reportedly linked resumption of China's rare-earth shipments to the resumption of Nvidia GPU sales to China — [Yahoo Finance / secondary aggregation of the linkage](https://finance.yahoo.com/news/chinas-rare-earth-export-controls-093000716.html).
- China opened a formal antitrust investigation into Nvidia in December 2024 (State Administration for Market Regulation, alleging violation of conditions from a 2020 acquisition approval), and in September 2025 China's Cyberspace Administration directly instructed major Chinese firms (reported to include Alibaba and ByteDance) to halt purchases and testing of Nvidia's China-market "RTX Pro 6000D" chip — [TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/17/china-tells-its-tech-companies-they-cant-buy-ai-chips-from-nvidia/), [The Register](https://www.theregister.com/2025/09/15/china_nvidia_antitrust/), [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-09-17/china-tells-companies-to-stop-buying-nvidia-s-repurposed-ai-chip).

### Inferences
- US export-control policy has not been a stable "ratchet" tightening over time as sometimes assumed; it has oscillated (tighten H20 → reverse H20 → scrap the broad Diffusion Rule for bilateral deals) largely in response to trade-negotiation leverage (rare earths) as much as pure security logic — a game modeling "export controls" as a static dial would misrepresent this; it should be a lever that both governments can pull and reverse, tied to a linked trade-leverage resource (rare earths ↔ chip access).

### Takeaway
The single clearest bilateral government-to-government achievement is the November 2024 Biden–Xi agreement affirming that humans, not AI, should control nuclear-weapons-use decisions; below that governmental layer, most ongoing US–China AI contact happens through non-governmental Track 2 dialogues (IDAIS), while the dominant adversarial narrative in 2026 is a US intelligence-agency accusation that multiple named Chinese AI firms are running systematic "distillation" campaigns against US frontier models.

- On November 16, 2024, at the APEC summit in Lima, Peru, President Biden and President Xi agreed that "human control" should govern decisions to use nuclear weapons, with the White House stating "The two leaders affirmed the need to maintain human control over the decision to use nuclear weapons" — the first time China had made such a statement — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2024/11/17/biden-xi-agree-that-humans-not-ai-should-control-nuclear-arms.html), [NPR](https://www.npr.org/2024/11/16/nx-s1-5193893/xi-trump-biden-ai-export-controls-tariffs).

## Open weights as geopolitics (Chinese open-weight models, global ecosystem effects, US policy response)

### Takeaway
Chinese labs' open-weight releases — accelerating sharply after DeepSeek-R1 in January 2025 — have gone from a negligible share of global model usage to a claimed plurality/majority of tokens processed on at least one major routing platform by 2026, making "open weights" a genuine instrument of Chinese soft power and global AI-ecosystem influence, distinct from (and in some ways more consequential than) closed frontier-model competition.

### Cited Findings
- DeepSeek-R1's January 2025 release is repeatedly cited as the inflection point after which Chinese open-weight models went from negligible to majority share of global open-weight usage — [datagravity.dev](https://www.datagravity.dev/p/chinas-open-weight-takeover), [Inference Hub](https://inferencehub.org/blog/chinese-frontier-open-source-ai-models-2026/).
- By April 2026, eight of the top ten Chinese AI models were reported to be open-weight (downloadable, self-hostable, commercially usable under Apache 2.0 or MIT-style licenses) — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/chinese-ai-models-q2-2026-market-share-report).
- As of August 2026, open-weight releases span DeepSeek, GLM, Kimi K3, MiniMax, smaller Qwen and Ernie models, and Qwen3.8-Max's text weights — [Digital Applied](https://www.digitalapplied.com/blog/chinese-ai-models-q2-2026-market-share-report).
- Chinese-origin models' combined share of traffic on OpenRouter (a widely used third-party model-routing platform, used here as one proxy for real-world developer adoption rather than a comprehensive global measure) reportedly crossed 45% in April 2026, combining Xiaomi, Alibaba, MiniMax, Zhipu, DeepSeek, and StepFun — [Datagravity / Inference Hub secondary aggregation](https://www.datagravity.dev/p/chinas-open-weight-takeover).
- Fortune (September 13, 2026) reports Chinese labs are "tightening the gap with the US by just being more efficient," attributed to operating with less compute and fewer training tokens than US counterparts — consistent with export controls pushing efficiency-oriented, openly released model strategies — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/09/13/china-us-ai-models-efficient/).

### Inferences
- Open-weight release functions as a geopolitical strategy distinct from frontier-capability racing: even if China trails by several months on the absolute frontier (per the Capability Gap section), it can still shape the *global default stack* developers build on, by being first/cheapest/most-open — a form of influence a pure "who has the best model" framing misses, and a mechanic a game could represent as a separate "ecosystem share" track alongside "frontier capability."

## Transfer notes: how a 10–20 minute AI-lab management game could use this material

**(a) Representing race / pace / coordinate as a real choice, and why solo pausing is hard**

- Give the player's lab a single "development speed" dial (race / steady / pace / pause) each turn, but make **rival labs move independently and visibly** — at minimum: one or two rival Western labs and one fictionalized Chinese lab, each with their own dial the player cannot set. This directly mirrors the September 2026 real-world case: Amodei publicly proposed pacing, two rivals agreed in words, and within about ten days (per the one secondary source found — flag internally as dramatized/uncertain, but directionally realistic) a rival's ordinary release cycle broke the informal truce, and the pledging lab itself released next. The lesson to encode: **a public pledge with no enforcement mechanism decays roughly as fast as the next scheduled product launch.**
- Make "pause" or "pace" alone (no reciprocity) carry a real, visible cost: lost market share, lost investor confidence, and a rival's capability lead growing — while the safety benefit (reduced accident/incident risk) is real but probabilistic and delayed. This reproduces the actual asymmetry labs describe (Anthropic's own RSP v3 rationale: a unilateral pause by one developer while others don't reciprocate can leave the world *less* safe overall, not more, because the less-safety-conscious developer's models still ship).
- Model "coordination" as a separate, harder-to-reach state than either racing or unilateral pausing: it requires (i) the player's own credible commitment (costly signal, e.g., inviting a third-party evaluator — the real "embedded evaluators" mechanic from Amodei's essay), (ii) at least one rival also committing, and (iii) government backing that shields the coordination from being treated as anti-competitive collusion (the real "legal/antitrust" concern named in the essay). Coordination should be reachable but fragile — a single defection (by any actor, including a government) should partially or fully unwind it, echoing Bletchley→Seoul→Paris→India's drift from firm "risk" framing toward looser "opportunity" framing, and echoing the Paris summit's own outcome (UK refusing to sign a watered-down declaration).
- Represent "compute thresholds" and "red lines" as a mechanic the player/governments can *propose* (like Seoul's 27-country agreement to "jointly develop" thresholds) but which a later political event can simply rescind (like EO 14110's repeal on day one of a new administration) — teaching the player that coordination mechanisms are only as durable as the political coalition that created them.

**(b) A fictionalized Chinese frontier lab and the two governments as actors**

- Give the Chinese lab (a fictional amalgam — do not use real company or leader names) a distinct strategic profile grounded in the real pattern found here: **less top-end compute access, higher efficiency, more open-weight releases, faster low-cost deployment.** Its "win condition" should not simply be "close the gap" but include a separate "global ecosystem share" score (mirroring the real open-weight adoption surge) — a lab can "win" on ecosystem share even while trailing a few months on frontier benchmarks, which is a more interesting and more real trade-off than a single capability race-bar.
- Give the two governments (a fictionalized "Western Coalition" government and a fictionalized "Eastern" government — not literally the real US or China) their own moves, drawn from the real toolkit found in this research:
  - *Export controls*, which can tighten or reverse (mirroring the real H20 tighten→reverse→AI-Diffusion-Rule-scrapped sequence) — make this a lever that visibly costs the Western government's own chipmaker allies when it tightens, so the player feels the real domestic political pressure to reverse it.
  - *Domestic-substitute promotion* by the Eastern government (a fictionalized "Ascend-like" domestic chip line at roughly 60% of frontier chip performance) plus *leverage moves* like restricting a critical mineral export, and *procurement bans* on specific rival hardware — mirroring the real rare-earth controls and the September 2025 Nvidia-chip procurement halt.
  - *Open-weight drops* as a government-encouraged or at least government-tolerated lab strategy — a "soft power" move rather than a capability move.
  - *Summits*, as a recurring scheduled event (every few turns) where governments and labs can sign non-binding declarations that raise trust/coordination temporarily but decay if any actor defects — mirroring the real Bletchley→Seoul→Paris→India arc of raised-then-diluted ambition.
  - *Espionage / distillation accusations*, as a mechanic either government can invoke against the other's lab — realistic to model as raising tension and justifying export-control tightening, escalating (as in reality) from a single company's accusation to a joint intergovernmental accusation over a few turns, without necessarily being *provably true within the game* (mirroring the real one-sided, contested nature of the distillation accusations found here).
- At key decision points, a "policy advisor" character (fictional, an amalgam, not a real named official) should surface the real tensions found in this research rather than a simple good/bad framing: e.g., when the player is tempted to pause unilaterally, the advisor should say something like *"If we hold back, models keep shipping abroad tonight — Anthropic said the same thing about looser competitor-contingent commitments before their last policy revision. Slowing down alone doesn't slow the frontier down."* When the player is tempted to race flat out, the advisor should surface the real "18–24 months down to under 6" contested-gap framing: *"We don't actually know the gap for certain — estimates run from a few months to nearly a year, and the last three governments couldn't agree on where to set a red line before it got rescinded or diluted. Racing on an uncertain gap is still a bet."* When a summit is coming up, the advisor should flag the real pattern that summit ambition has been drifting from "risk" to "opportunity" framing, and ask the player whether to push for teeth or for investment headlines.
