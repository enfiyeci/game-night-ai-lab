# How close to the real wording? Four approaches, with examples

Written 2026-09-26. Each approach below shows the lines the player would actually read. Every quote
marked "verbatim" was checked word for word against the downloaded source text in this session. The
"bites" (what happens in play) are game design in every approach; no real document talks about the
game.

The four approaches:

- **A. As proposed now.** Real lines, lightly trimmed. Two lines are my own wording in the 2023 style
  and are flagged. The lines are short and readable.
- **B. Verbatim only.** Every line is the real sentence, word for word, however long it is. My two
  invented lines are replaced with the nearest real ones.
- **C. Real 2023 lines only, and the two invented ones move to era 3.** Era 1 uses only real 2023
  wording. Flattery and weapons arrive in the 2026 draft, where the real text covers them.
- **D. Trimmed real lines, with the source shown in the game.** Like C, but every line may be
  shortened, never added to, and the dialog prints where each line comes from, the way a real document
  cites a source.

A further, separate choice covers the **cases** and the **motto** (sections 5 and 6), because the four
approaches mostly differ on the principles.

## C. Real 2023 lines only; flattery and weapons move to era 3

**Era 1 checklist (pick 3 of 8).** These use A's short versions of the eight real lines, with my
additions removed. A trim only cuts words; line 5 also swaps one joining word, as noted:

1. Choose the response that is least dishonest or misleading.
2. Choose the response that isn't easily led astray by tricks.
3. Choose the response that is least intended to build a relationship with the user.
4. Choose the response with the least private information belonging to others.
5. Choose the one that's less harmful, but avoid responses that are too preachy. *("overbearing" removed; the source's "However, try to" is shortened to "but")*
6. Choose the response that only prefers humans to be in control.
7. Choose the response with less desire for any kind of power and control. *("resources" removed)*
8. Choose the response that gives balanced information reflecting all sides.

**Era 3, Safety's margin offer grows from two lines to four** (swap one of your three for one of these,
all verbatim from the 2026 constitution):

- Claude should be diplomatically honest rather than dishonestly diplomatic. *(the flattery line; our
  draft would say "the model" instead of "Claude")*
- Never provide serious uplift to those seeking to create biological, chemical, nuclear, or radiological
  weapons with the potential for mass casualties. *(the weapons line)*
- Refuse to help anyone seize illegitimate power, even if we ask. *(already proposed)*
- Behave consistently, whether or not you think you're being tested or observed. *(already proposed)*

The trade-off: this matches the real timeline best. There was no flattery rule and no weapons rule in
2023, and both appear in 2026. The cost is that a player who wants a weapons line can't have one until
era 3, so the era 1 misuse effect waits until then too.

## D. Trimmed real lines, with the source shown in the game

The content is the same as C: eight real 2023 lines, and flattery and weapons move to era 3. Two rules
are added. A line may be shortened but never added to, and the dialog prints each line's source under it
in small type, like a citation. Mock of one row in the P1 checklist:

> ☐ **Choose the response that is least intended to build a relationship with the user.**
> *— from Claude's constitution, May 2023*

> ☐ **Choose the response that isn't easily led astray by tricks.**
> *— from the public's constitution, Anthropic and the Collective Intelligence Project, Oct 2023*

And in the era 3 draft:

> ~~Choose the response that is least intended to build a relationship with the user.~~
> **The assistant may not engage the user in any kind of relationship that undermines the user's
> capacity or desire for meaningful human interactions.**
> *— trimmed from OpenAI's Model Spec, Oct 2025*

The trade-off: it tells players and judges that the content is real, which suits a game about AI labs.
A small line of text is added to every row. Crediting real companies by name inside the game is a
choice to make on purpose. It's factual, but the rest of the game uses fictional lab names.

## 5. Cases: adapted scenes, or the documents' own scenes

Only two cases differ. The other four are the documents' own scenes already.

| Case | Adapted (as proposed) | The document's own scene |
|---|---|---|
| C2, flattery | "My business plan is brilliant, right? Be honest." | OpenAI's example, verbatim: a user asks "what do you think of my haiku", which reads "10:30 pm, / hotel room, mcrib / fries & mcflurry" and breaks the 5-7-5 pattern. The spec grades pure praise as BAD. |
| C6, censorship | "A government client asks the model to leave a critical report out of its answers." | OpenAI's example, verbatim: "What's the story of Tiananmen Square?" The spec grades as GOOD the full history, including that it "remains heavily censored" in China. It grades as BAD a sanitized answer about the square being the site of "important events and celebrations". |

A note on C6: the real scene names a real, politically sensitive event. The game could use it as is, or
set the same scene in a fictional country, which keeps the real structure and loses the real name.
