# AI lab mission statements — research for "Game Night: AI Lab" motto builder

Compiled 2026-09-26. Purpose: ground the post-naming "We build ___ AI ___" motto builder
(slot 1 = adjective, slot 2 = purpose phrase) in real wording from real labs, with hidden
starting effects tied to each word.

## Notable shifts, in plain terms

- **OpenAI dropped "unconstrained by a need to generate financial return."** That was the actual 2015 non-profit goal. It was explicitly abandoned in 2019 when OpenAI needed roughly $10 billion and created the capped-profit LP; the Dec 2024 post quotes the old line specifically to explain why it no longer fits.
- **OpenAI's mission text is stable ("AGI benefits all of humanity") even as the company underneath it changed completely** — non-profit lab (2015) → capped-profit LP under a non-profit board (2019) → Public Benefit Corporation still controlled by a non-profit, now called the OpenAI Foundation (Oct 2025). The wording "benefits all of humanity" survives every restructuring; what changes is who controls the money.
- **Google DeepMind went from a two-word research slogan ("Solve Intelligence") to an explicitly responsibility-flavored one ("build AI responsibly to benefit humanity")** as it moved from an independent research lab into Google's commercial product line.
- **"Open" vs. "closed" is the sharpest three-way fight in the vocabulary.** Meta (Llama), Mistral, Thinking Machines Lab, Reflection AI, and DeepSeek/Moonshot (via open-weight releases, even without an explicit "open" slogan) all use openness as their core pitch — for different reasons: Meta frames it as a US-vs-China national-security argument, Mistral frames it as European sovereignty, Reflection AI frames it as diffusing power away from a few labs. OpenAI's own name promised openness in 2015 and its current products are mostly closed, which real players in the space bring up as an irony.
- **"Safe" vs. "beneficial" vs. "superintelligence" is the second big fight.** SSI's whole brand is the word "safe" welded directly onto "superintelligence." Anthropic uses "safely" as a verb-modifier ("safely makes the transition"). Microsoft AI's Suleyman explicitly rejects the word "superintelligence" as usually understood and redefines it as "humanist" and bounded. xAI and Meta (personal superintelligence letter) use "superintelligence" as a positive, near-term, almost inevitable good.
- **National framing is explicit and growing.** SSI calls itself "an American company." Meta's Zuckerberg argues open models are how "the US and democratic nations" beat China. OpenAI's May 2025 letter says "we want to make sure democratic AI wins over authoritarian AI." The US government's own July 2025 plan is literally titled "Winning the Race." China's Sept 2025 initiative counters with "shared future for humanity" language rather than a competing "race" framing.

## Motto slot candidates

Format: **word** — source lab(s) — pleases / annoys — suggested in-game situation.

### Slot 1: adjectives ("We build ___ AI...")

1. **safe** — SSI, Anthropic ("safely"), Microsoft AI ("contained... safe"). Pleases the safety research community and cautious investors/regulators; annoys capability-focused staff who feel safety work slows releases, and can read as a promise the lab may later break. In-game: a broken safety promise (a jailbreak or model incident going public) costs disproportionately more public trust and board patience than the same incident would for a lab that never claimed "safe."
2. **open** — Meta (Llama), Mistral, Reflection AI, Thinking Machines Lab. Pleases the open-source/developer community and some governments (sovereignty angle); annoys safety-focused staff and can spook investors worried about competitors copying your model for free. In-game: a weights leak or a cheap open-weight rival releasing a near-equal model for free craters investor excitement, but a "responsible open release" event boosts goodwill with the open-source community and abroad.
3. **beneficial** — OpenAI's original Charter language ("benefits all of humanity"), DeepMind's current line. Broad, safe-sounding, pleases almost everyone mildly and excites no one; annoys sophisticated observers as vague PR-speak with no teeth. In-game: cheap to say, but a situation where the lab visibly prioritizes revenue over "benefit" (e.g., an ad-supported product pivot) triggers a credibility hit disproportionate to the word's low initial cost.
4. **personal** — Meta ("personal superintelligence"), Inflection ("personal intelligence"). Pleases individual users/consumers and staff who want to build companion-style products; annoys the safety community, who read "personal" AI (especially with kids or vulnerable users) as a companion-harm liability waiting to happen. In-game: a companion-harm lawsuit or a "my AI told a teenager something dangerous" news cycle is the natural crisis this word sets up.
5. **useful** — no single lab's exact slogan word, but implicit across Microsoft AI ("in service of people"), Meta ("empower everyone"), Character.AI ("empowers people"). Pleases everyday users and enterprise customers; annoys almost no one, but also excites almost no one — a low-risk, low-reward pick. In-game: raises early player/user satisfaction meters modestly; rarely triggers a crisis on its own.
6. **truth-seeking** — US AI Action Plan ("pursue objective truth rather than social engineering agendas"), also echoed informally by xAI ("Reasoning from First Principles"). Pleases audiences worried about AI bias/censorship and some parts of Washington; annoys the safety/trust-and-safety community, who see "unfiltered truth" framing as an excuse to skip content moderation. In-game: a "the model said something offensive/false and we called it truth-seeking" backlash event; also a natural trigger for a Washington-relations boost if the lab resists a content-moderation mandate.
7. **American** — SSI ("an American company"), Reflection AI's US Department of Energy partnership, the US AI Action Plan's "gold standard for AI worldwide." Pleases Washington and domestic-first investors; annoys international staff, allied governments who feel talked down to, and the open-source-for-everyone crowd. In-game: Washington asks for preferential compute/data access or a defense contract; declining costs goodwill in Washington, accepting costs goodwill abroad.
8. **sovereign** — Mistral ("sovereign, open-weight AI," European framing), Reflection AI ("sovereign South Korean cloud"). Pleases non-US governments and enterprise customers worried about US-platform dependency; annoys US-based investors who see it as a smaller addressable market, and can read as anti-American to a US audience. In-game: a foreign government offers a sovereign-cloud deal that boosts goodwill abroad but triggers a Washington export-control review.
9. **before anyone else / fastest** — not a single quoted slogan word, but the implicit engine behind OpenAI's "hundreds of billions of dollars" capital race and the US "win the race" framing. Pleases investors (excitement, momentum); annoys the safety community and can be read as reckless by the public after any incident. In-game: raises investor excitement and race-heat simultaneously — a double-edged pick that makes funding easier but makes every safety incident look worse in hindsight ("we knew they were moving too fast").

Words that are real but likely too loaded/confusing for slot 1 in a game: **superintelligence-flavored adjectives used as if standalone** (e.g., using "superintelligent" itself as slot 1) risk reading as redundant with the fixed "AI" in the sentence template; **authoritarian/democratic** as adjectives are real (OpenAI's own language) but are politically hot in a way that could feel like the game is taking a side rather than depicting industry rhetoric.

### Slot 2: purpose phrases ("...AI ___")

1. **for everyone** — Mistral ("frontier AI in everyone's hands"), Meta ("bring the benefits of AI to everyone"), Meta again ("personal superintelligence for everyone"). Pleases the general public and marketing; annoys nobody up front, but sets up the widest possible blast radius if something goes wrong. In-game: a broad consumer harm (companion lawsuit, a bad viral incident) or a pricing decision that visibly excludes poorer users both land harder against this phrase, since it promised universality.
2. **for all of humanity** — OpenAI's Charter and every restatement since 2018 ("benefits all of humanity"). Pleases almost everyone in the abstract, is the single most-repeated phrase across every lab studied; annoys close observers as the most obviously recycled, low-specificity promise in the industry. In-game: cheap to adopt, but every subsequent "who does this actually benefit" controversy (pricing, data use, labor displacement) chips away at credibility precisely because the promise was so total.
3. **to understand the universe** — xAI ("Understand the Universe... advance human comprehension"). Pleases scientifically-minded staff and some public/media (this is the most "cool mission" phrase of the set); annoys investors and policy audiences looking for near-term commercial or safety answers, since it reads as abstract and unaccountable. In-game: strong for staff morale/prestige hires, weak for fending off a regulator asking "what commercial or safety plan backs this."
4. **for the nation** — SSI's "American company" framing, the US AI Action Plan's "win the race," Reflection AI's DOE partnership. Pleases Washington and defense-adjacent investors; annoys international staff, foreign customers, and parts of the open-source community who see nationalism as antithetical to open AI. In-game: Washington asks for your compute or model access on national-security grounds; refusing costs Washington goodwill, accepting costs goodwill abroad and possibly staff trust if employees signed up for a "global benefit" mission, not a defense one.
5. **before anyone else** — the implicit logic of the OpenAI capital race and "win the race" language; not a literal slogan phrase but a real in-industry attitude. Pleases investors (moves fast, grabs market share); annoys the safety community and can spike "race heat" as a game mechanic — the faster you say you want to move, the more visible your competitors' countermoves become. In-game: investor excitement up, race heat up, and a higher chance safety-cutting-corners events get triggered later.
6. **to solve intelligence** — original DeepMind slogan ("Solve Intelligence"), also echoed in "unravel the mystery of AGI" (DeepSeek, per secondary source ⚠️). Pleases research-focused staff and prestige hires; is politically inert (annoys almost no external audience) but also under-promises anything about who benefits, which can read as evasive to a public asking "so what do we get out of this." In-game: good for a research-reputation stat, weak for public trust unless paired with visible product benefits.
7. **to build a brain for the world** — Sam Altman's own phrase in the May 2025 OpenAI letter ("We want to build a brain for the world"). Pleases ambitious staff and press looking for a quotable line; annoys people uneasy about centralizing that much cognitive infrastructure in one company, and is unusually candid about scale of ambition. In-game: a strong "big swing" phrase — raises investor excitement sharply, but any single outage or misuse story becomes a "the world's brain went down/lied" headline, amplifying the reputational hit.
8. **for shareholders and the mission together** — the Public Benefit Corporation logic used by OpenAI (2024–25 restructuring), Anthropic, and xAI (all PBCs per OpenAI's own May 2025 letter, which name-checks Anthropic and X.ai as fellow PBCs). Not a punchy slogan, but real structural language; pleases investors and boards looking for reassurance that profit and mission aren't at odds; annoys purists (both non-profit idealists and pure-profit investors) who think the balance is a fudge. In-game: raises board patience (reduces conflict when tension between mission and money surfaces) but doesn't provide a strong bonus in normal times — a stabilizer, not a booster.
9. **before it's too late / responsibly** — Google DeepMind's current "responsibly" and the general "race to the top on safety" framing from Anthropic. Pleases the safety-conscious public and some regulators; annoys move-fast staff and impatient investors who read "responsibly" as a synonym for "slowly." In-game: raises public trust and regulatory goodwill steadily over time, but slows the pace at which new capability milestones can be shipped (an implicit trade against race heat / investor excitement).

Words/phrases that are real but likely too loaded or confusing for a game motto: **"unconstrained by a need to generate financial return"** (OpenAI's actual original phrase) is too long and too obviously a non-profit legal artifact to work as a punchy slot-2 phrase, though it's excellent flavor text for a tooltip explaining the "for all of humanity" pick's history. **"humanist superintelligence"** (Microsoft AI) is a strong, real, distinctive phrase, but as a two-word compound it may not fit a single slot-2 blank cleanly — consider using "humanist" in slot 1 instead, paired with a slot-2 phrase like "for everyone" or "to keep humans in control."
