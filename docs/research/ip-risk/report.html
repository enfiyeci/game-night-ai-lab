Low legal risk overall. Redraw two screens and pick a new name before the App Store.

Borrowing Game Dev Tycoon's ideas is legal everywhere we looked. What sits close to the line is the expression: the training-run dialog mirrors its Development Stage dialog piece by piece, and the top HUD copies its layout. The name "AI Lab Tycoon" is already used by four other AI-lab games, one of them launching on Steam on November 18, 2026. "Model Mogul" came out cleanest of the alternatives checked.

The changes that matter most

In order of importance. The first two are the only places where our screens follow Game Dev Tycoon's expression closely enough that a reviewer comparing screenshots would see it at once. The pictures of Game Dev Tycoon are layout sketches drawn from its official Steam screenshots, not copies of its art.

The training-run builder copies the Development Stage dialog

Same title pattern ("Training run · Stage 1" against "Development Stage 1"), project name under it, three vertical sliders with coloured fills, a three-colour "Time allocation" bar, one orange button, a "Team" panel on the left and a "Selected techniques" panel on the right (Game Dev Tycoon: "Staff" and "Selected Features"). Fix. Keep the rule (split effort three ways in each stage). Replace the three vertical sliders and the "Time allocation" bar with one control of our own, move techniques and team out of the two side panels, and retitle the dialog. The phone lane's new builder mockup (stage halves) is a good moment to do it, since it is being redrawn anyway.

The name: "AI Lab Tycoon" is taken

"Tycoon" on its own is safe: no one has been found enforcing it, and hundreds of "X Tycoon" games coexist. The problem is the whole phrase. Apple's guideline 2.3.7 asks for a "unique app name", and an older game using the same name in the same market usually has the stronger claim to it, through use alone, without any registration. A new name also means changing the title screen and the itch.io page.

Where the law draws the line

US courts and UK and EU courts agree on the basic split. Developers lost when they copied a lot of concrete expression at once; they won when what matched was rules, genre and ideas.

Free to copy

  • Rules, mechanics, win conditions (17 U.S.C. §102(b))
  • Genre structure and stock elements: scoreboards, coins, shops, health bars, menus
  • Ideas of progression, eras and themes
  • A program's functionality (EU: SAS v. World Programming)

Protected

  • Art, characters, animation, sound, text
  • The specific look of a screen: shapes, layout, colours, arrangement
  • Many copied details taken together, judged by "total concept and feel"
  • Trade dress, if buyers could think it is the same maker

Tetris v. Xio

D.N.J., May 30, 2012 · summary judgment for Tetris
Free
The rules of a falling-block line game.
Protected
Piece look and movement, the exact 10×20 board, ghost piece, next-piece preview, lock colour change, end animation.

The court objected to "wholesale copying of the Tetris look", and said a case is likely lost "if one has to squint" to tell two games apart.

Spry Fox v. LOLApps

W.D. Wash., September 18, 2012 · motion to dismiss denied, then settled
Free
The 6×6 grid, coins, a shop, the matching rules ("at best" thin protection).
Protected
The themed grass-to-hut ladder, the bear, the setting, near-paraphrased tutorial text.

Re-theming a specific progression ladder is not enough. Yeti Town left the App Store.

DaVinci v. Ziko

S.D. Tex., April 27, 2016 · win for the copier
Free
"Nearly identical" rules, roles, win conditions and character abilities.
Protected
Names, art and setting, which were not copied.

Copying the whole rule set of a game was legal because the expression was new.

Data East v. Epyx

9th Cir., 1988 · reversed for the copier
Free
Features that follow from the idea of a karate game.
Protected
Only Data East's own scoreboard and backgrounds, which differed.

Realistic subjects get thin protection. A management sim about real AI labs is closer to this than to Tetris.

Atari v. Philips

7th Cir., 1982 · injunction for Atari
Free
A maze-chase game; mazes, dots and scoreboards.
Protected
The fanciful PAC-MAN characters and their animation.

Characters carry the weight. Ours are all original.

UK, EU and Korea

Nova v. Mazooma (UK, 2007) · SAS v. WPL (EU, 2012) · King v. Avocado (Korea, 2019)
UK, EU
"A combination of ideas is still just an idea." Functionality is not expression.
Korea
The Supreme Court protected a copied combination of game components as a whole. ⚠️ From news reports; judgment not read.

Korea is the strictest market found. Worth knowing if the game launches there.

App Store rules and what has happened

Apple does not judge copyright. It relays complaints, and it can reject or remove for "any reason". The rules give a rights holder an easy hook if our screens look borrowed. Guideline text below was checked on Apple's page, "Last Updated: October 16, 2026".

4.1 Copycats"Don't simply copy the latest popular app … or make some minor changes to another app's name or UI."

4.1(c), added November 2025: no other developer's brand or product name in your app's name or icon.

2.3.7 Names"Choose a unique app name."

30 characters at most; no trademarked terms or popular app names in metadata; subtitles may not reference other apps.

5.2.1 Intellectual propertyNo "misleading, false, or copycat representations, names, or metadata."

The developer agreement makes us indemnify Apple against IP claims.

How a complaint works

A rights holder files Apple's App Store Content Dispute form (or the App Name Dispute form for a trademark). Apple contacts us and asks both sides to settle it directly. If it is not settled, Apple may remove the app; serious legal issues can mean removal at once. There is no published counter-notice step or timeline.

A name in App Store Connect is held from the moment an app record is created, so whoever creates the record first holds the name in that store, but a trademark owner can still challenge it.

What has actually happened

  • 2011–12Atari complained twice about Vector Tanks (a Battlezone look-alike: green wireframe tanks, sounds). Apple pulled it two days after the second notice.
  • 2012Yeti Town removed after Spry Fox's lawsuit survived dismissal and settled.
  • 2014Apple rejected new games with "Flappy" in the name for trying to "leverage a popular app". Same-gameplay clones with other names hit #1.
  • 2022Wordle clones using the name were purged within days. Look-alikes with other names stayed.
  • 2025A developer reports "Backrooms: Escape" was removed under 4.1 over a Steam game with a similar name, then restored after appeal. ⚠️ Forum account only.

How Greenheart behaves

Game Dev Tycoon is itself an openly inspired game. That cuts both ways: Greenheart knows the line, and it has never been found policing it.

Their own origin

Patrick Klug: they "were inspired by Game Dev Story on the iPhone (made by Kairosoft)" and wanted their "own spin on it". In 2013, answering "rip-off" charges, Greenheart said "our entire game is our own creation".

Enforcement found

None. No DMCA notice, App Store dispute, lawsuit or cease-and-desist against anyone, across their blog and news coverage. At least five game-making "Tycoon" apps (for example "Ultimate Game Dev Tycoon", "Game Developer Tycoon") sit beside theirs on the App Store. They publish their EULA under Creative Commons.

What they do own

GAME DEV TYCOON is a registered US trademark (no. 4345689, class 9, renewed 2023). No EU or UK filing turned up, though their site says "other countries". The game is on the App Store (since November 28, 2017) and was Apple's Game of the Day, so it competes directly with an iPhone release of ours.

Where a lawyer is worth paying for

Most of this does not need one. Three moments do, in this order.

Worth it now

Clear the new name

A trademark clearance search on the chosen name in the US and EU (games and software classes), before the App Store record and any marketing. Our checks cover the stores and public databases; a lawyer reads the registers properly and judges "confusingly similar". Ask for a flat-fee quote.

If you plan to grow it

Register the name

Filing the name as a trademark is what lets you use Apple's App Name Dispute form against a future copycat. Worth it if the game will be sold or marketed for years; skip it for a hobby release.

Only if it happens

Answer a complaint

If Apple forwards an IP complaint, talk to a games lawyer before replying. A reply under the developer agreement is a statement you are bound by, and you indemnify Apple. Not needed in advance.
