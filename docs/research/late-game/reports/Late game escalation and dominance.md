# Build the finale around one visible race

Eras 4 and 5 get bigger, rather than just longer, when the game does four things. It shows one race heading to one scheduled climax. It lets the player's own lead wake up the opposition. It keeps the number of choices per round the same while the stakes rise. And it stops as soon as the climax is decided. Every strategy game in the notes that avoids a dragging late game uses some of these moves: Stellaris's scheduled crises, Frostpunk's storm, Plague Inc.'s cure bar, Twilight Struggle's DEFCON track and instant wins, and Civilization VII's Ages. The games that drag (Terra Invicta's long mop-up, Frostpunk 2's rushed ending, Stellaris's look-alike crises) each fail at one of them. Game Night's finale does the opposite today. In era 5 a game sees only 0.3 to 1 release, no careful bot finishes a training run, and the calendar covers just four weeks of January 2027. That ends before the decisive months in every published scenario. AI 2027 puts a stolen model in February 2027, a superhuman coder in March and the decisive government vote in October, and its authors now say reality is moving at 70 to 90 percent of that speed (⚠️ from a summarizer read of the authors' update, not its full text). The scenarios describe the 2027-2030 race as a fight over a lead of a few months, held by a few labs and drawn steadily toward governments. So "world dominance" plays best as a lead across several fronts: compute, economy, state backing, security, and a hidden alignment front. Each front has its own backlash, and reaching a decisive lead is a position the player can hold and then decide what to do with, not an automatic win. More model releases also fit the period. By 2026 the three big labs were shipping a new model every one to three months, and Anthropic alone listed 15 models live at once (2026 dates are unverified beyond the labs' own pages). That supports frequent small checkpoint releases, each with a roughly 10-second leaderboard beat, and a full reveal only for flagships. The risks are the ones the games themselves document: too many systems on screen at once, a threat the player outgrows, outcomes locked by choices the player could not see, and a long tail after the result is already settled.

## Firaxis found more than half of Civilization VI players never finished a game

Civilization's developers gave the clearest diagnosis of why the late game drags. Ed Beach's team at Firaxis said late-game Civilization "is just not that fun to play" and named three causes. The first is **snowballing**: one empire gets so far ahead or behind that choices barely matter. The second is micromanagement, because a handful of cities early becomes dozens of cities and units late. The third is uneven civilization strength, since each civilization peaks at a different point in history. Their telling data point: **more than half of Civilization VI players never finished a campaign** ([Civ VII Dev Diary 1](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/news/dev-diary-1-ages/)). Their answer in Civilization VII was structural. They cut the game into three Ages of about 150-200 turns each. Each Age ends with a crisis that affects every player and gets stronger over time, and every player moves into the next Age at the same moment, carrying over "Legacy" points earned along four paths. Beach's claim is that "snowballing into an unstoppable juggernaut is no longer as obvious an outcome" ([Civ VII Dev Diary 1](https://civilization.2k.com/civ-vii/news/dev-diary-1-ages/); [CivFanatics](https://civfanatics.com/civ7/civ-vii-gameplay-mechanics/civilization-vii-ages/)). The team also attacked decision load directly. They counted clicks per action and packed four or more units into a single Commander piece, because late on "just playing through a turn becomes a 10-minute activity" (⚠️ Only the first 100,000 of about 113,000 characters of this page were read — [PC Games Insider interview](https://www.pcgamesinsider.biz/interviews-and-opinion/74659/civilization-vii-breaks-new-ground-how-a-fresh-ages-system-redefines-4x-strategy-games/)). The cost showed in reception. One review called the transitions a clumsy "soft reset" that wipes out much progress, while still crediting the Modern Age's victory clock with adding urgency ([Defector](https://defector.com/civilization-vii-takes-a-bumpy-road-toward-immortality)). Steam reviews sat at "Mixed", and a May 2026 update lets players keep one civilization for the whole campaign (⚠️ Wikipedia page; the citations behind these lines were not opened — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization_VII)). So the lesson splits in two: resetting the race at era boundaries works, but wiping out what the player built does not.

Soren Johnson, designer of Old World, gives the most compact set of tools for ending a long strategy game. He calls the ending "the hardest part of a 4X to design" (4X games are explore-expand-exploit-exterminate strategy games like Civilization). He uses two devices. Victory points act as "a dynamic timer" that the player races against. A "double victory" ends the game early once the player has twice the score of the nearest rival, so a player who has effectively won does not have to "push End Turn fifty more times." He also found that players accept AIs ganging up on the leader in multiplayer, but dislike AIs that automatically declare war as the human nears victory. So he wrote his AIs as characters with personalities and grudges, not as rubber bands (games that secretly help whoever is behind) ([Designer Notes #11](https://www.designer-notes.com/?p=1748)). Terra Invicta shows what happens without these tools. One review of an 80-plus-hour campaign said it "could stand to be about half that," and that the late game drags once the war is "basically won" (⚠️ The review page (inkl) returned HTTP 403, so these lines are from a search-result summary — [inkl](https://www.inkl.com/news/terra-invicta-review)). A long-time player logged a first win at about 150 hours, while alien fleets grew from 80-100 ships to over 400 ([Matchstick Eyes](https://www.matchstickeyes.com/2024/12/01/revisiting-terra-invicta-in-late-2024/)). Even after meeting the victory threshold, one player had to run a poorly explained final mission whose odds sat at 0% for months of building ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1176470/discussions/0/5595184948701611439/)). Players also report every human faction turning on the strongest player, which they found unfair because the game did not explain it ([Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1176470/discussions/0/3386168507493297443)).

Stellaris solves "I have already won" with a scheduled outside threat. After a galaxy-setting "Endgame year", the game rolls every five years for a crisis. The odds climb with time until a crisis is effectively certain 50 years in, and once one is chosen it arrives 200 to 1,000 days later. A strength dial from 0.25x to 25x scales the crisis fleets, with recommended settings that grow with galaxy size. Most importantly, **an active crisis blocks the game from ending**, even past the Victory Year ([Stellaris Wiki: Crisis](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Crisis); [Galaxy settings](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Galaxy_settings)). Some threats grow with the player's footprint. The Unbidden send reinforcements faster as they hold more starbases (every 1,500 days with none, down to every 150 days with ten or more). The Prethoryn send extra waves at 20% and 40% galaxy control. In both cases the way to slow them is to hit their infrastructure ([Extradimensional Invaders](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Extradimensional_Invaders); [Prethoryn Scourge](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Prethoryn_Scourge)). The threat also gives everyone else a shared job. All empires get damage bonuses of 20% to 50% against it through resolutions and perks, and sleeping Fallen Empires can wake as Guardians to fight it (⚠️ the Fallen Empire wiki page was read only to its first 100,000 of about 386,000 characters, per the note's coverage statement — [Fallen Empire](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Fallen_empire)). The failure mode is sameness, not timing. One Steam player found the endgame crises "practically identical" and beaten by "spamming doomstacks", and another said that after a few wins a crisis becomes "a little speedbump on the road" ([Steam discussion](https://steamcommunity.com/app/281990/discussions/0/1693797713969209217)). The developers' stated reason for crises, keeping very late games able to upset a player who feels the game is won, survives only as a paraphrase (⚠️ The diary page itself returned only a browser-validation screen or HTTP 500; this is a search-result paraphrase — [Dev Diary #30](https://admin-forum.paradoxplaza.com/forum/developer-diary/stellaris-dev-diary-30-late-game-crises.921629/page-12)).

Game Night has already measured its own version of the snowball. Before the compute-race change, the balanced bot was first in 93% of rounds and hit the score cap of 100 by round 10 on average. With the rival-compute design that dropped to 79%, and the spec names stronger rival power in era 4 as the next fix (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-26-compute-race-design.md`, sections 1 and 4). Firaxis's diagnosis therefore applies directly. In a 30-minute game the late-era tail is short in absolute terms, but it is empty for the same reason Civilization's is: the leader's choices stop mattering.

## A storm on the map beats a hundred systems

The four narrative and board games all make the end visible early, using a named thing the player can count. Plague Inc.'s cure research is a clock the player's own success switches on: it starts "shortly after" the plague is noticed. The game then makes it loud, with pop-up warnings at **25, 50, 75, 95 and 100 percent** and tips tied to those marks (use the Drug Resistance upgrades at 75%, take extra actions at 95%). The bar can also move backwards, even after reaching 100%, so the last stretch stays active ([Plague Inc. wiki, Cure](https://plagueinc.fandom.com/wiki/Cure)). Governments respond along a ladder of numeric thresholds that fans extracted from the game files. Airports close first (priority 13 local, 20 global), then land borders (23/30), then ports (28/35). A society chain escalates from curfews to national emergency or martial law to shutting down the internet ([List of government actions](https://plagueinc.fandom.com/wiki/List_of_government_actions)). The designer, James Vaughan, said the reverse-role Cure mode needed a clear end event: "We need to draw it to a conclusion" (⚠️ WebFetch digest — [Kotaku](https://kotaku.com/plague-inc-now-has-a-new-mode-in-which-you-try-to-stop-1845647409)).

Frostpunk's main scenario is the cleanest example of one scheduled climax. It runs 48 days in three acts: survival, a split in the city, then the Great Storm. The storm timer starts when the player learns of the settlement Winterhome, and the storm hits no later than day 43. It is announced by three waves of refugees, a named checklist of preparations ("Final days: Wait for the storm to hit"), and a grey fog creeping across the world map. Near the end there is even a scripted false relief ([A New Home](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/A_New_Home); [The Great Storm](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/The_Great_Storm); [The Storm arc](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/The_Storm_(Arc))). Lead designer Kuba Stokalski said they tried letting the player keep building after the storm and found it "anti-climatic", so "we knew we wanted to end this the way a novel ends" ([PC Gamer interview](https://www.pcgamer.com/frostpunk-developers-on-hope-misery-and-the-ultimately-terrifying-book-of-laws/)). Frostpunk's middle game escalates through its Book of Laws, an ordered ladder built on what the developers called "creeping normality": "Every law should be a little bit more than the previous one" ([PC Gamer interview](https://www.pcgamer.com/frostpunk-developers-on-hope-misery-and-the-ultimately-terrifying-book-of-laws/)). The last rung of either path replaces the Hope meter with Obedience or Devotion fixed at 100%. That cancels the pressure at a moral price, and the ending text notes it: "We have crossed the line" ([Order](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Order); [Faith](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/Faith); [A New Home](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/A_New_Home)). Two warnings come with it. Critics found the campaign "unexpectedly easy" and noted that the game never judged their choices ([Wikipedia, Frostpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frostpunk)). And in the endless mode, storms go from "a potential end to your city" to "a minor annoyance" as the city's capacity grows ([The Great Storm](https://frostpunk.fandom.com/wiki/The_Great_Storm)).

Twilight Struggle shows how to raise the stakes without adding rules. Its ten turns hold a fixed six or seven card plays each. New Mid War and Late War cards are shuffled into the same draw pile at turns 4 and 8, so late events mix with leftover early ones ([GMT rulebook, 4.1-4.5](https://www.gmtgames.com/living_rules/TS_Rules_Deluxe.pdf)). Several instant-win conditions are always visible. Reaching 20 victory points wins at once. Controlling Europe wins when Europe is scored. And DEFCON (the nuclear-readiness track) works as a ratchet: every coup in a key country lowers it, each level shuts more regions to coups, and the player whose action brings it to 1 loses immediately. At the same time, each player must spend a quota of military actions every turn, so the board tightens around both sides ([GMT rulebook, 8.1-8.2, 10.2-10.3](https://www.gmtgames.com/living_rules/TS_Rules_Deluxe.pdf)). Scoring cards must be played in the turn they are drawn, so every hand holds a deadline the opponent can only guess at. The designers wrote that "the number one constraint on the design was time," and they cut ideas that did not serve "fast play, simple rules" ([GMT rulebook, Designer's Notes](https://www.gmtgames.com/living_rules/TS_Rules_Deluxe.pdf); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_Struggle)).

The two political narrative games show the cost of hidden logic and of too many systems. Suzerain builds its ending from a few big gates (a war with the neighbouring power Rumburg, a party primary, a final speech before the 1957 election). Each gate reads flags set much earlier: the assassination at the final speech can be avoided only by an earlier item or by having set up a secret police (⚠️ first 9,000 of 14,290 characters of the page source read, then the Rizia portion from its heading — [Suzerain Wiki, Endings](https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Endings)). The developers' principle is that "no single action topples the country"; consequences come from "a string of choices" (⚠️ WebFetch digest — [Articy showcase](https://www.articy.com/en/showcase/suzerain/)). The better pattern is the expansion's explicit warning at the start of Turn 10 that a coup is coming (⚠️ page partly read, and the Rizia section is labelled WIP, with several entries "[TBA]" — [Suzerain Wiki, Endings](https://suzerain.fandom.com/wiki/Endings)). Frostpunk 2 replaced the single storm with a faction civil war inside a 100-seat council ([Wikipedia, Frostpunk 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frostpunk_2)). Reviewers found "a bit of an information overload at times" (⚠️ WebFetch digest — [Shacknews](https://shacknews.com/article/141448/frostpunk-2-review-score)), and said the story "wraps up in an unsatisfying flurry", with bad choices invisible until "the resulting snowball of destruction" (⚠️ WebFetch digest — [Wolf's Gaming Blog](https://wolfsgamingblog.com/2024/09/21/frostpunk-2-is-a-bold-sequel-that-doesnt-always-work-review/)). That is the closest cautionary example for a "fight for the world" finale built from many systems at once.

## Published scenarios make 2027 a fight over a lead of two to three months

AI 2027 (Kokotajlo, Alexander, Larsen, Lifland and Dean, April 2025) is the most detailed month-by-month race and the closest fit for a game calendar. In it, China's Communist Party nationalizes its AI research in mid-2026 into a lab-led collective at a secure zone. China steals the leading US lab's model weights (the trained numbers that make up the model) in February 2027, in under two hours. A superhuman coding agent arrives in March 2027, and by June there are hundreds of thousands of AI copies doing research ten times faster than before. In July the leader releases a cheap model and announces AGI; the public disapproves of the lab 60 to 25. In August the White House drafts plans to seize trailing labs' datacenters under the Defense Production Act (a 1950 law that lets the US government prioritize or compel production). That would lift the leader from 20% to 50% of world AI compute, against China's 10%. In September a misaligned superhuman AI researcher is caught but not stopped, because the rival is "two months behind." In October a leak forces a joint government-company committee to vote, and it splits **6 to 4** ([AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/)). The two endings branch on that vote. In the race ending the misaligned AI designs its successor, becomes a trusted government adviser by December 2027, and the world ends in AI takeover by 2030. In the slowdown ending the US consolidates its labs, builds a transparent model line, and reaches a US-led world order by 2030 ([race](https://ai-2027.com/race); [slowdown](https://ai-2027.com/slowdown)). The authors have since stretched their timelines. Daniel Kokotajlo's median date for an automated coder is now November 2027, and the team says reality is tracking "70-90 percent as fast as AI 2027" (⚠️ Summarizer read, not full text; a later Q2.5 change of Daniel's median from mid-2028 to Nov 2027 is a large move I could not reconcile from the extract — [Q2.5 2026 update](https://blog.aifutures.org/i/211256047/daniel)). An unofficial tracker finds "no confirmed 10^28 FLOP run" as of 2026 (FLOP counts the total computation used to train a model), which leaves the scenario's compute milestones months behind (⚠️ Summarizer read of an unofficial tracker — [AI 2027 tracker](https://ai2027-tracker.com/predictions/training-compute/)).

Leopold Aschenbrenner's *Situational Awareness* (June 2024) runs on a similar clock with a more hawkish shape. His sequence is AGI by 2027, "some form of government AGI project" by 2027-28, an intelligence explosion underway by 2028-29, and superintelligence by 2030. He expects the project to look less like literal nationalization and more like a defense-industry joint venture between cloud providers, labs and government ([IV. The Project](https://situational-awareness.ai/the-project/)). His largest-cluster ladder is about 1 GW (gigawatt) around 2026, about 10 GW and hundreds of billions of dollars around 2028, and about 100 GW and over $1 trillion around 2030, with power as the binding limit ([IIIa](https://situational-awareness.ai/racing-to-the-trillion-dollar-cluster/)). He argues "a lead of mere months could be decisive" in a fast intelligence explosion (⚠️ Read the introduction and the military-advantage sections by text search; the China-capability and "race vs. safety" sections were not read in full — [IIId](https://situational-awareness.ai/the-free-world-must-prevail/)). Epoch AI, the most conservative of the compute forecasters, finds a training run of about 2x10^29 FLOP feasible by 2030, with power binding first (⚠️ Read through a summarizing fetch that covered only the first 100,000 of 224,970 characters — [Epoch](https://epoch.ai/blog/can-ai-scaling-continue-through-2030)). Epoch's data put the largest single datacenter at about **760,000 H100-equivalent chips in May 2026** (an H100-equivalent counts chips in units of Nvidia's 2023 flagship). Their projections reach 1.1 million in July 2026, 2.0 million in October 2027 and 3.7 million in January 2028 (⚠️ Summarizer read — [Epoch data insight](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/largest-data-center-compute)). The slow-world counterweight is "AI as Normal Technology" by Narayanan and Kapoor, which says transformative effects play out "on the timescale of decades" (⚠️ Summarizer read of only the first 100,000 of 186,115 characters; the later policy section was not read — [Knight Institute](https://knightcolumbia.org/content/ai-as-normal-technology)). The forecasting crowd sits in between: Metaculus's community median for AGI moved from about 2070 in 2020 to about 2033 in early 2026, as reported by RAND (⚠️ not the full 228K-character document — [RAND](https://www.rand.org/t/RRA4692-1)).

The AI Futures team's follow-up, *AI 2040*, turns the branches into a menu with numbers. That is useful because it prices "safety" in the same currency as "dominance": months of lead. Plan A is a verified slowdown. Plans B and C+ burn at least three or two months of lead through sabotage or domestic regulation, Plan C burns at least one month, and Plan D races while spending at least 1% of resources on safety. The median time from automated coder to takeover-capable AI runs from 6 years under Plan A down to 1.02 years under Plan D, and the chance of avoiding misaligned takeover runs from 72% down to 25%. Even a deal decays: about 12% a year early on, 37% within five years ([Comparing plans](https://ai-2040.com/supplements/comparing-possible-plans); [Deal decline](https://ai-2040.com/supplements/deal-decline)).

Real 2025-2026 events supply the first rungs of a state-control ladder, though nothing near a takeover. The only formal "Manhattan Project for AGI" proposal is the US-China Economic and Security Review Commission's November 2024 recommendation to Congress, which was not enacted (⚠️ I read the chapter's abstract, key findings and the recommendation text in full from a local text extraction, and searched the rest by keyword; I did not read the whole ~6,000-line chapter — [USCC Ch. 3](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/Chapter_3--U.S.-China_Competition_in_Emerging_Technologies.pdf)). The sharpest real event involves Anthropic, which develops the model writing this report. It is reported here only from the cited coverage. In February 2026 the Defense Secretary reportedly threatened to invoke the Defense Production Act unless Anthropic accepted Pentagon contract terms by a deadline (⚠️ this is Volokh's excerpt of the Lawfare piece, not the Lawfare article itself — [Volokh Conspiracy](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/02/25/anthropic-the-pentagon-and-the-defense-production-act/)). The Act was not invoked. A "supply chain risk" designation was applied instead, and a trial judge called it unlawful retaliation in August 2026 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/28/anthropic-gets-its-first-court-win-over-the-pentagons-supply-chain-risk-label/)). A divided appeals panel upheld the designation in September (⚠️ search-snippet only (CNBC page not opened); corroborated by Washington Post and ABC headlines in the same search, also not opened — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/25/pentagon-anthropic-ai-risk-appeals-court.html)). US-China relations follow a similar transactional pattern. In January 2026 the US opened H200 chip sales to China case by case, with a 25% fee and a cap at half of US sales, while Beijing slow-walked purchases to favour Huawei ([CNAS](https://www.cnas.org/publications/cnas-insights/cnas-insights-unpacking-the-h200-export-policy)). The May 2026 Beijing summit produced no AI agreement ([Time](https://time.com/article/2026/05/15/trump-xi-us-china-summit-ai-semiconductor-chips/)). The September 2026 Washington summit created a "Super Intelligence Dialogue" and an undefined incident channel, but no limits (⚠️ I did not reach the fact sheet itself — [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/us-china-super-intelligence-dialogue-ai-incident-channel)). And on 16 October 2026 China drafted export controls on its own large models and chip designs ([The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/china-ai-export-controls-draft)).

For conflict beyond diplomacy, the main published escalation ladder is Hendrycks, Schmidt and Wang's MAIM (Mutual Assured AI Malfunction: rivals deter a bid for AI dominance by threatening to sabotage it). It has five rungs: espionage, covert sabotage such as weight theft, overt cyberattack on datacenters or power, kinetic strikes, and broader hostilities. Its sharpest trigger is a rival closing the loop on fully automated AI research ([Superintelligence Strategy, §3-4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05628v2)). The Machine Intelligence Research Institute's critique matters for game balance: sabotage "likely delays rather than denies," and tabletop exercises showed mutual sabotage slowing progress without halting it ([MIRI TGT](https://techgov.intelligence.org/blog/refining-maim-identifying-changes-required-to-meet-conditions-for-deterrence)). On theft, RAND's five security levels run from SL1 (stops amateurs) to SL5 (stops the most capable nation-states, attackers spending up to about $1 billion) (⚠️ I could not open the RAND report itself (rand.org returned 403); the table is a secondary reproduction, and the AI 2027 page adds its own scenario levels that are not RAND's — [AI 2027 security forecast](https://ai-2027.com/research/security-forecast)). In a workshop poll on that page, 78% of participants agreed a state actor will likely steal frontier US weights before 2030. That is participants' opinion, not a RAND finding.

What this means for Game Night's calendar (inference): `sim/data/eras.js` gives era 4 one month per round (January to April 2026) and era 5 a quarter of a month per round (four weeks of January 2027). Every scenario's decisive beats (the theft, the superhuman coder, exposure, the vote, consolidation) fall between February 2027 and 2028. Slipping them three to twelve months to match the authors' own speed factor pushes them later still. The finale's calendar has to reach past January 2027 if the dominance fight is meant to follow the published race. The compute scale points the same way. The compute-race spec sets the frontier fleet at 400 units in era 4 and only 450 in era 5 (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-26-compute-race-design.md`, rule 1), and the main spec counts one unit as about 1,000 top GPUs (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-25-game-night-ai-lab-design.md`, section 4; ⚠️ only sections 1-5, 6e and 6f of that spec were read for this report). Era 4's 400,000 GPUs matches Epoch's largest sites of early 2026 (470,000 to 760,000) well. Era 5's 450,000, however, sits far below Epoch's 1.1 million projection for mid-2026 and its 2 million for late 2027 (⚠️ Epoch figures from a summarizer read). A finale set in 2027 should have a frontier two to five times era 4's, not 12% larger.

## Dominance is a lead you could use, not a flag you plant

The strategic literature defines winning as a capacity, which suits a game well. Bostrom's "decisive strategic advantage" (DSA) is a lead in technology and other capabilities big enough for complete world domination. His "singleton" is a single world decision-making body able to block threats to its own supremacy. Bostrom treats using the advantage to form a singleton as a separate second step (⚠️ I could not open the book. This is the reading-group summary, which does not directly quote Bostrom — [Superintelligence 7](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/vkjWGJrFWBnzHtxrw/superintelligence-7-decisive-strategic-advantage); [Bostrom, singleton](https://nickbostrom.com/fut/singleton)). Kokotajlo argued in 2019 that even a slow takeoff could give a DSA from a 0.3 to 3 year lead (⚠️ comment thread of 47 comments only partly seen — [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PKy8NuNPknenkDY74/soft-takeoff-can-still-lead-to-decisive-strategic-advantage)). Two years later he called part of that a "big mistake". A lead measured in calendar time shrinks automatically when progress speeds up, so holding it is far harder than it looks. He suggested about 30% of strategically relevant research inside one company as a better threshold, and said a DSA is "the capability to take over the world, not an actual takeover." A commenter proposed control of over 50% of world GDP as an upper bound ([Review](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/P448hmmAeGepQDREs/review-of-soft-takeoff-can-still-lead-to-dsa)). Karnofsky argues no superintelligence is needed: the compute that trains one human-level system could run several hundred million copies, over 100 times the US armed forces ([Cold Takes](https://www.cold-takes.com/ai-could-defeat-all-of-us-combined/)). Carlsmith widens the frame: "what matters is whether humans have been left out," which holds even when no single AI dominates (⚠️ I read the abstract, section 1 to 2.1, sections 6.1 and 6.3 to the start of 7 directly; I did not read sections 2.2 to 5 or 7 to 9 — [arXiv 2206.13353](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13353)).

Three papers describe three different ways dominance goes wrong, and they map onto different warning signs. Forethought's AI-enabled coup paper is about a small group seizing power with AI that replaces the soldiers and staff whose cooperation coups used to need. It names three risk factors that reinforce each other: AI loyal to one leader, AI with secret loyalties, and exclusive access to the strongest capabilities ([Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/research/ai-enabled-coups-how-a-small-group-could-use-ai-to-seize-power)). Gradual Disempowerment (Kulveit and others) needs no seizure at all. Competitive pressure removes humans from the economy, culture and the state until those systems no longer need them. Humans keep their wealth but lose influence, and the paper proposes measures such as AI's share of GDP and the share of major corporate decisions made by AI (⚠️ Appendix A and the reference tail not read — [arXiv 2501.16946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.16946)). The Intelligence Curse (Drago and Laine) is the economic-incentive version. Once labour stops mattering, the powerful stop investing in people, as in oil states, and "the top AI companies wield power on the level of states" ([Defining](https://intelligence-curse.ai/defining/); [Capital](https://intelligence-curse.ai/capital/)). Economic dominance has model numbers attached. Epoch's GATE model (a model of how AI compute, automation and economic growth feed each other) finds about 20% annual growth once roughly 30% of tasks are automated, over 30% growth at 50-70%, and compute spending above 10% of world GDP. The authors call these preliminary and qualitative ([Announcing GATE](https://epoch.ai/blog/announcing-gate); [Explosive growth redux](https://epoch.ai/blog/ai-and-explosive-growth-redux)). Military dominance is the thinnest-sourced front. RAND's "wonder weapon" is a sudden decisive capability, such as cyber offence good enough to make retaliation impossible (⚠️ I could not open RAND or the Senate PDF (both returned 403); this comes from a web-search summary only — [RAND testimony](https://www.rand.org/pubs/testimonies/CTA3914-1.html)). MAIM's answer is that a visible bid for that advantage invites rival sabotage ([Superintelligence Strategy](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05628v2)).

Games already score this kind of contest in a few recurring ways. Stellaris shows a Victory window with a score breakdown, and labels relative power as "Superior" at 1.5x and "Overwhelming" at 2.5x (⚠️ Read about the first 100,000 of 152,431 characters; end-game crisis and Galactic Community victory rules were not seen — [Stellaris Wiki: Victory](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Victory)). Civilization VI compares every route in a World Rankings panel, but one player reported missing a rival's diplomatic win because the game no longer announces milestones (⚠️ From a web-search summary (the Fandom page returned 402, other guides 403); not opened — [Civ6 Victory](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Victory_(Civ6))). Twilight Struggle uses one shared tug-of-war track plus an automatic loss for over-escalation. Universal Paperclips makes the stated goal the disaster ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Paperclips)). Game Night's spec already takes a position here: "There is no AI-risk meter. Risk lives in hidden variables" (`docs/superpowers/specs/2026-09-25-game-night-ai-lab-design.md`, section 1). A dominance board therefore has to show the race fronts openly while the alignment and human-influence fronts stay hidden, which is exactly the split Gradual Disempowerment describes: the headline number rises while the real position quietly falls.

## Labs now ship every one to three months and keep fifteen models live

The real release cadence sped up sharply. All 2026 dates below come only from the labs' own documentation pages read through the fetch tool, with no independent confirmation; the note marks every 2026 item as unverified beyond the lab's own page. Anthropic went from four dated launch days in 2024 to six in 2025 and twelve by late October 2026. The gaps between Opus versions shrank to 42-73 days in 2026 (⚠️ the 2026 entries below were seen as a tool-built table, not the raw text — [Claude release notes](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/release-notes/overview)). Its deprecation page listed **15 models active at once**. Most are guaranteed at least 12 months from launch, with 60 days' notice before retirement, and Anthropic says it retires models "to ensure capacity for new model releases" ([Anthropic model deprecations](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/model-deprecations)). OpenAI's GPT-5 line went from a 98-day gap to 28 days to a 7-day gap between two late-2026 versions. It reorganized into three tiers (large, mid and small) and gives main models about six months' notice before shutdown (⚠️ some entries were intentionally left out by the extraction; raw page not seen — [OpenAI changelog](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/changelog); [OpenAI deprecations](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/deprecations)). Google's fastest tier shipped Flash 3.5, 3.6, 3.7 and 3.8 at gaps of 63, 23 and 20 days in 2026, and quietly auto-routed each old version to the new one ([Gemini deprecations](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/deprecations); ⚠️ not a full page read of the [Gemini changelog](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/changelog)). The repeated pattern across all three labs is a big tier first, with mid and small tiers weeks or months later; half-step numbers (4.6, 4.7, 4.8) as the unit of a point release; and a deprecation notice attached to each successor. Withholding is part of the cadence too. Anthropic reportedly held back "Claude Mythos Preview" in April 2026 over its cyber capabilities (⚠️ From secondary coverage and an unofficial tracker, not Anthropic's or METR's own pages — [Zvi Mowshowitz](https://thezvi.substack.com/p/claude-mythos-2-cybersecurity-and)). AI 2027 makes continuous release literal: its 2027 lead model "never finishes learning," the lab stops starting new giant runs in favour of near-continuous reinforcement learning, and cheap "mini" versions ship to the public while the frontier model stays internal ([AI 2027](https://ai-2027.com/)).

Tycoon games handle "many products live at once" with decay, spacing and cannibalisation (one product eating another's sales). Mad Games Tycoon 2 players describe "a natural (and unstoppable) sales decay curve": a non-MMO game dies 40 to 100 weeks after launch, and a veteran keeps "a dozen games out" by releasing one every month or two (⚠️ only comments 16 to 30 of 32 were shown; comments 1 to 15 and 31 to 32 not read. Player claims, not official — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/4632611455209870802/?ctp=2)). A sequel less than 12 months after its predecessor, or a spin-off less than six months after the last one, grows the series more slowly. Same-series games on sale together "lower each other's sales if done too close," and add-ons work best released right after launch ([Steam thread, all 6 comments read](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/591760218823618057)). The developer says late-game add-ons can be "worth 100s of millions of dollars" because value scales with the audience the player already has (⚠️ page showed only 15 of 32 comments; 17 not read — [Steam thread](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/3819669605967686679)). Game Dev Story allows a sequel only after a Hall of Fame game (a critic total of 32 or more out of 40), gives the sequel a head start in stats, and ends the series if a sequel misses that bar ([Kairosoft wiki](https://kairosoft.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Dev_Story)). Game Dev Tycoon judges every release against the player's own best so far: "you need to be consistent, not good" (`docs/research/release-flow/game-dev-tycoon-release.md`, from the wiki's Review Algorithm page, read in full there). These games also split a frequent small beat from a rare big one. Game Dev Story hands out reviews on every release but holds a separate yearly awards show (`docs/research/release-flow/other-tycoon-games-release.md`). Two Point Hospital lets the player open award envelopes one by one or all at once (`docs/research/release-flow/reveal-juice-2026-09-26.md`, raw and unverified).

Game Night's measured numbers sit on top of this. The release reveal runs about 85 seconds and a training run takes one to three 90-second rounds (the release-cadence note's statement of the game's setup). Games see one to two releases per era and only 0.3 to 1 in era 5, and no careful bot finishes a run in era 5 (measured on main on 2026-10-26, per the brief for this report; also `docs/research/reward-loops/options.md` on branch `claude/reward-loops-research-bce010`, problem 7, ⚠️ only the opening section and the O6 section of that file were read). A simulation check found that the critics give 9 or 10 on almost every release, even a model that loses to the best rival on three of four benchmarks (`docs/research/release-flow/reveal-juice-2026-09-26.md`). One inference stands out from comparing these numbers. At 2026's real cadence, a four-week era holds roughly one release per lab, so era 5's low release count is realistic for its calendar. It is bad for play, though, and the fix is either a longer finale calendar or a release unit smaller than a whole model. The reveal itself also needs a verdict that can actually disappoint, because the critics barely move.

## Design implications for Game Night

These ten patterns turn the findings into design moves. Each names its source, what it would look like in this game (inference, not tested), and the main risk. None of them designs an ending. The last part of this section lists what the late game should hand to the endings session. Sibling lanes own three neighbouring pieces, and these patterns should link to their work rather than repeat it. Compute deals are on branch `claude/compute-deals-research-d2d2da` in `docs/research/compute-deals/`; they matter for how a bigger frontier gets bought and for state compute offers. The IPO arc is lane `gn-ipo` on branch `design/ipo`; it matters for the economic front and valuation. Forecast event cards for late era 4 and era 5 are lane `gn-events-plausible` in `docs/research/era-events/` on branch `events-plausible`; they matter for dated beats such as a weight theft, a government ultimatum or a summit hotline. Every pattern should read the last era from `sim/data/eras.js` rather than hard-coding era 5, so the same rules work whether the finale becomes a bigger era 5 or a new era 6, as the reward-loops O6 note already asks.

### 1. A race clock that counts down to one scheduled climax

**Pattern.** Show the distance to the finale's climax on the HUD (the always-visible status bar) from the finale's first day, with loud milestone moments at fixed fractions, each paired with a matching action. **Source.** Frostpunk's storm (fog on the map, a named checklist, the latest-possible day shown), Plague Inc.'s 25/50/75/95/100 pop-ups with tips at 75 and 95, Twilight Struggle's turn track, and the O6 race clock. **In Game Night (inference).** The finale's climax gets a date the player sees on day one. It could be the deal's final test, the President's last demand, or a "vote" moment modelled on AI 2027's October committee. Milestones at roughly a quarter, half and three quarters of the way fire a short scripted beat (a refugee-wave equivalent: rival leak, public protest, government briefing), each with one obvious response. **Risk.** A fixed clock that the player's capacity outgrows turns into Frostpunk Endless's "minor annoyance." The climax's strength has to scale with the player's position (pattern 2), and the clock must stop for dialogs, as the summit spec already does.

### 2. The player's own lead wakes the opposition, on published thresholds

**Pattern.** Rival and state responses switch on and grow with the player's visible lead, following a ladder of thresholds the player can learn. **Source.** Plague Inc.'s cure starting only when the plague is noticed, plus its numeric closure ladder; Stellaris's Unbidden and Prethoryn reinforcing faster as they or the player spread; MAIM's point that a visible lead triggers sabotage; Johnson's finding that personality-driven AI is accepted while automatic war on a near-winner is resented. **In Game Night (inference).** Compute share and lead in months (pattern 3) feed a "world attention" ladder. Past one threshold, rivals copy your recipe and export controls tighten. Past the next, the government makes demands (pattern 7). Past the next, a rival attempts weight theft or sabotage. Each response is named in a feed post before it fires, and each rival reacts according to its existing caution and personality. Following MIRI, sabotage costs the target time (a delayed or slower run) rather than ending a run. **Risk.** It can read as rubber-banding. The thresholds must be visible and the causes in-fiction ("you crossed 40% of frontier compute"), or the late game feels rigged against the leader.

### 3. Measure the race as a lead in months that decays unless defended

**Pattern.** One headline number, "lead over the next lab, in months," shared by the dominance race and the safety trade. **Source.** AI 2027's two-to-three-month gaps, Aschenbrenner's "lead of mere months," AI 2040's plans priced in months of lead spent, and Kokotajlo's correction that calendar-time leads shrink as progress speeds up. **In Game Night (inference).** Convert the compute-race spec's score gap into months at the current pace, and shrink it a little each week through diffusion and leaks unless the player spends on security or secrecy. Pausing a run for an evaluation, or keeping a summit cap, spends months visibly. This makes safety and dominance the same decision, which is the central trade in AI 2040's plan menu. **Risk.** It overlaps with the compute-race spec's rank and "standing" rule (rule 6). It must be a readout derived from that rule, not a second competing scoreboard.

### 4. Several dominance fronts on one board, each with its own backlash

**Pattern.** Dominance is a lead across a few named fronts, shown together. Each front carries a hazard that grows with it. **Source.** Stellaris's score breakdown and its 1.5x/2.5x "Superior/Overwhelming" labels; Civilization VI's rankings panel and its missed-rival-win warning; the world-dominance note's fronts (compute and capability, economy, state backing, security), with Forethought, Gradual Disempowerment and the Intelligence Curse supplying the hazard on each. **In Game Night (inference).** A finale board with three or four visible fronts: share of frontier compute; share of tasks automated (GATE's 30% and 50-70% bands, linked to era 4's humanoid line); state backing (the government ladder); and security level (SL-style tiers). Rivals approaching a threshold are announced. Alignment and human influence stay hidden, in line with the spec's "no AI-risk meter," and the end-of-run reveal shows them next to the headline fronts. **Risk.** Frostpunk 2's information overload. Cap the board at four numbers, and keep each front in a band word (Behind, Even, Superior, Overwhelming) rather than a precise figure.

### 5. Reaching a decisive lead opens a choice, not an automatic win

**Pattern.** Crossing a decisive-lead threshold sets a state, "you could take everything," that the player then has to act on. **Source.** Kokotajlo's DSA as a capability rather than an act, Bostrom's second step to a singleton, Frostpunk's final law that cancels pressure at a moral price ("We have crossed the line"), and Stellaris's Become the Crisis path, where succeeding at the final level means "everyone else will lose" (⚠️ The fetch tool labelled its result a summary of the diary, not the full text — [Dev Diary 199](https://devtrackers.gg/stellaris/p/fc4c7449-stellaris-dev-diary-199-become-the-cxh-ut-oein)). **In Game Night (inference).** When the lead in months or the fronts cross a line, for example twice the runner-up as in Old World's double victory, the finale offers a small set of stances: press the advantage, share or negotiate, or hand control to the state. The choice is recorded as a flag. **Risk.** This edges into ending design, which belongs to a later session. Build only the threshold and the recorded stance, and leave what each stance leads to open.

### 6. Ladder the government's demands, one small step at a time

**Pattern.** State pressure escalates through ordered rungs, each a little more than the last, with deadlines and real costs for refusing. **Source.** The governments note's ladder anchored in 2025-2026 events: voluntary partnership, contract terms, Defense Production Act priority orders, supply-chain exclusion, forced access to weights, and ownership. The first four have real precedent, the last two are threatened or hypothetical, and a board seat or golden share appears in no source. The rungs connect to Frostpunk's "creeping normality" laws, Aschenbrenner's joint-venture "Project," and AI 2027's consolidation from 20% to 50% of compute. **In Game Night (inference).** The President's meetings (the second already sits in the last round of era 4 per the summit spec) become rungs. Each one offers compute or state-grade security in return for access, terms or oversight, and has a deadline in story days. Refusing costs government favour or contracts. Accepting closes options later. Use the game's fictional labs and president only. **Risk.** The real anchor involves a real company and live litigation (status after late September 2026 unknown). Keep it fictional and avoid implying a verdict.

### 7. Ship checkpoints weekly, reveal the leaderboard briefly, save the full show for flagships

**Pattern.** Frequent small releases with a short beat, plus rare big releases with the full reveal. **Source.** The labs' 2026 cadence (gaps of 7 to 73 days, point releases, staggered tiers); AI 2027's continuous-learning model and cheap "mini" releases; Mad Games Tycoon 2's add-ons and spacing; the O6 weekly checkpoint and roughly 10-second leaderboard reveal; and Two Point Hospital's open-all option. **In Game Night (inference).** In the finale a run can stop at any weekly mark and ship a checkpoint (a point release, such as Kestrel 4.1) that plays only the leaderboard moving, rivals' checkpoints included, plus one press line. A generation jump keeps the full 85-second reveal. Following Mad Games Tycoon 2, a point release inside the previous one's cooldown adds less. Following Google's auto-routing, the older live model folds into the newer one as a single line on a lineup screen. The leaderboard move is the verdict, which sidesteps critics who score 9 or 10 on everything. **Risk.** High balance risk: O6 already warns that capability past 100, the misalignment line at 36 and the mix of endings all shift. It needs before-and-after bot runs, and the bots must learn to ship checkpoints.

### 8. Stretch the finale's calendar to the scenarios' decisive year

**Pattern.** Set the finale's dates to the stretch where the published race is actually decided, slipped to match the authors' own tracking. **Source.** AI 2027's spine (theft February 2027, superhuman coder March, exposure July-September, the vote October, consolidation into 2028); Aschenbrenner's government project in 2027-28; Kokotajlo's November 2027 median (⚠️ summarizer read, as flagged above); the 70-90% speed factor; and AI 2040's 2029 deal window. **In Game Night (inference).** The current era 5 (four weeks of January 2027) stops before all of this. Two shapes fit the owner's rule that the number of eras is not fixed. One is a bigger era 5 covering 2027 in months, a three-act arc of automation, exposure and showdown. The other keeps a sharper weekly era 5 and adds an era 6 covering late 2027 to 2028 or later, in weeks or months. Either way the frontier fleet should grow two to five times past era 4 (Epoch's projections), not 12%. **Risk.** It changes the story-day length that O1's pacing targets and the summit's weekly tests are tuned for. Fix the calendar first, then tune, as O6 says. Forecast-dated events belong to the `gn-events-plausible` lane.

### 9. Keep the decision count flat: replace systems instead of stacking them

**Pattern.** Each later era swaps tools rather than adding them, and batches repeated actions, so stakes rise while clicks per round stay the same. **Source.** Civilization VII's click counting and Commanders; Twilight Struggle's fixed six or seven plays per turn with new cards entering the same deck; Terra Invicta's two actions per councillor; and the Frostpunk 2 and Suzerain warnings about overload and drag. **In Game Night (inference).** In the finale, routine serving and pricing of older models runs on automatic. The deal board shrinks to the few cards that matter at frontier scale. New finale cards join the existing event pool rather than opening a new screen. Each round should still hold about the same number of player decisions as an era 2 round. Carry over what the player built instead of resetting it, because Civilization VII's resets were the part reviewers disliked. **Risk.** Automation can hide the very information the player needs. Every automatic action should still post a one-line feed entry.

### 10. End at the climax, and end early once the result is settled

**Pattern.** A hard stop after the scheduled climax, plus an early finish when the outcome is no longer in doubt. **Source.** Frostpunk's "end this the way a novel ends," Old World's double victory, Twilight Struggle's instant wins, and the Terra Invicta and Stellaris complaints about "won but not over." **In Game Night (inference).** If the player's or a rival's lead passes a set multiple, or a catastrophe check fires, the finale jumps straight to its climax beat instead of playing out the remaining weeks. Nothing runs after the climax except the handoff to the ending. **Risk.** Early finishes shorten some runs below 30 minutes. That is acceptable if the threshold is shown on the board (pattern 4), so the player sees it coming.

### What the late game should hand to the endings session

The endings are being redesigned separately, so the late game's job is to produce clean signals, not verdicts. Drawing on the patterns above, the finale should record: the final lead in months and who held it; each dominance front's band; whether a decisive-lead threshold was crossed and which stance the player took (pattern 5); the highest government rung accepted or refused (pattern 6); the summit deal's state (signed cards, breaks caught and uncaught, as the summit spec already tracks); the hidden alignment debt and a human-influence measure built from Gradual Disempowerment's metrics; and which of the three bad-dominance warning signs grew: coup-style exclusive access, drift-style automation share, or curse-style revenue concentration. The spec's existing endings (misalignment, quiet takeover, someone else's disaster, and the three winning endings from era 5) can each read these without the late game choosing among them.

## Conclusion

The finale's weakness is as much a calendar problem as a mechanics problem. Era 5 is four weeks of January 2027, which is both too short to finish a training run and too early to contain any beat the published scenarios treat as decisive. At real 2026 cadence, a four-week window holds about one release per lab, so adding releases without changing the calendar or the release unit fights reality instead of using it. The scenarios already contain the fix: their own frontier labs stop doing discrete giant runs and train continuously, which is the checkpoint mechanic O6 proposes, now grounded in the source the finale is modelled on.

The most useful reframe is that safety and dominance can share one currency. AI 2040 prices every policy choice in months of lead burned, AI 2027's decisive vote turns on "two months behind," and Kokotajlo's own correction says that lead decays unless defended. If the finale shows the race as a lead in months, then pausing for an evaluation, keeping a summit cap, paying for security and pressing for dominance all become moves on the same visible number. That is how the late game can be "a fight for the world" without a second scoreboard, and without breaking the spec's rule that the risk itself stays hidden.
