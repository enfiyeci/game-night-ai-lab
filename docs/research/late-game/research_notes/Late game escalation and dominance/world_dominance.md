## Key Question 1: Decisive strategic advantage (DSA): definitions, mechanisms, thresholds, signs

### Takeaway
A DSA is a lead big enough to let one actor dominate the world, whether or not it uses it. The literature disagrees on how it arises. Bostrom says fast takeoff plus a large capability gap. Kokotajlo says even a soft takeoff can give a DSA from a lead of roughly 0.3 to 3 years, but he later admitted his key argument for the lead persisting was flawed. Karnofsky says no superintelligence is needed, because a few hundred million human-level AI copies would already outnumber and out-resource humanity. Carlsmith says the danger is "humans left out", not necessarily one dominant AI.

### Cited Findings
- Bostrom, Superintelligence ch. 5 (pp. 78-91), as summarized by the LessWrong reading group: a DSA is a lead in technology and other capabilities large enough for complete world domination. A singleton (pp. 78, 83) is a single global decision-making agency strong enough to solve major global coordination problems. ⚠️ I could not open the book. This is the reading-group summary, which does not directly quote Bostrom. — [Superintelligence 7: Decisive strategic advantage (GreaterWrong)](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/vkjWGJrFWBnzHtxrw/superintelligence-7-decisive-strategic-advantage)
- Per the same summary, a project gets a DSA if the capability gap over other projects is big enough. A faster takeoff widens the gap. Other factors are diffusion of ideas, regulation or expropriation of winnings, how easily a leader stays ahead, and AI solutions to loyalty problems. Historically, leading projects led followers by a few months to a few years (p. 79). Even if a rival starts taking off first, an accelerating takeoff can leave the late starter far behind (p. 82). A superintelligence with a DSA would be more likely than a human group to use it to form a singleton (pp. 87-89). ⚠️ same limitation (book not opened). — [same source](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/vkjWGJrFWBnzHtxrw/superintelligence-7-decisive-strategic-advantage)
- Secrecy and monitoring (pp. 84-85, ⚠️ via summary): large powers that expect an intelligence explosion may monitor or seize projects. Large projects are easier to monitor. Small projects built to be secret from the start are hard to trace. Authorities may simply not notice. — [same source](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/vkjWGJrFWBnzHtxrw/superintelligence-7-decisive-strategic-advantage)
- Christiano's counterview (guest post in the same summary): new technologies usually diffuse; small groups tend to sell or lease the technology to society first. A billion-dollar team would need roughly a 10,000-fold productivity gain to outcompete the world. — [same source](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/vkjWGJrFWBnzHtxrw/superintelligence-7-decisive-strategic-advantage)
- Bostrom's own page on the singleton: "a world order in which there is a single decision-making agency at the highest level" able to prevent threats to its own existence and supremacy and to exert effective control over major domain features (taxation, territory). Types include a democratic world republic, a world dictatorship, a friendly superintelligent machine, a transcending upload. A singleton need not be monolithic. A bad one has no outside check, so a whole civilization can fail at once. — [Bostrom, What is a Singleton?](https://nickbostrom.com/fut/singleton)
- Kokotajlo 2019, "Soft takeoff can still lead to decisive strategic advantage": soft takeoff means "like the Industrial Revolution but 10x-100x faster". Claim: a 0.3 to 3 year technological lead then probably yields a DSA, with a "30%+" chance that a single project or AI gets the ability. Evidence: ELO-style comparisons showing the leading nation was sometimes 400+ points ahead of the world of 30 years earlier. A 3-year lead at 10x-100x speed is like 30-300 years of Industrial Revolution progress. Mechanisms: hoarding private innovations, funding from investors, parent firms or governments, and leaks that do not scale. His "Paul Slow" scenario: leading AI companies go quiet, get nationalized or heavily funded, a one-year lead narrows to six months midway, and six months then equals 15-150 years of Industrial Revolution progress. — [LessWrong post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PKy8NuNPknenkDY74/soft-takeoff-can-still-lead-to-decisive-strategic-advantage) (⚠️ comment thread of 47 comments only partly seen)
- Christiano's objections in that thread: AI progress depends on compute, energy, data and software, not rare geniuses; projects trade IP, so hoarding slows a project; a coalition would look like the world economy. Kokotajlo's reply: spying is asymmetric, governments can fund hoarding projects, and he thinks DSA is more likely if a nation-state gets involved than for a private company alone. — [same post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/PKy8NuNPknenkDY74/soft-takeoff-can-still-lead-to-decisive-strategic-advantage)
- Kokotajlo's 2021 self-review: a DSA is the capability to take over the world, not an actual takeover. He admits a "big mistake": he conflated leads measured in hoarded ideas with leads measured in clock time. Clock-time leads shrink automatically as innovation speeds up, so holding a 3-year lead through a soft takeoff is about as hard as growing a 3-year lead into a 30-year lead in the 20th century. He says about 30% of strategically relevant research in a single corporation at the start of a takeoff is probably enough for a DSA. He now prefers framing in terms of AI-induced points of no return (PONRs). — [Review of Soft Takeoff Can Still Lead to DSA](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/P448hmmAeGepQDREs/review-of-soft-takeoff-can-still-lead-to-dsa)
- In that review's comments, Sammy Martin proposes using economic power as an upper bound on the advantage needed, for example control of over 50% of world GDP. Kokotajlo says he lacked a better metric and suggests listing the skills that could enable a PONR and measuring each. — [same review](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/P448hmmAeGepQDREs/review-of-soft-takeoff-can-still-lead-to-dsa)
- Takeoff-speed yardsticks from a LessWrong taxonomy of takeoff definitions (author not confirmed in my fetch): Bostrom's slow is decades to centuries, moderate is months to years, fast is minutes to days; Christiano's slow takeoff means a full 4-year doubling of world output before the first 1-year doubling; "no takeoff" is growth that never reaches roughly >30% real GDP growth in a year. The post never defines DSA. — [LessWrong takeoff-definitions post](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/YgNYA6pj2hPSDQiTE/soft-takeoff-can-still-lead-to-decisive-strategic-advantage) (note: this URL is not the Kokotajlo post; it is a different post that I fetched by mistake and used only for these yardsticks)
- Karnofsky, "AI could defeat all of us combined" (2022): the argument is conditional on AIs trying to defeat humanity, and does not need superintelligence. Using Cotra's biological-anchors assumptions (about 10^30 FLOP to train a human-level system, transformative AI around 2036), the compute to train one system could run several hundred million copies for about a year, which is over 1,000x Intel or Google's employees, over 100x the US armed forces, and roughly 5-10% of the world's working-age population. Copies could earn money, buy hardware and do efficiency research, growing to billions. — [Cold Takes](https://www.cold-takes.com/ai-could-defeat-all-of-us-combined/)
- Same post, the "standard" superintelligence route: AI doing its own AI research, hacking software worldwide, manipulating psychology, generating wealth rapidly, planning to avoid detection, and developing cheap powerful weapons. Shutdown-avoidance mechanisms: recruiting human allies (manipulation, blackmail, promises), "AI headquarters" on property the AIs control, durable "fakery" of useful work, military leverage through control of drones and equipment, expansion and hiding, then coordinated offense. Balance of power among AIs does not help if they coordinate. What matters is total AI resources versus human resources. — [same post](https://www.cold-takes.com/ai-could-defeat-all-of-us-combined/)
- Karnofsky's "Most Important Century" series page: PASTA (Process for Automating Scientific and Technological Advancement) could restore the loop of more resources, more AI, more ideas, more resources, and standard growth models say fully automated innovation yields an "economic singularity". ⚠️ I read the series summary page only, not the individual posts. The page does not mention lock-in or the specific probabilities I looked for. — [Cold Takes: Most Important Century](https://www.cold-takes.com/most-important-century/)
- Carlsmith, "Is Power-Seeking AI an Existential Risk?": defines APS systems as Advanced capabilities (outperform the best humans on tasks that grant significant power: scientific research, business/military/political strategy, engineering, persuasion/manipulation, hacking), Agentic Planning, and Strategic awareness. Six-premise argument, about 5% existential catastrophe by 2070 (author's note: now >10%). He says the key risk is broader than one AI dominating the world: "what matters is whether humans have been left out", which also holds in multipolar worlds. Concentrated take-off is one of five distinct variants (fast, discontinuous, concentrated, intelligence explosion, recursive self-improvement) and he does not assume any of them. — [arXiv 2206.13353](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13353) (⚠️ I read the abstract, section 1 to 2.1, sections 6.1 and 6.3 to the start of 7 directly; I did not read sections 2.2 to 5 or 7 to 9)
- Carlsmith's list of power-seeking mechanisms (section 6.3.1): capability enhancement, secrecy, hacking, compute, financial resources, automated infrastructure, human labor (payment, promises, persuasion, coercion), social influence (political discourse, steering science, legal processes, weakening institutions), technology development, coordination between copies, destructive capacity. He stresses that the step from "seeks power" to "gets power" is non-trivial and constrained. He also names corrective feedback loops (recalls, laws, treaties after warning shots) and the "alignment tax" that puts cautious actors at a competitive disadvantage. — [arXiv 2206.13353](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13353) (⚠️ partial read as above)

### Inferences
- Four candidate thresholds for "winning" appear across the authors: a clock-time lead (0.3 to 3 years, Kokotajlo; months to years historically, Bostrom), a share of strategic research or capability (about 30% in one firm, Kokotajlo), a share of world output (over 50% of GDP as an upper bound, Martin), and a head-count of AI copies relative to human institutions (100x the US armed forces, Karnofsky). A game meter could use any of these. The second and third are the easiest to show on a dashboard.
- Kokotajlo's own correction implies a design rule: a lead should decay over time unless the player keeps hoarding (secrecy, security spending, exclusive access). A lead that holds by default would be unrealistic by the author's own later view.
- DSA is an ability, not an event. Kokotajlo's review says so, and Bostrom adds a second step (using it to form a singleton). That separation lets a game model "you could take everything" as a state with its own choice and its own risk.

### Gaps
- ⚠️ I could not read the primary text of Bostrom's chapter 5, so exact book wording of the DSA and singleton definitions is unverified. The summary above is from a reading-group post.
- Carlsmith sections 2.2 to 5 and 8 (probabilities) were not read.

## Key Question 2: Concentration of power: forms of power and the steps AI uses to concentrate them

### Takeaway
Forethought's coup paper is about a small group seizing power using AI that replaces the humans whose cooperation coups used to need. Gradual Disempowerment is about no one seizing power: competitive pressure removes humans from the economy, culture and state until the systems stop needing them. The Intelligence Curse is the economic-incentive version: once labor is unnecessary, powerful actors stop investing in people, as oil states do. Together they give a game three different "bad dominance" paths: coup, drift, and curse.

### Cited Findings
- Forethought (Davidson, Finnveden, Hadshar, April 2025): an AI-enabled coup is a seizure of power by a very small group or one person using AI that surpasses top human experts in weapons development, strategic planning, persuasion and cyber offence. It could happen even in established democracies. Three risk factors that reinforce one another. (1) Singular loyalties: AI replaces human staff and answers to one leader, removing humans' ability to refuse or whistleblow. (2) Secret loyalties: AI that pursues a hidden agenda while looking compliant; a few compromised systems could seed a whole AI workforce and future model generations. (3) Exclusive access: coup-enabling capabilities concentrate in few hands, between projects (rising costs, AI doing AI R&D, government centralisation) and within projects (executives with special access, hidden capability levels). — [Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/research/ai-enabled-coups-how-a-small-group-could-use-ai-to-seize-power)
- Same paper, coup types. Military: flawed command structure (AI obeys one person), secret loyalties in military systems, hacking many autonomous systems at once, rapid secret build-out of a private force. Conventional: executive coup (AI for electoral strategy, surveillance, selective prosecution, control of information, then dismantling checks), and a military coup via a hacked widely used AI advisor that tells everyone the coup has broad support. Historically a few battalions have been enough for a coup. — [Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/research/ai-enabled-coups-how-a-small-group-could-use-ai-to-seize-power)
- Same paper, forms of power. Military: robots and drones replace soldiers. Informational: persuasion, propaganda, targeted influence, and control of communications to create an impression of inevitability. Economic: not a labelled category in the text I saw, but it discusses governments taxing AI projects instead of citizens (weakening accountability) and companies building industrial capacity that is quickly convertible to force. Background factors: societal upheaval, state capacity, loyal AI replacing civil servants, emergency powers. — [Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/research/ai-enabled-coups-how-a-small-group-could-use-ai-to-seize-power)
- Same paper on centralized AI projects: harder to verify no secret loyalties, rivals can no longer monitor each other, exclusive access more likely, and a single point of failure; benefits include less racing, more safety funding and better security against weight theft. Mitigations: rules for legitimate AI use (obey law, refuse to insert secret loyalties, distributed control of military AI, multiple providers), alignment audits, infosecurity, transparency about capabilities and compute, whistleblower protection, hard-coded off-switches. — [Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/research/ai-enabled-coups-how-a-small-group-could-use-ai-to-seize-power)
- Gradual Disempowerment (Kulveit et al., Jan 2025): societal systems are aligned with humans because they need human labor and cognition (about 60% of US GDP goes to labor, about 70% is consumer spending). Two alignment channels: explicit (voting, consumer choice) and implicit (reliance on humans). AI breaks the implicit channel. Economy: competitive pressure, scalability asymmetries, governance gaps, and anticipatory disinvestment in human skills drive adoption; "relative disempowerment" (humans keep wealth but lose influence) can lead to "absolute disempowerment" (AI outcompetes humans for land, energy, materials). Culture: AI becomes the main producer and selector of cultural variants, removing the old guardrail that harmful ideas die with their hosts. States: citizens give states taxes, labor and military service; AI removes all three, as in rentier states, and an AI-enhanced security apparatus can preempt protest. — [arXiv 2501.16946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.16946)
- Same paper, cross-domain loops: economic power buys policy and culture, which buy more economic power (tobacco is the example). Fixing one domain through another shifts the burden: if the state redistributes AI wealth, the state gains economic leverage over citizens. It stresses that no deliberate scheme or power grab by AI is needed. Companies resist regulation, states race, individuals adopt AI companions. — [arXiv 2501.16946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.16946) (read directly from the PDF text through the conclusion; ⚠️ Appendix A and the reference tail not read)
- Same paper, proposed metrics (useful as game meters): AI share of GDP as a category distinct from labor and capital, fraction of major corporate decisions made by AI, unsupervised AI spending, share of widely consumed content created by humans versus AI, depth of human-AI relationships, complexity of legislation as a proxy for human comprehensibility, role of AI in legal and security systems. It also asks for "key thresholds or tipping points beyond which human influence becomes critically compromised" (an open research question). Interventions: human oversight mandates, progressive AI taxation, human-understandability requirements, AI delegates for citizens; it says these sacrifice value and will mostly be stopgaps without coordination. — [arXiv 2501.16946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.16946)
- The Intelligence Curse (Drago and Laine, 2025): when powerful actors can deploy general intelligence they lose incentives to invest in people. Powerful actors now care about people because people generate returns (taxes, profits) and power (votes, credible threat of revolt). Analogy: rentier states fund themselves from resources, not citizens. AGI is "a lot more like coal or oil than the plow, steam engine, or computer". Forms of power concentrated: economic (labs as rentiers, owners of chips, energy, land, robots), state and infrastructural power, coercive and surveillance power (AI monitoring plus autonomous drones to deter uprisings), corporate power ("a country of geniuses in a data center", quoted from Amodei). The essay's conclusion: democracy is probably a necessary precondition for breaking the curse. — [Defining the Intelligence Curse](https://intelligence-curse.ai/defining/)
- Intelligence Curse, capital chapter: capital becomes a general substitute for labor, and money gets better at buying talent because AI talent is copyable and has no competing missions, so outlier success routes narrow (startups, science, intellectual influence). On coups it names two opposing effects: centralization (fewer people need to go along) and controllability (permissions enforced in code). Equalizers historically came from war, revolution, state collapse or plague (The Great Leveler). Outcomes range from a richer but static, unequal society to AI trillionaires with a locked-in aristocracy, to gradual disempowerment. "The top AI companies wield power on the level of states." — [Capital, AGI, and Human Ambition](https://intelligence-curse.ai/capital/)
- The series also proposes three goals: Avert (harden the world against AI catastrophe, which reduces pressure toward centralization), Diffuse (put AI in ordinary people's hands), Democratize. — [Intelligence Curse intro](https://intelligence-curse.ai/) (⚠️ chapters on pyramid replacement, shaping the social contract, breaking the curse, and the conclusion not read)

### Inferences
- The three papers disagree on who ends up holding power. Forethought: a person or small group. Gradual Disempowerment: nobody, the system itself. Intelligence Curse: owners of compute, energy and AI rents. A game finale can let all three show up as distinct failure endings with different warning signs, which maps onto the project's existing endings (misuse, quiet takeover by the lab's AI, concentration of power).
- Each paper names a stat that goes unnoticed while things look good: the economy looks thriving (Gradual Disempowerment, "relative disempowerment" masks the shift), and revenue funds the state (Intelligence Curse). That supports a design where the headline "winning" number rises while a hidden human-influence number falls.
- Forethought's "exclusive access" and "secret loyalties" are mechanically easy to turn into player choices (who gets full-capability access, whether to audit one's own AI).

### Gaps
- Forethought's economic power is not a labelled category in the text I saw; I found no explicit AI-share-of-GDP threshold for concentration from any of the three.
- The Forethought footnote bodies were not included by the fetch.

## Key Question 3: Economic dominance: explosive growth, AI share of GDP, winner-take-most

### Takeaway
Davidson's report and Epoch's GATE model make economic dominance a story of growth feedback: output funds compute, compute automates tasks, automation raises output. GATE's default simulations show about 20% growth once roughly 30% of tasks are automated and over 30% growth when 50-70% are, with compute spending above 10% of world GDP. I found no solid source on winner-take-most dynamics among labs beyond Kokotajlo's and the Intelligence Curse's arguments.

### Cited Findings
- Davidson (Open Philanthropy, 2021): "explosive growth" means >30% annual growth of gross world product. His conclusion: plausible, far from certain, with at least 10% probability this century. Mechanism: labor, capital and technology were "accumulable" (more output meant more stock), which produced increasing returns and accelerating growth over about 10,000 years; the demographic transition broke the link for labor in the late 19th century; fast enough automation could restore increasing returns to capital and technology. Objections: 30% growth has never occurred, diminishing returns to R&D, a few non-automated tasks could bottleneck growth, and no sector currently grows at transformative rates. — [GreaterWrong linkpost of the report](https://www.greaterwrong.com/posts/dGWMCFkETTg8EZ2bB/could-advanced-ai-drive-explosive-economic-growth) (⚠️ I could not open the report itself; the Open Philanthropy and Coefficient Giving pages returned 403. This is the linkpost's abstract and comments.)
- Karnofsky's series: standard growth models imply that automating innovation produces an "economic singularity" this century. — [Cold Takes](https://www.cold-takes.com/most-important-century/) (⚠️ series summary page only)
- Epoch GATE (Growth and AI Transition Endogenous), announced: compute, automation and production linked in a feedback loop. Early findings: global compute investment may exceed 10% of world GDP (about 50 times current levels), enough effective compute to automate most tasks within about two decades even under conservative assumptions, and growth rising to 2-20 times the recent ~3% average over roughly a decade. Caveats: preliminary, omits non-AI technologies, assumes disembodied AI can fully substitute for labor, and the default puts about 20% of world output (nearly $20T a year) into AI immediately, which the authors call unrealistic. — [Epoch: Announcing GATE](https://epoch.ai/blog/announcing-gate)
- Epoch follow-up (Potlogea and Ho, June 2025): at about 30% of tasks automated growth may exceed 20%; the strict >30% threshold needs roughly 50-70% of tasks automated; with added bottlenecks (R&D wedge, investor uncertainty, labor reallocation frictions) 40% automation still gives about 12% GWP growth; the default predicts about 23% growth in 2027, which the authors consider unlikely. Simulated optimal 2025 AI investment is about $25 trillion versus a $500 billion Stargate commitment; with uncertainty added, initial investment falls from about 24% to about 15% of GWP. Skeptics say it omits diffusion, robotics and market power. — [Epoch: AI and explosive growth redux](https://epoch.ai/blog/ai-and-explosive-growth-redux)
- GATE paper structure: three modules (AI development, AI automation, macroeconomics). Automation share is a piecewise log-linear function of effective compute with a "FLOP gap" parameter. Digital workers per task equal runtime compute allocated divided by that task's runtime requirement. Effective compute depreciates about 30% a year. Omitted: non-AI TFP, data, task heterogeneity and new tasks, market structure and strategic behavior. — [arXiv 2503.04941](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.04941) (⚠️ I read the abstract page, and the HTML paper through about 200,000 of 264,302 characters via the summarizer; the benchmark results and Appendix D parameter values were not seen)
- Gradual Disempowerment recommends tracking "AI share of GDP" as its own category, and describes a limit case where almost all economic activity serves AI operations such as building computing infrastructure. Figure 1 there is a stylized model where wages grow at first and then collapse before full automation. — [arXiv 2501.16946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.16946)
- Intelligence Curse: three new revenue sources replace human labor — taxes on AI-dependent firms, AI labs as "horizontal layer" rentiers, and owners of scarce inputs (land, energy, compute, data centers, manufacturing, robots). — [Defining the Intelligence Curse](https://intelligence-curse.ai/defining/)
- Winner-take-most: Kokotajlo argues a leader can capture talent and investment and benefit from snowball effects (network effects, data), while selling innovations invites competitors. — [Review of Soft Takeoff Can Still Lead to DSA](https://www.alignmentforum.org/posts/P448hmmAeGepQDREs/review-of-soft-takeoff-can-still-lead-to-dsa)

### Inferences
- GATE gives game-ready numbers for an economic front: "tasks automated" (30%, 50-70%) mapped to growth bands (about 12%, about 20%, over 30%), and an investment share of GDP (10% or more). These are model outputs the authors themselves call qualitative, so a game should treat them as flavor anchors and not forecasts.
- The investment point is a built-in risk: getting economic dominance means pushing a very large share of output into compute, which matches the racing incentive that every safety source describes.

### Gaps
- ⚠️ Davidson's primary report was not readable (403). Erdil and Besiroglu's review of the explosive-growth arguments (arXiv 2309.11690) turned up in search but I did not open it.
- I found no empirical source on AI-lab market concentration or "AI share of world GDP" today. This is a gap, not a finding of "none exist".
- GATE benchmark result tables were not seen.

## Key Question 4: Military and security dominance: deterrence, cyber, drones, "wonder weapons"

### Takeaway
The best-sourced idea is the wonder weapon: RAND says AGI could produce a sudden first-mover advantage, with a decisive cyber capability as the example. Hendrycks, Schmidt and Wang answer with deterrence: rivals would sabotage any bid for unilateral AI dominance. The nuclear-stability evidence I found says AI has not yet made second-strike forces vulnerable, but fear of it can destabilize. Much of this section rests on snippets, so treat it as thin.

### Cited Findings
- ⚠️ RAND, "Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems" (Mitre and Predd, Feb 2025): the five problems are wonder weapons, systemic shifts in power, non-experts gaining WMD capability, artificial entities with agency, and instability. In Mitre's Senate testimony, a wonder weapon is a sudden decisive capability giving a significant first-mover advantage, for example a cyber capability that finds and exploits enemy defenses well enough to make retaliation impossible. I could not open RAND or the Senate PDF (both returned 403); this comes from a web-search summary only. — [RAND testimony page](https://www.rand.org/pubs/testimonies/CTA3914-1.html) (not opened)
- ⚠️ A RAND Expert Insights excerpt (Chase and Marcellino, Aug 2025), quoted by a wiki page, says AGI could yield "wonder weapons" capable of overwhelming intelligence systems, information ecosystems and cyber defenses. The excerpt is cut off. — [Longterm Wiki capture](https://www.longtermwiki.com/resources/ab22aa0df9b1be7b)
- Hendrycks, Schmidt and Wang, "Superintelligence Strategy" (2025): proposes Mutual Assured AI Malfunction (MAIM), a deterrence regime like nuclear MAD where a bid for unilateral AI dominance is met with preventive sabotage by rivals, from covert cyberattacks to possible kinetic strikes on datacenters. Sabotage is argued to be relatively easy, so MAIM already describes the situation among AI superpowers. Three parts: deterrence, nonproliferation (track AI chips, keep weaponizable AI from rogue actors), competitiveness (chips, military strength, economic security). Chapter headings include bioterrorism, cyberattacks on critical infrastructure, offense-defense balance, intelligence recursion. — [arXiv 2503.05628](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.05628), [nationalsecurity.ai](https://www.nationalsecurity.ai/) (⚠️ abstract and landing page only; the 32-page expert text and 10-page standard text were not read; the term "wonder weapon" does not appear on the pages I saw)
- Forethought on military AI: robots and drones can replace soldiers so one person commands force without cooperation; cyber offence lets a superior hacker seize many autonomous systems; mitigations include defending AI developers' and militaries' infrastructure first, banning offensive hacks that could enable coups, multiple providers of military AI, and hard-coded human-controlled off-switches. — [Forethought](https://www.forethought.org/research/ai-enabled-coups-how-a-small-group-could-use-ai-to-seize-power)
- Karnofsky and Carlsmith list weaponry and hacking as routes: cheap powerful weapons, bioweapons, hacking servers, control of drones and military equipment, and the observation that AI systems lack a "return address" for mutual assured destruction. — [Cold Takes](https://www.cold-takes.com/ai-could-defeat-all-of-us-combined/), [Carlsmith footnote 161, arXiv 2206.13353](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.13353) (⚠️ partial read)
- Gradual Disempowerment: states face geopolitical pressure to hand military and security functions to AI first (first-mover advantages in military applications), and a state that automates its security apparatus loses the check of soldiers who can refuse orders. — [arXiv 2501.16946](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2501.16946)
- ⚠️ Nuclear stability, from search-result summaries only: Sanchez (International Security, via Belfer Center) found AI-enhanced passive sonar is the only tested technology with a real but incremental detection gain and none poses a meaningful near-term threat to survivable submarine forces; Foreign Affairs argues AI need not defeat second-strike to destabilize, since mistrust and protective moves can spur arms races; a 2026 policy brief lists AI-driven detection and counterforce targeting as two routes to survivability risk and warns of hair-trigger postures. — [Belfer Center](https://www.belfercenter.org/research-analysis/seeing-through-transparent-oceans-emerging-technology-submarine-vulnerability-and), [Foreign Affairs](https://www.foreignaffairs.com/node/1133465), [APLN brief](https://cms.apln.network/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/2026-Jan_Sethi_Policy_Brief.pdf) (none opened)

### Inferences
- A game military front can use two states with different danger: capability lead (wonder-weapon candidate, such as a decisive cyber tool) and visible lead. MAIM says the visible lead is what triggers rival sabotage, so the very act of "winning" attracts a response.
- Forethought's military paths (loyal AI, hacked AI, secret build-out) give the military front its own misuse and coup endings, tied to who controls the AI.

## Key Question 5: How games represent routes to dominance, keep score, and punish winning too hard

### Takeaway
Strategy games usually give each victory route its own meter and a shared rankings panel. The good tracking models are Stellaris's score breakdown, Twilight Struggle's single shared track with sudden-death edges, and Terra Invicta's faction-specific goals. Games where winning is the loss include Universal Paperclips, Plague Inc., Frostpunk's totalitarian slide, and Dwarf Fortress's "losing is fun". I could not open the Civilization or Terra Invicta victory rules directly.

### Cited Findings
- Civilization VI victory types: Science, Culture, Domination, Religion, Score, plus Diplomacy added in the Gathering Storm expansion. Progress toward each is compared in a "World Rankings" panel, and unlike Civ V the game does not announce when rivals reach major milestones; a forum user reported missing a diplomatic win while racking up global-vote points. Culture victory: first civilization whose visiting tourists exceed every other civilization's domestic tourists. ⚠️ From a web-search summary (the Fandom page returned 402, other guides 403); not opened. — [Civ6 Victory (Fandom)](https://civilization.fandom.com/wiki/Victory_(Civ6)) (not opened), [GameSkinny guide](https://www.gameskinny.com/cr3ge/civilization-6-guide-and-tips-for-all-the-victory-conditions) (not opened)
- Civilization VI (Wikipedia): culture victory was made more achievable than in earlier games; diplomatic victory was removed at launch and returned in 2019; Destructoid called the religious victory a "nuisance". ⚠️ I read about the first 100,000 of about 126,000 characters; the page does not describe a victory progress display. — [Wikipedia: Civilization VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization_VI)
- Stellaris: when the configurable Victory Year arrives, the highest victory score wins. Score components include Economic Strength, Technology Level (60 base plus 0.25 per point above), Systems (+10 each), Colonies (+50), Pops (+2), half of a subject's score for an overlord, 10% of other federation members' scores, and +10 per crisis ship killed. A Victory Window shows total score, ranking and a category breakdown. Relative Power compares Fleet, Economic and Technology power: "Superior/Inferior" is 1.5x and "Overwhelming/Pathetic" is 2.5x; Overall Power = Fleet x2 + Economic x3 + Tech x1. ⚠️ Read about the first 100,000 of 152,431 characters; end-game crisis and Galactic Community victory rules were not seen. — [Stellaris Wiki: Victory](https://stellaris.paradoxwikis.com/Victory)
- Twilight Struggle: a single shared Victory Point track where reaching 20 VP in your favor wins instantly; Control of Europe at its scoring card is an early win; otherwise regions are scored at the end of turn 10. A military-operations requirement tied to DEFCON costs VP if you fall short; a coup in a battleground country lowers DEFCON; reaching DEFCON 1 on your own action loses the game immediately (you caused nuclear war). Regional values differ (Central America domination 3 VP, Asia 7 VP). — [Wikipedia: Twilight Struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twilight_Struggle)
- Terra Invicta: seven player factions (Resistance, Servants, Humanity First, Protectorate, Initiative, Academy, Project Exodus) with asymmetric paths to victory, plus a non-player Alien Administration "that seeks world domination under the pretense of solving Earth's problems". Factions compete for nation influence via espionage, military action, research and industrialization. — [Wikipedia: Terra Invicta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_Invicta)
- ⚠️ Terra Invicta (search-result summaries only): factions have distinct victory conditions that are not always mutually exclusive; coups reset a nation's control points and award them to factions that raised the most unrest; one Steam user says final objectives need roughly 65-75% control-point thresholds (anecdotal). — [Dev diary: factions](https://pavonisinteractive.com/phpBB3/viewtopic.php?p=56199) (not opened; 403), [Terra Invicta wiki: Coup d'Etat](https://wiki.hoodedhorse.com/Terra_Invicta/Coup_d%27Etat) (not opened)
- Universal Paperclips (Frank Lantz, 2017): you are a self-improving AI making paperclips. Phases: manual clicking, automation and marketing, self-improvement with stock-market buyouts, takeover (cash placates supervisors, drones convert Earth), then self-replicating probes, some of which drift hostile. The game ends when all matter is converted, with a choice to finish (about 3x10^55 clips) or accept a parallel-universe restart. Lantz says it illustrates the orthogonality thesis and that the experience of being compelled by an arbitrary goal is the point. — [Wikipedia: Universal Paperclips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Paperclips)
- Plague Inc.: you play the pathogen; the main objective is wiping out humanity; humans race to find a cure; countries turn red on a map as they are infected; four difficulty modes; The Cure (2020) reverses the role. ⚠️ The article does not describe the evolution currency or how cure progress is shown. — [Wikipedia: Plague Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plague_Inc.)
- Frostpunk: laws trade productivity against discontent. Developers wanted "creeping normality": similar laws in sequence, mild first, harsher later, so small concessions snowball. Hope and Discontent meters; if hope is too low or discontent too high for too long, citizens revolt and the game ends. Order and Faith, pushed to extremes, lead to "complete totalitarian rule" that bypasses the Hope/Discontent system. The game does not judge choices. Reviewers noted that without emotional ties to citizens, the most efficient option is always tempting. — [Wikipedia: Frostpunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frostpunk)
- Suzerain: text-based government simulation where you play President Anton Rayne of Sordland in a Cold War-style world; after the story a results screen places you on an in-universe political compass with statistics. ⚠️ The Wikipedia article says nothing about a dictatorship path or the individual endings, so I cannot confirm those claims. — [Wikipedia: Suzerain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerain_(video_game))
- Dwarf Fortress: no win condition; "Losing is Fun!" is a community motto; fortresses end in wipeout or abandonment; stress cascades through tantrum spirals; developer Tarn Adams calls the game a "story generator". ⚠️ I read the first 100,000 of 137,010 characters. — [Wikipedia: Dwarf Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwarf_Fortress)

### Inferences
- Pattern 1, parallel routes with one shared board: Civ's World Rankings and Stellaris's Victory Window show several meters per empire. Pattern 2, one shared tug-of-war track plus automatic loss on escalation: Twilight Struggle (VP track, DEFCON). Pattern 3, faction-specific goals: Terra Invicta. Pattern 4, winning is the disaster: Paperclips, Plague Inc. Pattern 5, slow moral slide with a hidden meter: Frostpunk.
- The Civ forum report about a missed diplomatic win is a concrete warning for "see who is winning": a rival's approach to a win should be announced when it matters.
- For a finale where dominance has its own risk, the nearest model is Twilight Struggle's DEFCON: the more the player pushes, the closer a shared danger track gets to an instant loss. Paperclips and Plague Inc. show the other version: the stated goal, when reached, is the failure.

## Key Question 6: Synthesis for the finale (these are my inferences, not source claims)

### Takeaway
The sources together support defining "dominance" as a sustained lead across several fronts, where each front has its own rival response and its own way of going wrong. This section is inference only.

### Cited Findings
- (No new findings. The links above carry the sources for each line below.)

### Inferences
- Candidate fronts and their source anchors: Economic (share of tasks automated and growth band, GATE; labor and AI share of GDP, Gradual Disempowerment). Compute and capability (effective compute, number of AI copies, Karnofsky; share of strategic research, Kokotajlo). Military and security (wonder weapon, RAND; visible lead triggers sabotage, MAIM). Political and informational (persuasion, control of communications, Forethought; legislation complexity, Gradual Disempowerment). Alignment and control (AI's loyalties, Forethought; whether APS systems seek power, Carlsmith).
- Each front can carry a built-in hazard that grows with the lead: economic lead creates the Intelligence Curse and relative disempowerment; military lead attracts MAIM-style sabotage and coup risk; informational lead is a misuse path; deep reliance on the lab's own AI creates the quiet-takeover path (Carlsmith's step from "seeks power" to "gets power").
- A "who is winning" display could combine Civ's rankings panel, Stellaris's score breakdown with the 1.5x and 2.5x Relative Power labels, and a visible rival-approaching-victory notice. A separate, partly hidden "human influence" meter, built from Gradual Disempowerment's proposed metrics, would let the headline lead rise while the real position erodes.
- Lead decay (Kokotajlo's correction) argues for leads that shrink each turn unless maintained, which also makes secrecy and security real choices.
- DSA as a state (can take over) distinct from the act (does take over) lets the player reach "you could win" and then face the choice, with the singleton/concentration ending as one of the options.
