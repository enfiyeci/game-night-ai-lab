# Release cadence and multi-product lineups: tycoon games and real AI labs (2023 to 2026)

Research date: October 27, 2026. Prepared for "Game Night AI Lab" (a Game Dev Tycoon style game about training and releasing AI models; each era is 6 minutes of running clock, a training run takes one to three 90-second rounds, and the release reveal is about 85 s).

How to read the flags in this file:
- "Read through WebFetch" means the fetch tool returned a processed answer to my prompt (a small model's extraction of the page), not the raw page. I never saw the raw HTML of any page. Where the tool said it filtered or truncated, I say so with a ⚠️.
- ⚠️ on a claim = that claim rests on a partial read (a search-result snippet only, a truncated forum page, or a page I could not open). The ⚠️ names what was missing.
- "Derived" = arithmetic or counting I did myself from the cited rows. Check these before quoting.
- All 2026 release and deprecation dates come only from the labs' own documentation pages as returned by the fetch tool. I found no independent news corroboration for any 2026 model. Treat all 2026 items as "unverified beyond the lab's own page" (see Gaps for the specific discrepancies I noticed).

## 1. Game Dev Tycoon (and Game Dev Story): release frequency, sizes, sales life, sequels, contracts, reviews

### Takeaway
I could not open the Game Dev Tycoon wiki (every Fandom page returned HTTP 402), so exact dev-time-per-size and the sales formula are NOT verified here. What I could verify from player threads: contracts are the filler work between big projects, medium and large games are gated by fan count and by publisher deals, reviews are judged against your best game so far, and in the closely related Game Dev Story a sequel is only allowed after a Hall of Fame game and carries starting bonuses, while contracts run 6 to 13 weeks.

### Cited Findings
- Game size gating: players say you need publishing deals to make Medium games until about 100K fans, and Large games until about 250K fans (that is when self-publishing becomes safe). — [Steam thread, Game Dev Tycoon 1480982338955468057](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/1480982338955468057) (read through WebFetch; player claims, not official). The same thresholds appear in a search snippet quoting a guide. ⚠️ guide itself not opened; snippet only: [Fandom Success Guide result](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Success_Guide)
- Team size per game: Medium games take "about 4 people (counting yourself)" to make efficiently; by a large office you have 7 people on a game (6 specialists plus one spare). — [Steam thread 1480982338955468057](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/1480982338955468057) for the 4-person claim; the 7-person claim is from a search summary of other Steam threads. ⚠️ the 7-person thread was seen only as a search snippet ([search results page, query on Steam guides](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/35221584434769579))
- Capacity by size: "There is a maximum amount of features you can apply to each game size", and Medium games have less feature capacity than Large or MMO (AAA MMO is the biggest). — [Steam thread 864977564304081035](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/864977564304081035) (read through WebFetch; the poster relays the wiki secondhand)
- Default run length is 35 years of in-game time (the poster asks whether 42 helps). A player says their company must stay afloat by "constantly releasing games"; another says a small game "sells millions of copies" late in a run. — [Steam thread 1480982338955468057](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/1480982338955468057)
- Reviews are judged against your best game: "judged by your best game ever thus far"; the original poster complains the game "compares new releases to my last best game" which lowers scores. Sequels should be tied to games that did well; one player reached 8s by putting sequels on newer systems. Avoid repeating a topic "on the same engine". — [Steam thread 1480982338955468057](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/1480982338955468057) and [Steam thread 864977564304081035](https://steamcommunity.com/app/239820/discussions/0/864977564304081035)
- Contracts (filler work): side jobs from anonymous clients that pay money and research points; unlocked by a character called Jason after your third release; each has a 24-week expiry; failing carries penalties. — ⚠️ search-result summary only; I could not open [Fandom Contracts page](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Contracts) (HTTP 402).
- Publisher deals: the publisher sets a required review score; hit it and you pay no penalty and get a share of sales; miss it and the penalty "can literally kill your studio". — ⚠️ search-result summary only (same Fandom pages, not opened).
- Sales mechanics (unverified detail): sales are estimated from market size and review score and then spread across the weeks after release; first reviews arrive about 3 ticks (weeks) after release. — ⚠️ search-result summary of the Fandom "Sales Algorithm" page; page not opened ([link](https://gamedevtycoon.fandom.com/wiki/Sales_Algorithm)). I have NO decay curve numbers for Game Dev Tycoon.
- Game Dev Tycoon Netflix Edition: an expansion that adds licensed film and TV games and "new marketing opportunities" (streamers) that Netflix describes as extending a game's life beyond launch. This is the publisher's framing, not independent evidence. — [search result summary of What's on Netflix coverage](https://www.whats-on-netflix.com/news/infernax-and-game-dev-tycoon-land-releases-on-netflix-games/) ⚠️ article not opened; snippet only.
- Game Dev Story (Kairosoft, the phone tycoon): contracts "usually take 6-13 weeks". A game enters the Hall of Fame at a total critic score of 32 or more. Hall of Fame games can get sequels on any platform and sequels start with extra levels in Fun, Originality, Graphics and Sound. A sequel that misses the Hall of Fame ends the franchise ("no more sequels can be made"). Game of the Year needs a total review score of 36 or more (and even 40 does not guarantee it). Awards happen once a year in December. Re-running the same ad type stops adding fans; post-launch advertising raises sales. — [Kairosoft wiki, Game Dev Story](https://kairosoft.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Dev_Story) (read through WebFetch; the tool reported the page does not give sales per period, total run length or console lifespan)
- Game Dev Story sales window: one source says a released game is on the market for 3 months with sales set by quality, reviews, console market share and magazine coverage. — ⚠️ single search-result summary of Giant Bomb's description; page not opened ([Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/Game_Dev_Story)).
- Real-games baseline (not a tycoon): one analyst source says the majority of video games earn nearly 85 percent of lifetime sales in their first year. — ⚠️ search snippet only; article not opened ([Game Developer, SIG analysts](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/game-platforms/sig-analysts-examine-the-deteriorating-video-game-life-cycle)).

### Inferences
- The game's own release cadence is driven by money and fan thresholds, not by a fixed calendar: contracts fill the gaps, publisher deals unlock larger sizes early, and the best-so-far review baseline makes every new release a risk to your "streak". A "best model so far" comparison would give the player a reason to ship small updates without fear (a small release cannot lower the bar) while big releases are judged against it.
- Game Dev Story's rule "sequel only if the original made the Hall of Fame, and sequel starts with stat bonuses, and a failed sequel closes the line" is a clean template for "v2 of a model line only if v1 was a hit; v2 inherits part of v1's quality".
- Contract work at 6 to 13 weeks (Game Dev Story) and 24-week expiry (Game Dev Tycoon, unverified detail) is short relative to a full game, which is the mechanical pattern for "small releases between big ones".

### Gaps
- Dev time per game size (small/medium/large/AAA) in Game Dev Tycoon: not found. All Fandom wiki pages returned HTTP 402 and web.archive.org is blocked from this tool. No Greenheart Games developer blog post on this was found.
- Game Dev Tycoon sales decay numbers (weekly percentages, number of weeks on sale), number of products on sale at once: not found.
- Number of games a typical Game Dev Tycoon run releases: no reliable figure found (only anecdotes such as "constantly releasing").

## 2. Mad Games Tycoon 2: parallel games, add-ons, re-releases, game lifetime, sequel spacing

### Takeaway
Mad Games Tycoon 2 is the best-documented model of "many products live at once": player threads describe an unstoppable per-game sales decay (non-MMO games die between 40 and 100 weeks after launch, about 40 to 45 weeks when self-published at maximum price), add-ons that give a small extra boost if released in the first month, a hard spacing rule for sequels (12 months) and spin-offs (6 months), and an explicit same-IP cannibalisation effect when releases are too close. The developer himself confirms add-ons can be worth very large sums late in the game.

### Cited Findings
- Decay and lifetime: "a natural (and unstoppable) sales decay curve" shaped by initial quality, trends and awards; "a non-MMO game will die out between 40 and 100 weeks from launch, regardless of its size"; self-publishing at maximum price makes most games "die out" at 40 to 45 weeks (trading shelf life for profit per week); bigger games do not live longer and fan count does not extend lifetime. A Game of the Year award gives "the biggest boost, but it's still a small boost." — Kyouko Tsukino, [Steam thread 4632611455209870802](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/4632611455209870802/?ctp=2) ⚠️ only comments 16 to 30 of 32 were shown; comments 1 to 15 and 31 to 32 not read. Player claims, not official.
- Same thread: "Adding content will (slightly) increase sales, but not lifetime"; adding items to normal or Deluxe editions lowers Collector's Edition sales; the poster does not port or remaster; arcade games can be ported to PC "and still survive"; newer game sales "more than make up for the loss of sales in older games." — same ⚠️ partial thread.
- Cadence of a veteran player: "one game every one or two months" with "a dozen games out" at once. — same ⚠️ partial thread.
- Sequel and spin-off spacing: IP growth is debuffed if a sequel comes "less than twelve months after its prequel" or a spin-off "less than six months before the last spin-off"; once the IP reaches 5 stars this stops mattering, reachable "by 1982". — same ⚠️ partial thread; corroborated by [Steam thread 591760218823618057, "Legendary Game Sales"](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/591760218823618057) (all 6 of 6 comments read): "The threshold for sequels is one year", spin-off threshold "six months".
- Same-IP cannibalisation: "games of the same IP and same type being in the market at the same time do affect each other's sales negatively"; same-IP releases "lower each other's sales if done too close, and they'll gain IP slower"; one suggestion is one game, one budget re-release and one spin-off per IP at a time. A sequel about a year after its prequel means the prequel is "in its death throes" and barely affects sequel sales. — [Steam thread 591760218823618057](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/591760218823618057)
- Add-ons: "Add-ons do add sales, but their effect is best if done ASAP" (claim: four add-ons with all choices selected the month after release maximise it). "MMOs have a better formula, where they can be kept around for a much longer time through add-ons", but even MMOs eventually are not worth keeping. "Games have a very obvious sales decay mechanic that cannot be stopped, only delayed." — [Steam thread 591760218823618057](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/591760218823618057)
- Add-on value, including a developer statement: one player calls DLC the least worthwhile feature apart from late-game content ("slightly increase the longevity of a game that's already making you money"). The developer (account "Panda") says owned subsidiaries pay $20 a copy, "which makes add-ons a nice bonus", and that late-game add-ons can be "worth 100s of millions of dollars". — [Steam thread 3819669605967686679](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/3819669605967686679) ⚠️ page showed only 15 of 32 comments; 17 not read.
- Pricing: a $39 to $49 price gives the most total profit per game; $59 to $79 gives more per week but less total (a lifetime vs per-week trade-off). — same ⚠️ partial thread; a second player tested and found $39 best.
- Cadence of IP building: "eleven spin-offs (or five when I'm taking it slow and only making one game every two months)" before a sequel; "one game per month at 90%+ score"; new games "raise your IP a lot quicker" than add-ons. — same ⚠️ partial thread. Another player carries 12 IPs with 4 to 5 subsidiaries; the original poster cycles 3 to 4 IPs. — [Steam thread 591760218823618057](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/591760218823618057)
- Scale: 20k copies a week is "all but impossible" in 1976 but "easy after the 2000s"; 50M units takes four platforms with "80m+ active users" in 2033. — [Steam thread 591760218823618057](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/591760218823618057)
- Staffing: "50+ employees by 1980 makes the game oh so much easier"; a graphics and sound studio is worth unlocking before the 1990s. — [Steam thread 3819669605967686679](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/3819669605967686679) ⚠️ partial thread.
- Sales volume of the game itself (context only): third-party estimates disagree: about 306.56k units and $5.11m (SteamData.AI) vs about 168.5k copies and $2.5m (Raijin, dated July 24, 2026, "medium confidence"); full release May 31, 2023 from early access. — ⚠️ search-result snippets only ([SteamData.AI](https://steamdata.ai/en-US/game/1342330/mad-games-tycoon-2), [Raijin](https://raijin.gg/app/1342330/Mad_Games_Tycoon_2), [4Gamer](https://www.4gamer.net/games/711/G071184/20230601044/)); not opened.

### Inferences
- A decay clock of roughly 40 to 100 in-game weeks, with the player able to withdraw or extend a game, maps onto a model's "relevant window". The numbers that matter for design are the ratios: new releases outperform extending old ones, add-ons help most if shipped right after launch, and releases from the same line step on each other.
- MGT2's two spacing thresholds (12 months for a sequel, 6 for a spin-off) are an existing, player-accepted way to make "spam small releases" self-limiting without a hard cap: too-close releases still work but lower each other's sales and grow the IP slower. A one-line analogue is "a point release inside the cooldown of the previous one is worth less".
- The developer's own comment that add-ons are worth little early and "100s of millions" late suggests a value that scales with the audience you already own, which is a natural fit for point releases that gain value as your user base grows.

### Gaps
- No official MGT2 documentation of the sales formula, add-on revenue percentages, or how the parallel dev rooms/teams work was found; the one search for "parallel studios" returned nothing relevant. The wiki was not opened (not found in search results).
- Re-releases: only one player's statement (budget re-releases and ports exist; bundles require budget re-release first). No numbers.
- Threads are partial reads (see ⚠️); comments I did not see may contradict the quoted figures.

## 3. Other tycoon and management games (Software Inc, Startup Company, Big Pharma, Production Line, Motorsport Manager, Two Point)

### Takeaway
I found almost no usable primary or measured material for these games. The only concrete item is a single Software Inc player report where an older product out-sold a newer, better one 10 to 1, which hints that products in that game keep their own installed base independently of newer releases. For the rest, my searches returned nothing on product portfolios, patches or end-of-life.

### Cited Findings
- Software Inc: a player on Impossible difficulty reported a new, higher-scoring product (score 10 during iteration, prominent marketing, regular bug fixes and updates) selling about 200 units per month and falling, while an older second product still sold about 2,000 per month; the player suspected a feature gap against a competitor that launched six months earlier. — ⚠️ search-result summary only; the Steam thread was not opened ([Steam thread 362620/592882992704132423](https://steamcommunity.com/app/362620/discussions/0/592882992704132423)). No developer response seen.
- Startup Company (Hovgaard Games): released on Steam April 2020; the player is CEO of a startup that builds a website; one outlet noted the developer seemed unable to keep it updated. No mechanics on versions or lifecycle found. — ⚠️ search snippets only ([GamingOnLinux](https://gamingonlinux.com/2020/04/business-simulation-sandbox-startup-company-has-left-early-access)).
- Big Pharma (Twice Circled / Positech): factory-building game; an expansion added executives who market drugs and can corner the market. No evidence found on whether it models patent expiry. The real-world pharma pattern that inspired such games: after patent expiry generics arrive and sales "typically plunge 80 percent" in the following year, and firms answer with new formulations, new indications and combinations (lifecycle management). — ⚠️ search snippets only ([Big Pharma on Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Pharma_(video_game)); [pharma patent cliff article](https://www.biopharmadive.com/news/patent-cliff-pharmaceuticals-top-drugs-monopoly-expiration/715062/)); neither opened.

### Inferences
- If a newer product of equal or better quality does not automatically replace an older one's sales (Software Inc anecdote), the player-facing rule can be "each product keeps its own audience"; cannibalisation then has to be an explicit tuned effect, as it is in MGT2 (section 2).
- The pharma "new formulation / new indication extends the life of an old product" pattern is a real-world template for a "point release" that revives a decaying model line.

### Gaps
- Nothing found for Production Line, Motorsport Manager (car upgrade packages and season cadence), Two Point Hospital/Campus, or phone tycoons beyond Game Dev Story. No patch/version mechanics, portfolio screens, or live-ops data were retrieved. Software Inc, Startup Company and Big Pharma wikis or patch notes were not reached.

## 4. Small releases versus one big release: how games make them feel different (reveals, batches, lineup screens)

### Takeaway
I found no sourced material on reveal formats (short versus full reveal, batching, lineup screens). The only design-relevant facts retrieved are indirect: Game Dev Story hands out reviews as development events, MGT2 exposes add-ons as a tick-box choice at release time, and Game Dev Tycoon reviews are scored against your best game so far. The rest of this section is inference.

### Cited Findings
- In Game Dev Story, magazine reviews arrive as development events and the wiki gives no score-to-sales relationship; the yearly December awards show is a separate, larger ceremony that a company enters only after a Hall of Fame game. — [Kairosoft wiki, Game Dev Story](https://kairosoft.wiki.gg/wiki/Game_Dev_Story)
- In MGT2 the same game can carry several add-ons chosen at release (a player claims four add-ons with all choices selected in the month after release is optimal), so a "big" product can be followed by small timed follow-ups. — [Steam thread 591760218823618057](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1342330/discussions/0/591760218823618057)
- Game Dev Tycoon shows first reviews roughly 3 ticks after release, i.e. a delay rather than an instant verdict. — ⚠️ search-result summary only (Fandom pages not opened).

### Inferences
- Tycoon games already separate the frequent small beat (review event, add-on, contract complete) from the rare big beat (awards show). The game can copy that split: a one-line toast or a 5 to 10 second "patch note card" for point releases, a full 85 s reveal only for a flagship, and a batched "lineup" reveal that shows several releases at once.
- A "lineup screen" naturally supports several live models (section 5 shows labs run 12 to 15 models live at once), and an auto-upgrade mechanic (Google routing an older Flash model to the newer one, section 5) can be shown as one line on that screen rather than a new reveal.

### Gaps
- No design postmortems, GDC talks or developer interviews on reveal length, batching or lineup UIs were found. This is the weakest section of the notes.

## 5. Real AI labs, 2023 to 2026: release counts, tiers, time live, shrinking gaps

### Takeaway
All three labs moved from roughly one flagship per year to a new model every 1 to 3 months by 2026, run tiered lineups (Anthropic Haiku/Sonnet/Opus plus Fable/Mythos; OpenAI sol/terra/luna; Google Flash-Lite/Flash/Pro), keep many models live at once, and retire each after a guaranteed minimum (Anthropic: at least 12 months from launch, 60 days of notice). The fastest line in the data is Google's Flash tier: 3.5, 3.6, 3.7 and 3.8 Flash shipped 63, 23 and 20 days apart, with the older version auto-routed to the new one.

### Inferences
- Gap shrinkage: Anthropic's Opus line went from 249 days (Sonnet 3.5 to 3.7) and about 87 to 130 days in 2025 to 42 to 73 days in 2026; OpenAI's GPT-5.x went from 98 days to 28 days to a 7-day gap between 6-sol and 6.1-sol; Google's Flash 3.5 to 3.8 gaps were 63, 23 and 20 days. Release counts per lab per year rose from about 4 to 5 (2024) to about 6 to 14 (2025) to 8 to 12 launch days in under ten months of 2026 (all counts are mine and depend on what I treated as a "model").
- A repeating pattern across all three labs: a staggered tier rollout (big model first, mid and small tiers weeks to months later, e.g. Anthropic Sonnet 5 on Jun 30, Opus 5 on Jul 24, Haiku 5.5 on Oct 7), a half-step number (4.6, 4.7, 4.8; 5.1, 5.2, 5.4, 5.5; 3.5, 3.6, 3.7, 3.8) as the unit of "point release", and a clear deprecation announcement attached to each successor.
- Time live is about 12 months for Anthropic (a guaranteed minimum), 8 to 22 months for OpenAI models, and 3 to 6 months for Google preview snapshots versus 12 to 16 months for GA. For a 6-minute era game, the useful part is the ratio: a flagship stays on the lineup for several successors, while preview-style snapshots disappear after one or two.
- Caution about "point release" meaning: labs also ship specialised variants (codex, cyber, pro, chat-latest) that are not new capability levels; if the game wants "meaningful" releases, these correspond to a specialised branch rather than a version bump.
