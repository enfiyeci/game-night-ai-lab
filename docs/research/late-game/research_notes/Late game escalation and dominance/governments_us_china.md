## 1. Nationalisation and state control of AI labs (Manhattan Project proposals, DPA, stakes, clearances, classified compute, merged projects)

### Takeaway
The only formal "Manhattan Project for AGI" proposal is the USCC's November 2024 recommendation to Congress; it has not been enacted. The most concrete 2026 event is not nationalisation but the Pentagon's February 2026 attempt to coerce one lab (Anthropic) with a Defense Production Act threat and a "supply chain risk" designation: the DPA was threatened and not invoked, the designation was applied, and courts have split. Expert commentary treats DPA compulsion of an AI lab as legally novel and contestable, and treats an outright takeover as a different, much bigger step for which I found no 2026 evidence.

### Cited Findings
- HAPPENED (proposal, not law): the USCC 2024 Annual Report, Chapter 3, recommends that "Congress establish and fund a Manhattan Project-like program dedicated to racing to and acquiring an Artificial General Intelligence (AGI) capability", with two specific actions: (a) "broad multiyear contracting authority to the executive branch and associated funding for leading artificial intelligence, cloud, and data center companies and others", and (b) directing the Secretary of Defense to give a Defense Priorities and Allocations System "DX Rating" to items in the AI ecosystem "to ensure this project receives national priority." The report defines AGI as systems "as good as or better than human capabilities across all cognitive domains." It lists this first among the chapter's recommendations. — [USCC 2024 Annual Report, Ch. 3 PDF](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/Chapter_3--U.S.-China_Competition_in_Emerging_Technologies.pdf) ⚠️ I read the chapter's abstract, key findings and the recommendation text in full from a local text extraction, and searched the rest by keyword; I did not read the whole ~6,000-line chapter.
- The report was released 19 November 2024 with 32 recommendations to Congress; Reuters noted it gave no specific investment strategy. — [Search snippets: Computerworld / MeriTalk / Reuters syndication](https://www.computerworld.com/article/3609516/us-commission-proposes-manhattan-project-like-initiative-for-ai.html) ⚠️ search-snippet only; pages not opened.
- The USCC chapter also says the US leads in total compute but China is "making rapid advancements" in AI, and describes Huawei's Ascend 910B/910C ambitions and a CUDA-alternative software stack (CANN/MindSpore) — [USCC Ch. 3 PDF](https://www.uscc.gov/sites/default/files/2024-11/Chapter_3--U.S.-China_Competition_in_Emerging_Technologies.pdf) ⚠️ read only the keyword-matched passages (lines around Huawei/compute), not the full section.
- PROPOSED critique of the Manhattan model: Hendrycks, Schmidt and Wang argue a US "Manhattan Project" would be "easily observed by satellite and vulnerable to preemptive attack" and "assumes that rivals will acquiesce to an enduring imbalance or omnicide," so it would more likely provoke escalation than secure stability. They instead propose deterrence plus nonproliferation plus competitiveness. — [Superintelligence Strategy, expert version, arXiv HTML v2, §3.4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05628v2)
- Same paper, §5.2: model weights and research ideas should be protected like WMD design details; recommends a government role in "threat intelligence", revising background-check rules "so that firms can evaluate security risks before granting clearance", and an AI-sector information-sharing programme modelled on CRISP; warns that removing foreign-national researchers would push talent abroad. — [arXiv HTML v2, §5.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05628v2)
- RAND "Securing AI Model Weights" (Nevo et al., 2024) defines five security levels SL1-SL5 matched to attacker classes OC1-OC5. As reproduced on the AI 2027 site: SL1/OC1 amateurs, about $1,000, days; SL2/OC2 professional opportunists, up to $10,000, weeks; SL3/OC3 cybercrime syndicates and insiders, up to $1M, months, team of about 10; SL4/OC4 standard operations by leading cyber-capable institutions (state-sponsored groups), up to $10M, a year, about 100 people; SL5/OC5 top-priority operations by the most capable nation-states, up to $1B, multi-year, decades-built infrastructure, about 1,000 people. — [AI 2027 Security Forecast (secondary reproduction of RAND's table)](https://ai-2027.com/research/security-forecast) ⚠️ I could not open the RAND report itself (rand.org returned 403); the table is a secondary reproduction, and the AI 2027 page adds its own scenario levels that are not RAND's.
- On the government role at SL5, the same page says private companies "are likely to need significant government support and resources"; a workshop poll found 63% agreed no AI company will reach SL5 voluntarily unless law or strong incentives require it and the US government helps; 78% agreed a state actor will likely steal frontier US model weights before 2030; 56% disagreed that SL5 could be done in under 6 months even with a $100B+ government effort. These are opinions of workshop participants in a scenario project, not RAND findings. — [AI 2027 Security Forecast](https://ai-2027.com/research/security-forecast)
- RAND's "Artificial General Intelligence's Five Hard National Security Problems" (Mitre and Predd; Senate Armed Services testimony, March 2025): wonder weapons, systemic shifts in power, non-experts with WMD capability, autonomous agents acting as global actors, instability; the authors say progress on one can undermine another. — [RAND testimony CTA3914-1](https://www.rand.org/pubs/testimonies/CTA3914-1.html) ⚠️ page returned 403; description comes from search snippets of third-party event pages only. I found nothing from RAND on how it treats the Manhattan Project analogy.
- HAPPENED (coercion, not takeover): on 24 February 2026 Defense Secretary Hegseth met Anthropic's CEO and threatened, per Lawfare's Alan Rozenshtein as excerpted by Volokh, to invoke the Defense Production Act unless Anthropic accepted Pentagon terms by that Friday. Anthropic refused to allow use for autonomous weapons or mass surveillance. A January 2026 Pentagon AI strategy memo had directed DoD AI contracts to adopt "any lawful use" language within 180 days. — [Volokh Conspiracy, 25 Feb 2026](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/02/25/anthropic-the-pentagon-and-the-defense-production-act/) ⚠️ this is Volokh's excerpt of the Lawfare piece, not the Lawfare article itself.
- Legal reading: the threat likely invoked DPA Title I (compulsion), "an enormous escalation"; the earlier Biden AI executive order used Title VII (information gathering) to require reporting on training, red-teaming and weights, which makes it harder to argue the DPA cannot reach AI but does not make every demand lawful; retraining a model to remove safety features is treated as more extreme than changing access terms. The DPA was most recently extended through September 2026. — [Volokh Conspiracy](https://reason.com/volokh/2026/02/25/anthropic-the-pentagon-and-the-defense-production-act/)
- Expert plausibility: Joel Dodge (Vanderbilt Policy Accelerator) says using the DPA to compel an unsafe product or dictate terms of service would be without precedent and he does not expect the government to prevail in court; Charlie Bullock (Institute for Law & AI) says litigation looks realistic if neither side backs down. Observers noted the tension between calling a vendor a supply chain risk and calling it essential enough for the DPA. — [Air Force Times / Federal News Network, 26 Feb 2026](https://www.airforcetimes.com/news/pentagon-congress/2026/02/26/what-to-know-about-defense-protection-act-and-the-pentagons-anthropic-ultimatum/)
- HAPPENED: the "supply chain risk" designation was applied (early March 2026); the administration ordered federal agencies to stop working with Anthropic; Anthropic sued in March (one suit in N.D. California, one in the D.C. Circuit). On 27 August 2026 Judge Rita Lin held the designation was "unlawful retaliation" (First Amendment), arbitrary and capricious, and a Fifth Amendment due-process violation, noting the government had floated the DPA (treating the company as essential) while also pursuing the designation. — [TechCrunch, 28 Aug 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/08/28/anthropic-gets-its-first-court-win-over-the-pentagons-supply-chain-risk-label/)
- HAPPENED: on 25 September 2026 a divided D.C. Circuit panel (2-1, Katsas writing, Henderson dissenting) upheld the Pentagon's designation, which bars the military and defense contractors from using Anthropic's models; Anthropic said it is considering further review. — [CNBC, 25 Sep 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/25/pentagon-anthropic-ai-risk-appeals-court.html) ⚠️ search-snippet only (CNBC page not opened); corroborated by [Washington Post and ABC headlines in the same search](https://abcnews.com/Business/anthropic-appeals-court-declines-block-pentagon-blacklisting/story?id=136755690), also not opened. Full-text status after late September unknown.
- HAPPENED: OpenAI announced a Pentagon deal in the same week as the ban. — [NPR, 27 Feb 2026](https://www.npr.org/2026/02/27/nx-s1-5729118/trump-anthropic-pentagon-openai-ai-weapons-ban) ⚠️ headline/snippet only; page timed out. The TechCrunch article also says the government used Anthropic's "Mythos" model for cybersecurity during the dispute; I could not corroborate Mythos elsewhere (UNVERIFIED beyond that one article).
- HAPPENED (government-lab partnership, not control): the "Genesis Mission" executive order of 24 November 2025 has the Department of Energy mobilise its 17 national laboratories, industry and academia into an integrated AI discovery platform, led by Under Secretary Darío Gil, with national-security AI as one of three priorities. — [PPPL announcement](https://www.pppl.gov/news/2025/energy-department-launches-%E2%80%98genesis-mission%E2%80%99-transform-american-science-and-innovation) ⚠️ search-snippet only; I found nothing on frontier-lab partnerships or classified compute under it.
- Political signal: Semafor (18 Oct 2026) reports a conservative group, Humans First, is pushing an "America First AI Policy" platform including export controls on US technology, and that such proposals "could have more sway" if Republicans do badly in the midterms with AI anxiety a factor. — [Semafor, 18 Oct 2026](https://www.semafor.com/article/10/18/2026/china-mulls-ai-export-rules)

### Inferences
- The realistic ladder of state control, from my reading of the above, is: voluntary partnership (Genesis-style) -> contract terms ("any lawful use") -> priority ratings/DPA Title I orders -> supply-chain exclusion -> forced access to weights or retraining -> outright public ownership. Steps 1-4 have real 2025-2026 precedent; steps 5-6 are threatened or hypothetical. Experts quoted treat step 5 as legally weak today (inference from Dodge and Rozenshtein).
- The 2026 court split means a game state could plausibly sit in "legal limbo": a lab can win at trial level and lose on appeal, so government pressure keeps working even when contested.
- RAND's SL5 logic implies the strongest practical reason for a government to take a seat in a lab is security: only the state can supply SL4-SL5-grade defence against OC5 attackers, so "classified compute / cleared staff / state-run datacenter" is the soft path to control before any takeover.
- The USCC proposal and the Superintelligence Strategy authors disagree about whether a centralised project is wise; both are PROPOSED, neither enacted.

### Gaps
- No primary RAND text on nationalisation, merged national projects, or golden shares was reachable; I did not find a RAND or CSET analysis of a government equity stake or golden share in a frontier lab.
- No 2026 evidence found of any actual US plan to take equity in or nationalise a frontier lab. (The 2025 Intel equity stake and similar deals were not researched here.)
- No information found on classified-compute arrangements for frontier labs beyond the Pentagon dispute.
- Status of the Anthropic litigation after 25 September 2026 (rehearing, certiorari) not checked.

## 2. US-China competition: export controls, chips, smuggling, Taiwan, race versus deal

### Takeaway
The Biden AI Diffusion Rule was rescinded in May 2025 and not replaced by a comprehensive rule; the Trump administration has instead moved to case-by-case licensing and a fee-based opening for H200 sales to China (January 2026), while Beijing slow-walks purchases to favour Huawei. At the leader level, the May 2026 Beijing summit produced no AI agreement, and the late-September 2026 Washington summit produced a "Super Intelligence Dialogue" and an incident channel, but no governance framework. China itself drafted export controls on its own AI models and chip designs on 16 October 2026.

### Cited Findings
- HAPPENED: on 13 May 2025 BIS rescinded the AI Diffusion Rule (issued 15 January 2025, due to take effect 15 May 2025), told enforcement staff not to enforce it, and said it will publish a formal rescission and a future replacement rule with no timeline. It issued guidance on Huawei Ascend chips, on diversion, and on the consequences of using US chips to train or run Chinese models, and added licence requirements for AI chips to Malaysia and Thailand because of known diversion. — [BIS press release, 13 May 2025](https://www.bis.gov/press-release/department-commerce-rescinds-biden-era-artificial-intelligence-diffusion-rule-strengthens-chip-related)
- Law-firm summaries read the Huawei guidance (Ascend 910B/910C/910D, based on General Prohibition 10) as saying use anywhere may violate US export controls; they differ on how strongly. — [Akin Gump](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/alerts/bis-rescinds-its-ai-diffusion-rule-and-issues-compliance-guidance-regarding-advanced-computing-items) and [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/us-department-of-commerce-rescinds-biden-administrations-ai-diffusion-export-control-rule-and-issues-new-guidance-on-huawei-chips-for-ai-purposes-and-diligence-expectations) ⚠️ search-snippet only. One search result's timeline mis-dates the rescission to early 2026; the BIS release says May 2025.
- HAPPENED: 13 January 2026 BIS rule moved certain advanced chip exports to China/Hong Kong/Macau to case-by-case review (buyer screening, certification, US third-party testing); 14 January 2026 proclamation set a 25% import tariff on the Nvidia H200 and AMD MI325X, which together act as a 25% export fee; exports to China capped at 50% of each chip's US sales (CNAS estimates about 900,000 H200-equivalents, up to 2.3 million). — [CNAS Insights, "Unpacking the H200 Export Policy", 16 Jan 2026](https://www.cnas.org/publications/cnas-insights/cnas-insights-unpacking-the-h200-export-policy)

CNAS estimates about 390,000 H200-equivalents of Chinese domestic compute in 2026 (Semianalysis: about 300,000 Huawei 910C; Bloomberg: 600,000 910C plus 100,000 910D), with large uncertainty. — [CNAS](https://www.cnas.org/publications/cnas-insights/cnas-insights-unpacking-the-h200-export-policy)

Current (October) delivery status UNVERIFIED.

### Inferences
- US policy under the current administration is transactional: controls are used as a bargaining and revenue instrument (fee, caps, case-by-case), while rhetoric rejects "global governance". A game "President" can credibly both loosen chip sales for a payment and demand loyalty from labs.
- China is choosing self-reliance over buying H200s, and now mirrors the US by drafting its own export controls on models and chip designs. A game "East" faction can therefore (a) refuse to buy, (b) restrict its own lab's weights from export, (c) treat its lab (Qilin) as a state asset.
- The incident channel plus "SI Dialogue" is a low-cost confidence-building step with no verification; the structure fits a game "summit" mechanic where each side signs process commitments but not capability limits.
- Taiwan/TSMC is both the main chokepoint and the main restraint on conflict (Time expert; Superintelligence Strategy), so a Taiwan crisis should be modelled as a very high-impact, low-probability event that hits everyone's compute.

## 3. International governance: summits, IAEA-for-AI ideas, compute caps, verification, and what would make a deal binding

### Takeaway
Summit diplomacy has moved from safety (Bletchley 2023, Seoul 2024) to action/impact framing (Paris 2025, New Delhi February 2026) and the UN now has a permanent scientific panel and dialogue (2026), but nothing binding on frontier training exists. Technical papers say some verification (in-person chip counting and datacenter inspection) could be done almost immediately with current technology, but only inside a prior political agreement to limit AI development; the most useful hardware mechanisms (compute metering, proof-of-training) are the least mature.

### Cited Findings
- HAPPENED: Paris AI Action Summit, February 2025: about 60-61 countries including China, France, India, Japan, Australia and Canada signed the "Statement on Inclusive and Sustainable Artificial Intelligence"; the US and UK did not sign; Vice President Vance argued against excessive regulation and against international cooperation with China. — [Al Jazeera, 12 Feb 2025](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2025/2/12/paris-ai-summit-why-wont-us-uk-sign-global-artificial-intelligence-pact) ⚠️ search-snippet only; counts differ by source.
- HAPPENED: Bletchley (Nov 2023) produced the Bletchley Declaration and a commitment to let AI safety institutes test new models; Seoul (May 2024) produced "Frontier AI Safety Commitments" from 16 companies (Amazon, Meta, Microsoft among them) and a separate Seoul AI Business Pledge from 14 companies. — [Akin Gump summary](https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/UK-Co-Hosts-AI-Seoul-Summit-with-the-Republic-of-Korea) and [Euronews](https://www.euronews.com/2024/05/21/ai-seoul-summit-big-tech-companies-make-commitment-to-ai-safety-as-second-world-summit-beg) ⚠️ snippets only; I found nothing on whether the commitments were kept.
- HAPPENED: India AI Impact Summit (February 2026) produced a non-binding "New Delhi Declaration on AI Impact"; the minister announced 86 countries and two international organisations on 21 February, with US, UK, Canada, China, Denmark and Germany among signatories (other outlets say 88 or 89); about $250bn in investment pledges; India joined the US-led Pax Silica initiative. — [Swarajya](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/news-brief/86-nations-and-two-international-organisations-back-indias-ai-vision-at-impact-summit-pledges-cross-250-billion-vaishnaw) and [Odisha Bytes](https://odishabytes.com/india-calls-for-ai-for-all-us-russia-china-france-among-88-nations-organisations-to-sign-new-delhi-ai-declaration/) ⚠️ snippets only; signatory count inconsistent; the declaration text was not read.
- HAPPENED: UN General Assembly Resolution A/RES/79/325 (26 August 2025) created the 40-member Independent International Scientific Panel on AI (term February 2026 to February 2029; co-chairs Yoshua Bengio and Maria Ressa) and the Global Dialogue on AI Governance. The first Dialogue met in Geneva 6-7 July 2026 with a preliminary Panel report the day before; Bengio told governments "science currently cannot guarantee that as capabilities continue to increase, AI will not cause catastrophic harm." ### Inferences
- A binding deal requires, in order: a shared threat perception (the Panel's catastrophic-risk statement helps), a negotiated threshold (compute or capability), a verification route that does not reveal weights or military uses, and an enforcement trigger. The Superintelligence Strategy's own scepticism about voluntary moratoria and the US "no global governance" line both make step one the binding constraint today.
- Verification technology lags politics in the other direction too: even if politics allowed a deal, only inspection-style verification is "ready now"; that favours a game mechanic where inspections are possible but intrusive and politically costly for labs.
- A game "summit" can credibly use three tiers: nonbinding declarations (Paris/Delhi pattern, cheap), process channels (incident hotline, dialogue), and verified limits (requires inspectors, chip-tracking, and the player's consent to host them).

## 4. Escalation ladders: steps between competition and conflict, and signals each side watches

### Takeaway
The main published ladder is the Superintelligence Strategy's five rungs (espionage, covert sabotage, overt cyberattack, kinetic strike, broader hostilities) under MAIM. It is the authors' framing, not a government doctrine. A MIRI critique finds the deterrence regime "unlikely to provide an effective or stable" outcome because red lines are hard to monitor and sabotage mostly delays rather than denies. RAND's SL1-SL5 framework supplies the cyber-theft rung's attacker model.

### Cited Findings
- Figure 4.2 of the Superintelligence Strategy (as summarised): (1) espionage of developer workspaces, devices and facilities; (2) covert sabotage threshold - model-weight theft, covert training degradation, GPU failure attacks; (3) overt sabotage threshold - cyberattacks on datacenters or power, code deletion; (4) kinetic threshold - strikes on datacenters or power plants; (5) broader hostilities - large-scale attacks and threats to non-AI assets. The authors say kinetic action is "likely unnecessary" because cyber options exist, compare covert sabotage to Stuxnet, and note non-deterministic training runs give cover for sabotage. — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.1-4.2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05628v2)
- Trigger in the paper: a "destabilizing AI project", defined by a state racing "toward a strategic monopoly" or a project any rival sees as an "unacceptable risk"; the sharpest trigger is a fully autonomous AI R&D loop (intelligence recursion, §3.3.3), which the authors say would make loss of control "highly likely and the default". — [arXiv HTML v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05628v2)
- Signals and legibility in the paper: clear escalation ladders so each side's willingness to "maim" is common knowledge; datacenter transparency; tamper-evident camera feeds and satellite imagery for undeclared facilities; compute as "the most robust tracker of AI capabilities"; siting large datacenters away from cities; rumours of underground hardening "alone can spark fears"; burying datacenters would take years longer and cost "several hundred billion dollars". — [arXiv HTML v2, §4.1-4.2, §5.1.1](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05628v2)
- Plausibility from the authors: MAIM "already describes the strategic picture"; "a potentially stable deterrence regime, but maintaining it could require care". — [arXiv HTML v2, §4](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.05628v2)
- Critique (MIRI, April 2025): seven conditions for deterrence; red lines (automated AI research) are poorly defined and recursion could leave a very short "breakout" window; monitoring is circular (threats are meant to force transparency but need monitoring to justify them); sabotage likely delays rather than denies (developers can detect and recover; Stuxnet reached only a fraction of its targets; tabletop exercises showed mutual sabotage slowing but not halting progress); a denial-only threat lacks potency when stakes are existential, so deterrence would drift to punishment-based and more escalatory; proposes earlier, more monitorable red lines (advanced fabrication, compute concentration) and inducements such as benefit-sharing; verdict "unlikely to provide an effective or stable deterrence regime." — [MIRI TGT, "Refining MAIM"](https://techgov.intelligence.org/blog/refining-maim-identifying-changes-required-to-meet-conditions-for-deterrence)
- Other critiques exist from the AI-safety community: that multipolar MAIM raises misinterpretation risk, and the authors' reply in AI Frontiers. — [AI Frontiers](https://newsletter.ai-frontiers.org/p/ai-deterrence-is-our-best-option); [LessWrong](https://www.lesswrong.com/posts/3Brp3F8uC5A9sRwx9/the-dangerous-illusion-of-ai-deterrence-why-maim-isn-t) ⚠️ search snippets only; pages not opened. I found no RAND, CSET or CNAS critique of MAIM.
- Cyber-theft rung evidence: the RAND SL framework puts nation-state OC5 operations at up to $1B and about 1,000 people; the AI 2027 workshop gave 78% agreement that a state actor will likely steal frontier US weights before 2030 (opinion, not forecast by RAND). — [AI 2027 Security Forecast](https://ai-2027.com/research/security-forecast)
- Real 2026 steps already on lower rungs: the White House's accusation of "distilling" of US models by China-based entities (Time, May 2026); US removal of DeepSeek from federal systems and China's draft model-export controls (Section 2); Beijing blocking an AI-talent acquisition (Manus). — [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/05/15/trump-xi-us-china-summit-ai-semiconductor-chips/); [The Next Web](https://thenextweb.com/news/china-ai-export-controls-draft)
- Taiwan risk: Superintelligence Strategy calls it a "critical chokepoint" (above); Xi warned at the May 2026 summit that overstepping on Taiwan could lead to "clashes and even conflicts" (Time). — [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/05/15/trump-xi-us-china-summit-ai-semiconductor-chips/)
- Blockade: not discussed as an AI-specific rung in any source I read. The Hormuz disruption described by Time shows energy/materials chokepoints already affecting chips, single-source. — [Time](https://time.com/article/2026/05/15/trump-xi-us-china-summit-ai-semiconductor-chips/)

### Inferences
- A usable game ladder, anchored to sources: (0) export-control tightening and talent restrictions -> (1) espionage and distillation accusations -> (2) weight theft or covert sabotage -> (3) overt cyberattack on power/cooling -> (4) strike on a datacenter or fab -> (5) blockade or general conflict. Blockade is my addition, not a sourced rung.
- Signals a faction watches (all sourced individually): chip counts and locations, new datacenter construction and rumours of hardening, evidence of an automated-R&D loop at a rival lab, exfiltration incidents, and talent flows.
- Because sabotage mostly delays (MIRI), a game that models sabotage as a time-cost to the target lab rather than a kill is better grounded than one where an attack ends a run.

### Gaps
- No source quantifies the probability of each rung; the only plausibility statements are the authors' and critics' qualitative ones.
- No government doctrine document on escalation in AI contests was found.
- No source on how China's leadership describes red lines for AI.
